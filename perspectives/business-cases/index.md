# Business cases

Three levels, one rule: a number appears only with its source, its basis (annual or one-off, upper or lower bound, estimate or fact), its geography and its verification state.

| Level | Question | Page |
|---|---|---|
| Economy (Volkswirtschaft) | What can the EU economy gain or lose? | [Macroeconomic case](macro/) |
| Domain and ecosystem | What does it change for supply chains, product passports, data spaces and reporting? | [Ecosystem case](ecosystem/) |
| Company (Betriebswirtschaft) | What does it cost, what can it save and what are the risks for one company? | [Company case](company/) |

## Source rule for business cases

Official documents (Commission impact assessment and press releases, Council, Eurostat, EIB, Europol, national authorities), standards bodies, and peer-reviewed journal papers or working papers of recognised institutions. Blogs, vendor material, consultancy marketing and trade press are not used, even where they quote the same numbers. A number that could not be traced to a primary source is listed as a finding ("not traceable"), never as a fact.

## How to read the numbers

- The headline savings are modelled upper bounds under an adoption assumption, not forecasts.
- The Commission's impact assessment states its own limits (small survey samples, averages, adoption assumptions); they are reproduced on the macroeconomic page.
- Costs, adoption and benefits are connected: a benefit figure without the adoption and cost assumption behind it is not a business case.
