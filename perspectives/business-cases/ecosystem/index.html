Ecosystem case

Summary

For ecosystems the economic argument is indirect: organisation identity and credentials are not the product, they are what makes processes in other policy areas cheaper to run. The official impact assessments quantify those processes (customs, VAT and e-invoicing, incident reporting, data contracts, product passports) but almost never the share that depends on identity. The numbers below are therefore the size of the processes in which a business wallet would play a part, not savings that the wallet alone would deliver. Evidence on costs is weakest for product passports and data-space onboarding.

Domain by domain

Product passports (ESPR). The Commission estimated the set-up of the central passport registry at about EUR 8 million one-off and at least EUR 1 million per year. For businesses the impact assessment gives a range of EUR 30 to 60 billion per year for a further 30 product-specific measures when fully incurred; this covers all sustainability requirements, not the passport alone. No official per-company passport cost was found. See EBW and DPP integration and CIRPASS-2.

Data spaces. The closest official quantification is about data contracts: the Digital Omnibus models about EUR 1 billion of savings from re-negotiating at least 100,000 contracts and cites about EUR 1 million per year of data-management agreement costs for large firms. No official cost of KYB, supplier qualification or onboarding into a data space was found. See DSP and DCP integration and the ecosystems.

Customs and trade documents. Complying with customs formalities costs economic operators about EUR 27.4 billion per year; the customs reform is modelled to save EUR 40 billion over 15 years (EUR 27 billion net). An OECD analysis associates better trade-facilitation indicators in four areas with 18% higher global exports, as an association and not a causal estimate, and stresses that process streamlining has to come first.

E-invoicing and VAT. The VAT in the Digital Age impact assessment models e-invoicing-related savings of EUR 1.9 to 14.5 billion and net benefits of EUR 138.9 billion (option 4a) to EUR 231.1 billion (option 4b) for digital reporting requirements. It cites a VAT gap of EUR 134 billion, which is the fiscal reason to identify businesses reliably.

Incident and regulatory reporting. The Digital Omnibus expects at least EUR 5 billion of administrative savings by 2029 (not a wallet figure) and models a single entry point for incident reports at about EUR 440 per incident, with savings of about EUR 41.5 million per year as a lower bound. The single entry point is described as interoperable with European Business Wallets. See B2G reporting.

How the pieces relate

The EU-wide wallet estimate (EUR 225 billion at full adoption in the proposal’s memorandum) and the sector estimates count overlapping processes: the same company onboarding, reporting or invoicing step can appear in more than one of them. They must not be added.

Evidence gaps

No official passport cost per company; no official data-space onboarding or KYB cost; no company-level fraud loss in the EU; no EU figure for the share of customs, VAT or reporting cost that depends on identity. Closing them needs primary data from participating companies, for example from the pilots (WE BUILD, CIRPASS-2) and data spaces (Catena-X, energy data-X).
