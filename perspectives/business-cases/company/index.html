Company case

Summary

For one company the case rests on two numbers that the Commission models and that no company has yet measured: what administration costs today, and what the wallet costs to adopt and run. The impact assessment gives per-entity figures for both. Annual maximum savings exceed annual costs by a wide margin for micro and small firms and by a smaller margin for large firms, but the savings are an upper bound and depend on counterparties and authorities that accept the wallet. What a company cannot yet find in official sources is the cost of onboarding a supplier or customer today, and its own fraud exposure.

Costs and savings per entity (impact assessment model)

Size One-off cost Annual cost Maximum annual benefit
Micro (under 10 staff) EUR 620 EUR 500 EUR 4,000
SME (10 to 250) EUR 2,600 EUR 5,000 EUR 42,250
Large (over 250) EUR 180,000 EUR 50,000 EUR 97,300

One-off items: training and onboarding, activation and IT implementation (ERP and CRM integration for larger firms), contracting. The model is the EU Standard Cost Model with an hourly labour cost of EUR 33.5. The corporate IT figure draws on a vendor-commissioned study, and the SME and corporate licence figures have no stated market source in the passages read; the only market evidence is a range of EUR 50 to 500 per year for basic functions. About 46% of EU enterprises used ERP software in 2025, which is why integration cost matters for roughly half of all firms.

Stand-alone services for the self-employed

For self-employed persons and sole traders who use their EUDI Wallet instead of a full Business Wallet, the impact assessment estimates annual recurring costs of EUR 45 for the new communications channel, EUR 150-200 for electronic signatures, EUR 350-400 for seals and EUR 235-285 for timestamps (about 100 items). This is not a price for companies.

Burden today

The impact assessment reports that 55% of SMEs name administrative burden as their greatest challenge (citing a trade association), quotes the EIB for compliance costs of about 1.8% of turnover for firms and 2.5% for small and mid-size companies, and that 86% of firms employ staff specifically for regulatory compliance. The Call for Evidence itself contains none of these figures, although secondary reports attribute them to it.

Risks and barriers

Costs fall disproportionately on small firms without legal, IT or compliance departments; limited traceability in the EUDI Wallet makes integration with ERP and KYC processes difficult; paper formalities and interoperability gaps remain. On fraud, the FBI reports about USD 2.77 billion in business email compromise losses in the United States in 2024; Europol names the same fraud types without euro figures. No EU company-level fraud loss figure was found.

Questions for a decision

What do you spend today on onboarding, certificates, reporting and document exchange, and how many of those counterparties would accept a wallet? Which of your systems would need integration? Which authorities you deal with are obliged to accept the wallet? The sources give the model; your own data gives the case.
