Macroeconomic case

Summary

The Commission estimates that European Business Wallets could save businesses up to EUR 150 billion per year if uptake is broad. The impact assessment behind that number is more careful than the headline: it models four adoption ranges, shows costs next to benefits, calls its maximum an upper bound and lists its own limits. Read together, the sources support a wide range with a positive net effect in most scenarios, not a single forecast. The only EU-wide quantification of indirect effects is small and correlational.

What the sources say

  1. Headline. Press release IP/25/2718 of 19 November 2025: the wallets “could unlock another EUR 150 billion in savings for businesses each year”, assuming broad uptake. The EUR 5 billion figure in the same release belongs to the Digital Omnibus, not to the wallets.
  2. Model. The impact assessment SWD(2025) 837 uses the EU Standard Cost Model: time per administrative activity and frequency from a survey, hourly labour cost from official statistics, and the number of entities (about 32.7 million economic operators, 94.2% of them microenterprises, and about 95,800 public sector bodies). It tests adoption of 10%, 33%, 50% and 75% of economic operators and treats 75% as the preferred range.
  3. Range. Direct benefits are between EUR 58.42 billion (minimum, derived from respondents’ expected cost reductions) and EUR 168.79 billion (maximum) per year; the press-release figure matches the economic-operator maximum of EUR 154.45 billion, rounded down.
  4. Costs. At 75% adoption, economic operators bear over EUR 45 billion in year 1 (about EUR 25 billion one-off, EUR 21 billion recurring) and about EUR 21 billion per year afterwards; public bodies EUR 7.33 billion in year 1.
  5. Net effect. In the preferred range the net direct benefit is between about EUR 6 billion and EUR 116 billion in year 1 and between about EUR 37 billion and EUR 147 billion in year 2. At the lowest adoption range the minimum net is slightly negative in year 1.
  6. Indirect effects. Trust in digital transactions EUR 16.66 billion (a correlational elasticity), fraud reduction EUR 4.96 billion, data quality EUR 1.35 billion, resource reallocation EUR 0.45 billion; described as minimum quantifiable, with overlaps.

Limits that the sources state themselves

Limited public cost data, no comparable benchmarks, hard-to-forecast licence fees, reliance on averages across very different firms, small survey samples (24 public bodies and 38 economic operators), and adoption that depends on factors outside the model. The impact assessment also says that the lowest adoption range is not a minimum scenario. Some totals are inconsistent inside the document and are flagged in the claim table.

Academic evidence

A study of Chinese listed firms finds that digital government raises corporate productivity by about 5% on average. It is useful for the direction of the effect and not for an EU number: listed firms only, one country. No peer-reviewed study that quantifies EU-wide savings from business wallets was found.

What this means

  • Use the range, not the headline: gross up to about EUR 150-169 billion per year at high adoption, EUR 58 billion at the minimum, net benefits positive in the preferred adoption range.
  • Adoption is the main uncertainty and a policy choice as much as an economic one: public acceptance duties and low entry costs for small firms drive it.
  • Open evidence gaps: cross-border trade effects, once-only and e-invoicing savings attributable to the wallets, and effects on GDP. No official figure was found for them.
