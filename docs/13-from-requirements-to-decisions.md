# From requirements to architecture decisions

Date: 2026-10-03. Describes how the clusters, decisions and patterns relate; the public version is on `/architecture/decisions/`.

## Chain

Source obligation -> requirement (EBW-<CAT>-<NNN>) -> cluster (K01 to K16) -> decision (DEC-nn) -> pattern (PAT-nn, planned) -> building block -> conformance check.

- **Cluster tagging** is done in `_data/review/requirement-review.yml` (generated by `scripts/merge_review.py` from reviewer passes). Clusters are defined in `_data/graph/clusters.yml`.
- **Decisions** are registered in `_data/graph/decisions.yml` and written as pages `architecture/decisions/dec-nn.md`.
- **Patterns** are not yet a data type. They will be added as `_data/graph/patterns.yml` once two decisions share an option.

## Rules

1. The cluster list is fixed unless a requirement fits no cluster; then a cluster is added and documented here (K16 became "AI-first and agent interoperability" with the category AIF).
2. A cluster becomes a decision when it has several P1 requirements and at least two options that differ on criteria that matter. Otherwise it remains a constraint set.
3. Criteria are marked *source* (stated in a law, standard or specification, with reference) or *driver* (no source states it; class A until a stakeholder confirms).
4. A decision page never ranks without saying what would change the reading.
5. Research notes behind a page are kept in `docs/research/`.

## Coupling between decisions

DEC-01 (formats and semantics), DEC-02 (employee wallet) and DEC-10 (agents, protocols, mandates) share the mandate and semantics questions. A mandate is a credential (DEC-01), it is issued and revoked by an owner (DEC-02), and an agent presents it under a protocol (DEC-10). Any change to one is checked against the others.

## Categories versus clusters

Categories (FUN, NFR, INT, TRU, CER, GOV, LEG, DAT, OPS, DOM, AIF, BIZ, CON, TRN) classify the *kind* of requirement and fix its ID. Clusters group requirements by the *architecture concern* they bear on, across categories. A requirement has one category and one to three clusters.

## Collection categories

AIF (AI-first and agent interoperability) is a collecting category. Requirements stay in it even where the same obligation is also held elsewhere (for example AI Act duties next to LEG-040 to LEG-044, or protocol-specific text next to a neutral requirement). The cross-references are in `_data/review/cross-references.yml` (relations also-covered-by, better-fit, profile-of), derived by `scripts/derive_cross_references.py` and shown on the requirement pages. The mechanism is generic: another category can be flagged `collection: true` and handled the same way.
