# 12 — Becoming the reference point for the European Business Wallet

Status: DRAFT ideas for discussion. Goal: establish authority on the EBW and be the place stakeholders go to first.

## What makes a site an authority

Accuracy that can be checked, visible independence, currency, usefulness for a decision, and being cited by others. The site already has traceability to official sources, a source rule, status banners and dated verification. The ideas below add credibility, reach and a community.

## Ideas, grouped

**Credibility**
1. **Editorial board and review rota.** Named reviewers per area (legal, standards, data spaces, DPP, AI) with short bios; a published corrections policy and a changelog (started). Reviewers sign the review gate (docs/08).
2. **Citable releases.** Tagged baselines with a DOI (Zenodo), BibTeX/CSL on every page, versioned requirement IDs that never change.
3. **Neutrality statement and funding disclosure.** The site is Spherity branded and Spherity takes part in some ecosystems; say so plainly (started on the research page) and show how requirements stay source-based. Invite other organisations as co-maintainers or reviewers (IDTA, DSSC, WE BUILD members, notaries, Bitkom, standards experts).
4. **Methodology note as a paper.** Submit the traceability method and the first results to a venue such as the Open Identity Summit; it links to the existing paper and gives academic citations.

**Currency**
5. **Legislative tracker.** One page that shows where COM(2025) 838 is (Council, Parliament, trilogue), with dated primary sources; the weekly watch feeds it.
6. **Changelog and RSS** (started), later an optional email digest.

**Usefulness**
7. **Decision pages.** "EBW in 10 minutes" for business leaders, "What the proposal requires" for policy makers, "Implementer's checklist" and "Researcher's map"; each links to concepts.
8. **Glossary and comparison tables** (EBW and EUDI Wallet, trusted list and list of trusted entities, levels of assurance across regimes).
9. **Business cases** (macro, ecosystem, company) with every number traced to a source (in progress).
10. **Traceability explorer**: the knowledge graph with filters, the coverage dashboard and a downloadable JSON-LD and CSV; a stable public dataset others can cite and reuse.
11. **Requirement catalogue and conformance profiles**, then test cases that implementers can run; links to reference implementations without endorsing products.

**Reach and community**
12. **Discussions and a consultation page**: structured ways to comment on a requirement, with a response policy; publish a response to Commission and Parliament consultations using the traceable material.
13. **Workshops and talks** with IDTA, DSSC, WE BUILD and standards bodies; publish summaries.
14. **Wikidata and neutral reference entries** for the concepts and the site; Google Scholar and ScholarlyArticle metadata for the papers and the white-paper style pages.
15. **German summaries** for DACH policy and industry audiences once the English baseline is stable (the decision so far is English only).

**Quality and trust signals**
16. Accessibility statement and checks, security.txt, a clear privacy and imprint page, a publication calendar, and visible "last verified" dates (partly in place).

## Suggested order

First: editorial board, DOI releases, legislative tracker, business cases, glossary, decision pages. Then: explorer and dataset, consultation, workshops, German summaries.
