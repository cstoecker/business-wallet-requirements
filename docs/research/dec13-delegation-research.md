# RES-dec13-delegation: how employees, machines and AI agents get authority to act for an organisation that owns a European Business Wallet (K03)

Scope: facts from primary sources only; no recommendation. Date of work: 2026-10-04. Decision question (DEC-13, planned): one integrated model for the creation, scope and limits, onward delegation, joint (four-eyes) representation, suspension versus revocation, termination (person leaves, machine decommissioned), audit and attribution, and assurance level of authority granted to three actor classes: employees (natural persons), machines (devices, plants, services, technical users, M2M) and AI agents.

## (A) FACTS FROM SOURCES

### A1 EBW proposal, Council text and Parliament draft: who may act, how authority is created

- A1.1 Purpose. The EBW enables owners "to enable the creation, management and delegation of mandates to authorised representatives" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 3(1)(c) | read. Council: "to create, manage and delegate authorisations to European Business Wallet users" | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Art 3(1)(c) | read. EP draft: "delegation of mandates and roles to authorised representatives and users" | SRC-EP-ITRE-DRAFT-REPORT Amendment 50 | read (draft).
- A1.2 Definitions (Commission). "'authorised representative' means a natural or legal person acting on behalf of the European Business Wallet owner in executing and operating functions of a designated European Business Wallet on the basis of an authorisation granted by a European Business Wallet owner"; "'mandate' means the authorisation granted by a European Business Wallet owner to an authorised representative" | Art 3(18), (19) | read. Both are natural or legal persons by definition; machines and agents are not mentioned.
- A1.3 Definitions (Council). Art 3(18) "deleted"; Art 3(19): "'authorisation' means the granting or recognition of a right or permission by a European Business Wallet owner to a European Business Wallet user to perform specified actions on specified resources or functionalities, and the corresponding access-control decision that permits each concrete request in accordance with applicable access-control policy" | Art 3(19) | read. The definition covers the decision per request, not only the grant. (RES-dec12 cites this as "Art 2(19)"; the article is Art 3, Art 2 is scope.)
- A1.4 User. "a natural or legal person, or a natural person representing another natural person or a legal person, that uses European Business Wallets" | Commission Art 3(22); Council Art 3(22) same; EP draft keeps it | read. A "legal person" can be a user; whether a service or device is a "legal person" user is not addressed.
- A1.5 Core function. Owners can "authorise multiple users to access and operate the European Business Wallet of the owner, and ... manage and revoke such authorisations" | Commission Art 5(1)(j) | read. Council adds "including authorisations associated to roles for the purposes of access to and operation of the European Business Wallet and without prejudice to any power of attorney or legal mandate" | Art 5(1)(j) | read. EP draft adds "with the possibility to create auditable and clearly defined and restricted delegations of powers, mandates and roles" | Amendment 64 | read (draft).
- A1.6 Authentication of representatives. Wallets "enable the use of qualified and non-qualified electronic attestations of attributes to allow European Business Wallet owners and their authorised representatives to authenticate themselves" | Commission Art 5(1)(h) | read.
- A1.7 Automatic interaction. Providers shall support protocols "to allow interaction with the European Business Wallets automatically without manual intervention or through direct user action" | Commission Art 6(1)(d) | read; Council Art 6(1)(d) "or through direct European Business Wallet user action" | read. This is the only EBW operative text that contemplates unattended use; it does not say under whose authority an automatic interaction runs.
- A1.8 Role integrity (Art 6(2)(b), Commission and Council). "mappings between roles and attributes are verifiable, auditable, revocable and traceable to their legitimate issuers"; "conflicts of roles, over-delegation, or expired authorisations are automatically detected and prevented in real time"; "all authorisation logic is interoperable across Member States" (Council: "between European Business Wallets") | read.
- A1.9 Recital 18 (Commission). "a mandate and role-based authorisation system"; "clearly defined technical mandates allowing the owner ... to grant full rights to generally use the solution and act on its behalf, and an administrative mandate, allowing the owner ... to assign roles and responsibilities to various users"; delegate "to multiple users, including employees or other authorised natural or legal persons"; "compatibility with the EU digital power of attorney, as established by Directive (EU) 2025/25"; access "controlled and auditable" | read. Council recital 18 replaces "mandate" by "authorisation" and adds "of a technical nature and to not create, limit, or otherwise affect any power of attorney or legal mandate provided under applicable national or Union law and procedures" | read. EP draft recital 18: authorisation system should "allow the use of European Digital Identity Wallets, but should not require users to have them" | read (draft).
- A1.10 Agents and assets. Recital 28 (Commission and Council): implementing powers allow "new technologies that would enable new use cases, such as agentic AI" (Council adds "or the provision of a digital identity to an owner's asset") | read. EP draft: new recital 20a: automated processes "executed by, for example, a digital agent or AI-driven agent authorised to perform actions under a valid, auditable and revocable authorisation issued by the European Business Wallet owner or user ... should always be verifiable and auditable and ensure an equivalent level of assurance and accountability compared with actions performed by a user"; new Art 3(19a) "'automated transaction' means a transaction executed by an authorised digital or AI-driven agent, performing actions under a valid, auditable and revocable authorisation" | Amendment 17 (recital 20a), Amendment 55 (Art 3(19a)) | read (draft report, not an adopted position).
- A1.11 Access-control mechanism (Annex 12; Commission Annex and Council Annex both). Decisions based on one or more of: "the electronic attestation of attributes of the acting subject"; "the formal role of the acting subjects within a recognised organisational structure or economic operator"; "the scope, validity and constraints of any mandate, delegation, or power of attorney"; "contextual information or policies and rules adopted at Union or national level" (12(1)). Outcomes "fine-grained and auditable": visibility "selective and conditioned on access rights"; "real-time validation of roles and mandates"; all events "logged, timestamped, and bound to cryptographically verifiable proofs of authorisation, suitable for audit and legal proceedings" (12(2)). Role mappings "verifiable, auditable, revocable"; "conflicts of roles, over-delegation, or expired authorisations are automatically detected and prevented in real time" (12(3)). Implementing acts to cover "formats for the representation of roles and attributes; interoperability mechanisms for mandates and delegations across wallets; protocols, policy language and constraint enforcement; requirements for secure logging" (12(4)) | SRC-EBW-ANNEX-CELLAR; SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex | read. No text names a depth limit for onward delegation or a four-eyes rule.
- A1.12 Authentication of wallet users (Annex 1, Commission and Council). Access only after authentication by "a notified electronic identification (eID) means" with at least assurance level substantial or "an alternative authentication mechanism recognised as equivalent" at least substantial | read. Annex 5(2): providers shall provide "mechanisms, independent of Wallets units, for the secure identification and authentication of Wallets users" | Commission Annex | read. A machine has no notified eID; the "alternative authentication mechanism" clause is the only route and its content is not specified.
- A1.13 Onboarding. Commission Art 6(1)(e): onboard the owner "remotely via an authorised representative with an electronic identification means of that authorised representative" at "substantial" or "high"; Council Art 6(1)(e): "via a legal representative or other persons that are lawfully empowered" at high; EP draft: level "high" only | read.
- A1.14 Revocation of the wallet and of unit attestations. Wallet validity revocable on owner request, compromise, "permanent or temporary cessation of activity" of the owner (Art 6(2)(f)). Annex 6: providers publish a revocation policy; inform affected users within 24 hours, with reason and consequences | read. Representatives must be informed of "the right to request revocation of their wallet unit attestation" (Art 7(6)(c)) | read. "Temporary cessation" is the only suspension-like wording in the EBW texts; it applies to the owner, not to a user or authorisation.
- A1.15 Logs and keys. Annex 7: log of signing, sealing, notifications of all transactions; fields are time, relying party, data types, reason for non-completion; accessible to the provider "where it is necessary" | read. The logged fields do not include the acting user or the authority relied on (Annex 12(2)(c) adds proofs of authorisation). Annex 8: users can "receive qualified certificates for qualified electronic signatures or seals" linked to "local, external, or remote" devices | read. Annex 3-5: back-end uses wallet secure cryptographic application and device to manage critical assets; keys protected | read.
- A1.16 Signature versus seal. Art 5(1)(d): "sign by means of qualified electronic signatures and seal by means of qualified electronic seals, as applicable"; recital 17: individuals with power to act "exercise their functions by signing" | read.
- A1.17 Equivalence. Art 4: the action of the owner using a core function "shall have the same legal effect as if the action had been lawfully carried out in person, in paper form"; Council limits equivalence to core functionalities and keeps "national administrative and procedural requirements" | read (Council per legislative train and cover note).
- A1.18 No EBW text read allocates liability between owner, user and provider, defines onward delegation depth, joint representation, suspension of an authorisation, or decommissioning (searched "liab", "suspen", "joint", "decommission", "sub-deleg").

### A2 Company law: power of attorney and representation

- A2.1 Digital EU power of attorney (Directive (EU) 2025/25, new Art 16c of Directive 2017/1132). Member States "shall ensure that, in order to carry out procedures within the scope of this Directive in another Member State, in particular the formation of companies, the registration or closure of branches, and cross-border conversions, mergers and divisions, companies listed in Annexes II and IIB can use a template for the digital EU power of attorney ... to authorise a person to represent the company" | SRC-DIR-2025-25 Art 16c(1) | read.
- A2.2 Creation and checks. It "shall be drawn up, amended or revoked in accordance with national requirements", which "shall at least include the verification by courts, notaries or other competent authorities of the identity, legal capacity and authority to represent the company of the person granting, amending or revoking"; it is "authenticated by means of trust services" and its granting, amendment or revocation is "compatible for use with the European Digital Identity Wallet" | Art 16c(1) | read.
- A2.3 Effect. "shall be accepted as evidence of the authorised person's entitlement to represent the company as specified in the document" | Art 16c(2) | read. Recital 28: "without prejudice to national rules on legal and statutory representation or any other types of powers of attorney"; "only exist in digital form" | read.
- A2.4 Register and template. Member States "may require" filing of the power, amendments and revocations with a register (Art 16c(3)); template with "at least data fields about the scope of representation, the person authorised to represent the company and the type of representation" (Art 16c(4)) | read. Implementing acts on the template were due "by 31 July 2026" (Art 24 as amended); not found published as of 3 Oct 2026 (company-law note, carried).
- A2.5 Limits of reach. The power is for "procedures within the scope of this Directive" in another Member State, for companies in Annexes II and IIB (limited liability and public companies). Employees acting day-to-day, machines and agents are not within that wording. Transposition by 31 July 2027, application from 31 July 2028 | Directive 2025/25 Art 4 | read.
- A2.6 Joint representation in company law. The register shall show "whether the persons authorised to represent the company may do so alone or are required to act jointly" | SRC-DIR-2017-1132 Art 14(d)(i) | read (original 2017 text; consolidation not obtained).
- A2.7 Limits of organs. "The limits on the powers of the organs of the company, arising under the statutes or from a decision of the competent organs, may not be relied on as against third parties, even if they have been disclosed" | Art 9(2) | read. Art 9(3): where national law allows representation by "several persons acting jointly", that law may allow it to be relied on against third parties if it relates to "the general power of representation". These rules concern organs; they do not say how limits on an employee's or agent's power of attorney are treated.

### A3 eIDAS and ARF

- A3.1 Natural person representing a legal person. PID "uniquely represents the natural person, legal person or the natural person representing the natural or legal person" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 5a(5)(f) | read; "user" same wording Art 3(5a) | read.
- A3.2 Attestation categories. Annex VI item 9: "Powers and mandates to represent natural or legal persons" | read. Non-qualified attestations: "shall not be denied legal effect or admissibility as evidence ... on the sole ground" that they are electronic or not qualified (Art 45b(1)); qualified and public-sector attestations have "the same legal effect as lawfully issued attestations in paper form" (Art 45b(2)) | read.
- A3.3 Revoked attestation status is final. "Where a qualified electronic attestation of attributes has been revoked after initial issuance, it shall lose its validity from the moment of its revocation and its status shall not in any circumstances be reverted" | Art 45d(4) | read. Same for qualified certificates for signatures and seals (Art 28(4), 38(4)). Temporary suspension: "Member States may lay down national rules on temporary suspension of qualified certificates for electronic seals" (and signatures); a suspended certificate "shall lose its validity for the period of suspension" and the status must be visible | Art 38(5), 28(5) | read. For attestations of attributes no suspension rule was found. Wallets themselves: Member State "shall, without undue delay, suspend the provision and the use" on breach, and revoke after three months if not remedied (Art 5e) | read.
- A3.4 Seals for non-human assets. "electronic seals can be used to authenticate any digital asset of the legal person, such as software code or servers" | SRC-REG-2014-910-ORIG (new) recital 65 | read. A seal is "uniquely linked to the creator of the seal" and made with data the creator "can, with a high level of confidence under its control" use (Art 36(a), (c)); creator of a seal is a legal person (Art 3(24)); qualified seal enjoys "the presumption of integrity of the data and of correctness of the origin" (Art 35(2)) | read. A signatory is a natural person (Art 3(9)).
- A3.5 e-mandates. Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 recital 19 refers to "enabling powers of representation and e-mandates" through EUDI Wallets | SRC-REG-2024-1183 (new) | partly-read.
- A3.6 ARF. Topic 28 "Wallet Unit for legal persons": LP_01 to LP_03 "Empty". Topic 29: RP_01 rulebooks for "representation attestations issued to a natural person representing another natural person" with attributes for "a validity period, the nature of the representation, and the operations the representative is authorised to perform"; RP_02 attestations "short-lived or that all entities which, according to applicable law, must have the ability to request revocation ... are able to do so" | SRC-ARF-HLR | read. ARF has no pattern for an employee, machine or agent acting for a legal person.
- A3.7 Who revokes in the personal wallet. Only providers of PID or attestations, and the wallet provider for unit attestations; triggers include request of the wallet user; no "employer request" or "employment ended" trigger | SRC-CIR-2024-2977 Art 5; SRC-CIR-2025-1569 Art 4; SRC-CIR-2024-2979 Art 7 | carried (RES-employee A3.7, read there).
- A3.8 Right-to-act check. Issuers "verify that the requester of the attestation has the right to act on behalf of the person that is the subject of the attestation" | SRC-CIR-2025-1569 Annex II 2(a) | carried.
- A3.9 Personal wallet limits. User has "full control" (Art 5a(14)); free QES may be limited by Member States to "non-professional purposes" (Art 5a(5)(g)); EUDIW at assurance level high (Art 5a(11)) | carried, read there. EBW proposal limits the mandatory EUDIW to natural persons (recital 55, Art 20) | carried.

### A4 WE BUILD material

- A4.1 PoX model. "Power of X (PoX) is the common semantic model used to describe the authority of a natural or legal person to act on behalf of a Economic Operator"; three authority models: PoA ("authority explicitly delegated ... through a legally valid mandate defining the scope, duration and limitations"), PoR ("authority derived directly from law, company statutes or an official register"), PoE ("authority granted by an organization to an employee or contractor to perform defined operational activities or access specific services"). "As a BU4 Onsite Berlin decission, PoE will NOT be covered in MVP Pilot scenarios" | SRC-WEBUILD-RB rb-poa-pox.md (v0.7, 2026-07-14, as cloned at de79cca) | read. The registered gap note refers to a v0.8 of 2026-09-04; section numbers below are those of the clone.
- A4.2 Attributes. Proxy is "mutually exclusive": legal-entity proxy (EBWOID, legal name) or natural-person proxy (names, birth data); no machine or agent proxy type. PoA scope has Type, GrantDate, a boolean Limitation, Faculty, IssuingAuthority(Type) QEAA/PubEAA/EAA, AssuranceLevel, optional Constraint (Type, Description; example "Prohibition to operate after business hours"), Mandator (natural person), ServiceAccess (relying party, services). PoR "Position" values include `Sole Administrator`, `Supportive Administrator`, `Joint Administrator`, `Board Member`; a Joint Administrator acts validly "only when performed collectively" | rb-poa-pox 2.2, 2.3, 2.9 | read. The attestation records the position; no attribute lets a relying party read how many co-signatories are needed or collect them.
- A4.3 Use. "Possession of a valid attestaion SHALL NOT automatically imply authorization"; relying party verifies identity of holder, integrity, issuer trust, status, scope, validity, restrictions; PID binding where national law requires (4.6 to 4.8) | read.
- A4.4 Lifecycle. Expiry is automatic. Revocation "permanently terminates" and "SHALL NOT regain it's valid status" (6.4). Suspension: "Some jurisdictions permit temporary suspension of delegated authority. Where supported ... During suspension the attestation SHALL be treated as invalid" (6.5). Status values defined: `active`, `revoked`, `expired`, `unknown`; "Relying Parties SHALL reject every attestation except those having status `active`" (6.9) - no `suspended` value. Revocation triggers marked SHALL include principal withdraws, company dissolved, "Representative leaves organization", representative dies; reason code `EMPLOYMENT_TERMINATED`; short-lived means validity not over 24 hours and then "expiration SHALL replace revocation" (6.6, 6.12, 6.17); revocation authority "SHALL be defined by applicable legislation" (6.7) | read.
- A4.5 Employee rulebooks (drafts). Employee Credential issued into the employee's natural-person wallet, issuer "must immediately revoke" on change; AuthorisedSignatories with "Statutory Power" or "Delegated Power"; Contact Person attestations held in the company wallet | rb-employee v0.1, rb-authorised-signatories, rb-contact-person | carried (RES-employee A3.11, A3.13).
- A4.6 Machine-to-machine and consent. cs-02 (Credential Presentation, v1.0): wallets "MUST NOT ... Auto-consent" and the holder "MUST explicitly consent" after the wallet displays verifier, types and claims. rb-eInvoice (v0.71) describes SC5 Scenario 4 as "wallet-to-wallet and machine-to-machine, without an intermediary access point", issued per invoice by the supplier's EBW and "presented ... in a machine-to-machine context"; rb-approved-supplier and rb-authorized-service-provider also use "machine-to-machine OpenID4VP flow". Handshake rulebook: Holder EBW backend decides "according to the holder EBW owners internal policies"; the front-end is involved "if verification or presentation decisions are made by a natural person" | SRC-WEBUILD-ARCH cs-02; SRC-WEBUILD-RB | read.
- A4.7 EBWOID and revocation scenarios. EBWOID: a natural person "requests and obtains the EBWOID on behalf of the Economic Operator"; blueprint 06 lists unauthorised use by representatives as a revocation scenario | carried (RES-employee A3.12, A9.3). No WE BUILD text on agents or technical users was found (searched "AI agent", "agentic", "non-human", "technical user", "service account", "IoT").

### A5 Credential and protocol specifications (format-level facts)

- A5.1 W3C VCDM 2.0 (Recommendation 15 May 2025). `validFrom`/`validUntil` express validity (4.9); `credentialStatus` is for "information related to the status of a verifiable credential, such as whether it is suspended or revoked" (4.10); `termsOfUse` carries policies (5.5, carried). The model has no delegation or onward-delegation construct; "delegating trust" appears only in a warning about certificate authorities | SRC-VCDM-2.0 | read (sections).
- A5.2 Bitstring Status List 1.0 (W3C Recommendation 15 May 2025). `statusPurpose` `revocation` "Used to cancel the validity of a verifiable credential. This status is not reversible"; `suspension` "Used to temporarily prevent the acceptance of a verifiable credential. This status is reversible" | SRC-VC-BSL-1.0 (new) | read (sections).
- A5.3 Token Status List (IETF draft-ietf-oauth-status-list-21, June 2026, expires 23 Dec 2026). Status types `0x01 INVALID` ("revoked, annulled, taken back, recalled or cancelled") and `0x02 SUSPENDED` ("temporarily invalid ... usually temporary") | SRC-IETF-OAUTH-STATUS-LIST (new) | read (status types). CIR 2026/1731 cites a Token Status List draft (carried, RES-dec12).
- A5.4 RFC 8693 token exchange (Standards Track, 2020). Delegation: "principal A still has its own identity separate from B ... any actions taken are being taken by A representing B. In a sense, A is an agent for B"; impersonation: "A is B within the context of the rights authorized". Chain: nested `act` claims; "the consumer of a token MUST only consider the token's top-level claims and the party identified as the current actor ... Prior actors ... are informational only"; `may_act` states "that one party is authorized to become the actor"; token revocation propagation "is not a general property of the STS protocol" | SRC-RFC-8693 | read (sections 1.1, 2.1, 4.1, 4.4, 5).
- A5.5 RFC 9396 Rich Authorization Requests (Standards Track, 2023). `authorization_details` with `type`, `locations`, `actions`, `datatypes`, `identifier`, `privileges`; type-specific fields such as `instructedAmount` {currency, amount} in the payment example; "The allowable values of all fields are determined by the API being protected" | SRC-RFC-9396 | read (sections 2, 2.2). This is where amount limits can be expressed at protocol level.
- A5.6 GNAP RFC 9635 (Standards Track, October 2024). "a mechanism for delegating authorization to a piece of software"; the client "is operated by the end user, or it runs autonomously on behalf of a resource owner"; client instances are identified by key; access tokens can be rotated and revoked via a token management URI; the access structure is "analogous to" RFC 9396. No chain or onward-delegation construct was found in the sections searched | SRC-RFC-9635 | read (sections 1, 2.1, 6, 8).
- A5.7 UCAN 1.0.0 (community specification, not a standards-body product). Delegation "Pass, attenuate, and secure authority"; "Each direct delegation MUST either directly restate or attenuate (diminish) its capabilities"; `exp` required, `nbf` optional; "Delegation provides a way to 'transfer authority without transferring cryptographic keys'"; `sub: null` ("Powerline") delegates all future delegations and "MUST NOT be used as the root". Revocation: "MUST be immutable and irreversible" (so no suspension) | SRC-UCAN-SPEC, SRC-UCAN-DELEGATION, SRC-UCAN-REVOCATION (new) | read (sections). Identifiers are DIDs; no link to a legal person or a trust list.
- A5.8 ODRL 2.2 (W3C Recommendation 15 Feb 2018). Policies with Permission, Prohibition, Duty, Constraint; Offer and Agreement "granting of Rules from assigner to assignee Parties"; "constraints (e.g., temporal or spatial constraints) and duties"; no delegation or revocation vocabulary found (searched the model and the common vocabulary) | SRC-ODRL-IM-2.2 (new, listed in RES-dec12 new sources), SRC-ODRL-VOCAB-2.2 | read (search).

### A9 Assurance level (summary)

- Wallet unit user authentication: at least substantial (Annex 1, Commission and Council). Onboarding via a representative: substantial or high (Commission), high (Council, EP draft). EBW critical operations: substantial (Art 6(1)(l)). EUDIW: high (Art 5a(11)). PoA and PoR rulebook attribute `AssuranceLevel` is by origin of information, "always High" where a business register is the source (rb-poa-pox 2.2). Digital EU power of attorney: verification of identity, legal capacity and authority by courts, notaries or competent authorities, then authentication by trust services (Directive 2025/25 Art 16c(1)). AP2 and RFC 8693 state no assurance level. No source ties a required assurance level to the type of actor or to the value or risk of the delegated authority (read in all sources above).

## (C) OPTIONS

Options describe how authority could be carried; none is recommended. "Enables / restricts / leaves open" refers to what the sources read support.

### Option A. Roles and authorisations inside the wallet, with unit users (EBW-internal access control)
The owner's EBW holds users and roles; each employee, service account or agent is a "user" of the owner's wallet unit with owner-managed authorisations; decisions by the provider's access-control mechanism (Annex 12).
Enables
- A native core function with owner-side manage and revoke (Art 5(1)(j)), role-conflict and over-delegation detection (Art 6(2)(b)), real-time validation of roles and mandates and proof-bound logs (Annex 12(2)); employees named in recital 18; automatic interaction without manual intervention (Art 6(1)(d)); EP draft would add agents as holders of "valid, auditable and revocable" authorisations (recital 20a).
- Provider-side termination is simple for the owner: remove the user, cascade within the provider; the user is informed within 24 hours of a unit attestation revocation (Annex 6(2)).
- Combines with seals and qualified certificates for users (Annex 8).
Restricts
- Authorisation is "of a technical nature" and creates no legal power (Council recital 18); relying parties outside the provider cannot see it unless it is presented as a credential; "interoperable between European Business Wallets" is required but the format is left to implementing acts (Annex 12(4)).
- User authentication is at assurance level substantial by a notified eID or an equivalent mechanism (Annex 1): employees need a personal eID; machines and agents have no defined route.
- The definitions of user cover persons only (A1.4); no onward-delegation depth, joint-approval or suspension text (A1.18).
- Provider concentration: logs readable by the provider "where necessary" (Annex 7(5)).
Leaves open
- Whether a machine or an agent may be a "user", what identifies it, how its key is custodied, how a relying party outside the wallet sees the authority, and what the audit record of the acting user contains.

### Option B. Mandates as verifiable credentials with scope and limits (issued by the owner's wallet)
The owner's EBW issues a mandate attestation (principal, agent, scope, limits, validity, status, optionally onward-delegation flag and depth) to the actor's key; presented to relying parties and checked against status.
Enables
- Relying parties outside the provider can verify who authorised what (Annex 12(1)(a), (c); Art 5(1)(g) chains; Art 5(1)(h) authentication with attestations); non-qualified attestations keep legal effect as evidence (eIDAS Art 45b(1)); validity and status in VCDM and SD-JWT VC mechanisms; suspension available through status lists (BSL, Token Status List draft).
- Patterns exist: WE BUILD PoX with constraints, mandator, service access, status and reason codes; ARF RP_01/RP_02 for persons; AP2 open and closed mandates bound to the agent key (`cnf`) with short `exp`.
- Machines: a mandate or "asset identity" credential can be bound to a device key; sealing under the legal person's seal for non-human assets is contemplated in eIDAS recital 65.
Restricts
- No vocabulary for machines or agents in any rulebook (rb-poa-pox proxy is a natural or legal person; PoE out of MVP); AI-agent mandate schema is unspecified (AIF-010 is a derived requirement).
- A mandate credential does not by itself authorise; relying party policy decides (rb-poa-pox 4.7; K03 TRU-064).
- Status mechanisms differ: the rulebook has no `suspended` value; qualified attestations cannot be un-revoked (Art 45d(4)); issuers of attestations in a personal wallet cannot be forced to check status in real time (ARF RP_02 offers short life as alternative).
- Presentation protocols assume holder consent (cs-02); unattended presentation is not specified (A4.6).
Leaves open
- Issuer of the mandate (owner as non-qualified EAA, QTSP, register-based PubEAA); legal weight versus a power of attorney; depth and attenuation rules; who keeps the status list and how fast; quantitative limits vocabulary.

### Option C. Legal power of attorney and representation credentials (Directive 2025/25 and company law)
Authority is evidenced by the digital EU power of attorney (Art 16c) and by register-based PoR/PoA credentials (rb-poa-pox, rb-authorised-signatories), drawn up under national law with notarial or court verification.
Enables
- Legal capacity: acceptance "as evidence of the authorised person's entitlement to represent the company" in all Member States (Art 16c(2)); register-based joint-versus-alone information (Dir 2017/1132 Art 14(d)(i)); trust-service authentication and EUDIW compatibility (Art 16c(1)); high assurance by origin; revocable per national rules; EBW recital 18 requires compatibility.
- Matches P4 and the Council's "without prejudice" wording.
Restricts
- Reach: company procedures within Directive 2017/1132 in another Member State, companies of Annexes II and IIB only; implementing acts on the template overdue; transposition 2027, application 2028 (A2.4, A2.5).
- Creation needs courts, notaries or competent authorities (A2.2): unsuitable as the instrument for day-to-day employee roles, machines or agents (a driver judgement; sources are silent on operational use).
- Limits on organs cannot be relied on against third parties (Dir 2017/1132 Art 9(2)), so internal limits may need a different instrument; the treatment of limits in a power of attorney is national law.
- Joint representation is carried by the register and by the position attribute, not by an enforceable n-of-m presentation protocol.
Leaves open
- How a wallet-based relying party combines a PoR/PoA with an employee identity; how fast revocation reaches relying parties; whether a machine or agent can be an "authorised person" in any Member State (not stated).

### Option D. Token-based delegation at the protocol layer (OAuth 2.0 / GNAP, UCAN-style capabilities)
Authority is carried as short-lived access tokens or capability chains issued by an authorisation server or by key-holders: RFC 8693 `act`/`may_act`, RFC 9396 `authorization_details`, DPoP/`cnf` key binding, GNAP, or UCAN delegations.
Enables
- Runtime enforcement per request, audience and scope binding, amount and action limits (RFC 9396 `instructedAmount`), distinguishing actor from principal (RFC 8693 delegation), short validity, key rotation and token revocation (GNAP 6.2), and works for unattended software ("runs autonomously on behalf of a resource owner", GNAP).
- Fits MCP, A2A client credentials, OPC UA AccessTokens and AAS ABAC claims (OPC 10000-2, IDTA-01004), i.e. existing M2M stacks.
- UCAN: attenuation as a rule, delegation without key transfer, offline verifiability.
Restricts
- Tokens do not identify the legal person or its authority to a third party; issuer is an authorisation server per domain; revocation propagation across exchanged tokens "is not a general property" (RFC 8693); only the current actor counts for access control (RFC 8693 4.1); UCAN revocation is irreversible and has no suspension; GNAP, RFC 8693 and RFC 9396 define no onward-delegation depth.
- Not an EBW core function; no EU text names it; legal effect depends on the owner's own records.
Leaves open
- Binding to a legal person and trust list; audit evidence suitable "for legal proceedings"; cross-domain federation and recursive delegation (named unsolved by OIDF); mapping between credential scopes and token scopes.

### Combinations
- Option E (A + B). Roles and users inside the wallet for access to wallet functions; the same authorisations exported as mandate credentials when a relying party or another wallet must verify them. Enables P1, P2, P4 (technical vs legal separation), P9, P13. Restricts: two representations of one authority must be kept consistent (Council Art 6(2)(b) "traceable to their legitimate issuers" helps); mapping rules not defined. Leaves open: the lead system for revocation.
- Option F (B + D). Wallet issues the mandate credential and binds the actor key; an authorisation server derives short-lived, audience-bound tokens from it (as DEC-10 option O4). Enables runtime limits and chain notation (`act`) with a verifiable root. Restricts: mapping of mandate scope to token scope; chain revocation. Leaves open: where the policy decision point sits (Annex 12 does not say).
- Option G (C + B + A, by actor class). Legal instruments (C) for statutory and registered authority and for cross-border procedures, mandate credentials (B) for operational authority of employees and agents, wallet users (A) for internal access, protocol tokens (D) at run time for machines and agents. Enables use of each source where it applies. Restricts: four mechanisms and four status regimes; propagation between them unspecified. Leaves open: precedence rules when sources disagree (for example an internal limit against a register entry).

## (E) CONFLICTS (between sources)

1. Annex 12 exists in the Commission text. RES-employee and RES-dec12 say Annex 12 is Council-only and that the Commission Annex was not available; the Commission Annex (SRC-EBW-ANNEX-CELLAR) has the same point 12 (A1.11). The Commission body lacks the "role-associated authorisations" wording of Art 5(1)(j) that the Council adds. Existing requirement notes (FUN-055 "Council only") need checking.
2. Legal meaning of authority. Commission recital 18 speaks of "mandates" and "compatibility with the EU digital power of attorney"; Council recital 18 deletes the mandate, says authorisations are "of a technical nature" and create or limit no power of attorney; EP draft recital 18 wants a "comprehensive framework". K03 requirements EBW-CON-005, GOV-028, LEG-069 depend on the Council wording; TRU-058 on the Commission one.
3. Natural-person-only definitions versus agents and machines. Art 3(18), (19) (Commission) and Art 3(19), (22) (Council) speak of natural or legal persons; EP draft adds automated transactions by "digital or AI-driven agent" (Art 3(19a)); Council recital 28 adds assets. K03 includes 27 AIF requirements on agent mandates with no operative EBW text for them.
4. Automatic interaction versus consent. Art 6(1)(d) (automatic interaction without manual intervention) and rb-eInvoice Scenario 4 (M2M) against cs-02 "MUST NOT ... Auto-consent" and the holder "MUST explicitly consent"; DCP says human-consent presentation protocols "are not applicable" to data-space participants.
5. Suspension. rb-poa-pox 6.5 says a suspended attestation is invalid, but 6.9 lists no `suspended` status and 6.9 makes every non-`active` status rejected; eIDAS Art 45d(4) and UCAN make revocation irreversible while BSL, Token Status List and eIDAS 28(5)/38(5) offer suspension; the EBW texts read give no suspension of an authorisation (A8).
6. Onboarding assurance. Commission "substantial or high"; Council and EP draft "high" (Art 6(1)(e)); unit user authentication substantial in the Annex (A1.12, A1.13).

## (H) REQUIREMENT CANDIDATES FOUND (for `ebw-add-requirements`, not written)

- Machine, device and service authority: where the owner delegates authority to a non-human actor, the delegation shall identify the actor by a key bound to the owner's legal-person identity and state scope, limits and validity (derived; hooks Art 6(1)(d), Council recital 28).
- Termination: where a person leaves, a machine is decommissioned or an agent is retired, the owner shall revoke the corresponding authorisation and keys, and relying parties shall see the status within a stated time (driver; WE BUILD 6.6 for persons only).
- Suspension: a user authorisation or mandate credential shall support a reversible suspended state distinct from revocation (driver; BSL and Token Status List; rb-poa-pox inconsistency).
- Onward delegation: a delegated authority shall state whether it may be delegated further and to what depth, and every hop shall attenuate (derived; RFC 8693, UCAN; AIF-025 is P2).
- Joint representation for wallet operations: an act requiring two or more representatives shall present all required co-authorisations to the relying party (driver; Dir 2017/1132 Art 14(d)(i); TRU-168 is P3).
- Assurance by actor class: the assurance level required to create or use a delegated authority shall depend on the actor class and the scope granted (driver).
