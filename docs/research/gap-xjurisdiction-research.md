## Findings by topic
1. **EUID.** Reach is limited: Annex II/IIB companies (2025/25 Art 16(1)); BRIS queries on legal entities that are not listed companies use "other registration number" (CIR 2021/1042 point 16.3.1). Third-country companies get a branch identifier (Art 36(4)(g), (5) of 2017/1132 as amended); its basis, Art 29(4), was not located in 2025/25 (to verify). Council recital 36 says the EBW framework "should rely" on EUID issuance and recording, also for AMLR entities: recital, not a duty, no requirement written.
2. **Unique identifier / EBWOID.** Council adds creation triggers (Art 9(2)) and a one-year deadline (Art 9(4), Art 8(7)); Commission text has neither. Council deletes the supervisory body's liaison role towards identification data providers (Commission Art 13(4)(b)). Neither text says who creates the identifier, whether the creating body is the same as the EBWOID provider, or how uniqueness is checked for EU operators without EUID (only for third-country operators, Art 18(6)). Gap, left open.
3. **LEI.** CIR 2021/1042 carries LEI as optional "Alternate ID" next to the EUID. GLEIF page: "Every LEI is unique and can represent only one entity", but LEI is not issued by a public register and covers only entities that obtained one; Art 9 provides no route to make it the unique identifier (D requirement written, to verify). EN 319 412-1 could not be re-fetched (ETSI returned an HTML page); no new clause extracted. vLEI covered by CON-002/003.
4. **Levels of assurance.** Council Art 6(1)(e) requires onboarding eID at level high; Commission text says substantial or high (TRU-008) and Council Annex point 1 requires substantial or higher for wallet unit access (TRU-070). The three levels differ by purpose (onboarding vs. access), but the Council text is stricter on onboarding: flag for review. eIDAS Art 6(1) recognition applies only to public sector services; Art 11a identity matching covers natural persons only (not written). CIR 2015/1502 has no level for legal persons in the sense of a wallet owner, only the registration table, and the status check was missing.
5. **Third-country recognition.** eIDAS Art 14 needs a Union agreement (conditions 14(2) written; reciprocity (b) noted in rationale only). EBW Art 17 recognition by implementing act: Council adds criteria (data protection, cybersecurity, independence from "high-risk governments"). Equivalence is not a GDPR adequacy decision; a D requirement keeps Chapter V applicable.
6. **Legal compliance across jurisdictions.** GDPR Arts 44, 46(1) written; Art 45 and 49 left out (permissions, covered by the same rule). Sanctions: no EBW source mentions them; one derived requirement based on Reg 269/2014 with the interpretation (wallet services as economic resources) flagged. Directive 2025/25 binds Member States (transposition 31 July 2027, application 31 July 2028); no source gives a rule for conflicts between national implementations of EUCC or power of attorney beyond Art 16d (no legalisation, EBW-LEG-055).

## EBWOID selective-disclosure conflict
Sources:
- COM(2025) 838 and Council Art 5(1)(b): the wallet shall enable the owner to "selectively disclose European Business Wallet owner identification data and attributes contained in electronic attestations of attributes".
- Art 8(5): the EBWOID "shall contain at least" official name and the unique identifier. Art 9(4): one identifier per owner. Art 10(3a) (Council): the identifier goes to the Directory.
- WE BUILD rulebook rb-ebwoid (EBW-INT-057, ecosystem): EBWOID attributes "SHALL NOT be selectively disclosable"; presentation is atomic.
- eIDAS Art 5a(16)(a) and recital 59: tracking, linkability and data minimisation for the EUDI Wallet; Art 5 pseudonyms (natural persons). Not applied to the EBW by the proposal (EBW-NFR-113 notes this).

Analysis:
1. The two rules sit on different levels. Art 5(1)(b) is a capability of the wallet, Art 8(5) defines the content of the attestation. Neither says that the identifier must be disclosed in every presentation, so a mandatory identifier in the attestation does not by itself conflict with selective disclosure.
2. The rulebook closes the gap by binding all EBWOID attributes into one disclosure unit. That is compatible with Art 5(1)(b) only if selection is read at the level of attestations ("present the EBWOID or not") and attributes of other attestations. Under a literal reading of "owner identification data and attributes contained in electronic attestations" it is not, because the owner cannot withhold the name when sharing the identifier.
3. Privacy: for legal persons the EUID is public (BRIS), so correlation risk is low. For sole traders and self-employed persons (Council recital 36), the identifier can identify a natural person; GDPR minimisation applies and a single persistent identifier presented to every relying party enables correlation. The EUDI Wallet rule on unlinkability is not mirrored.
4. Trust: a relying party that needs a verified identity needs the identifier; a relying party that needs only "is a company in Member State X" does not. No source defines these request profiles.
5. Options (facts, no choice): (a) atomic EBWOID, selective disclosure only between attestations (rulebook); (b) attribute-level disclosure with the identifier always bound to the name; (c) two attestations (name plus identifier, and a derived minimal one). Candidates written: the scheme must state the disclosure rules (EBW-INT new, D); a sole trader's identifier is disclosed only as requested (DAT new, D); LEI/secondary identifiers are additions to the Art 9 identifier (INT new, D). Open for the Commission scheme under Art 8(6) and the implementing acts under Art 8(7)/9(4); recommend that reviewers ask the WE BUILD rulebook owners to confirm the reading under (a).
