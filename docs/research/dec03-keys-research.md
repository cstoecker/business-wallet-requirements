# RES-dec03-keys: key custody and wallet architecture (DEC-03, cluster K04)

Scope: facts from primary sources only; no recommendation. Date of work: 2026-10-03. Question: where do the keys of an organisation live (device, hosted back-end, hardware security module, remote sealing and signing), and what does that mean for control, assurance and exit? Related: wallet unit attestation (K04), assurance and exit.

## (A) FACTS FROM SOURCES

### A1 What the EBW texts define and require (unit, back-end, secure device)
- K1.1 Wallet unit includes secure device (Commission). "provided by a provider to a European Business Wallet to a specific European Business Wallet owner" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 3(25); the unit is "a unique configuration" that includes front-end, back-end, wallet secure cryptographic applications (WSCA) and devices (WSCD) | read.
- K1.2 Council drops WSCA and WSCD from the definitions. Art 3(25) reads "includes European Business Wallet front-end and European Business Wallet back-end provided by a provider"; Art 3(26) lists front-end and back-end only; Art 3(28) and (29) are marked "deleted" | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Art 3(25), (26), (28), (29) | read.
- K1.3 Definitions of WSCA and WSCD (Commission). WSCD: "a tamper-resistant device that provides an environment that is linked to and used by the wallet secure cryptographic application" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 3(29); WSCA manages "critical assets" (Art 3(27), (28)) | read.
- K1.4 Back-end definition. "the server-side components, including software, services, and infrastructure" that form part of the unit | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 3(43); Council text same | read.
- K1.5 Back-end must use a WSCA and WSCD. "European Business Wallet back-end shall use at least one Wallet secure cryptographic application and Wallets secure cryptographic device to manage critical assets." | SRC-EBW-ANNEX-CELLAR Annex point 3(1); SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex point 3(1) (same wording) | read.
- K1.6 Commission: critical operations at assurance level substantial. Annex 3(3) and 4(1)(g): operations "shall be performed in accordance with the requirements for the characteristics and design of electronic identification means at assurance level substantial" | SRC-EBW-ANNEX-CELLAR Annex points 3(3), 4(1)(b), 4(1)(g); also SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 6(1)(l) | read.
- K1.7 Council removes those assurance-level clauses. Annex 3(3), 4(1)(b) and 4(1)(g) read "deleted"; Art 6(1)(l) is absent from the Council Article 6(1) list (list ends at point (k)). Council Annex 4(1) addresses "secure cryptographic applications and devices" jointly | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex 3, 4; Art 6(1) | read.
- K1.8 Commission: unit attestation holds keys protected by a device. Art 6(1)(k): "containing public keys and corresponding private keys protected by a wallet secure cryptographic device" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 6(1)(k); Annex 5(1) | read. Council Art 6(1)(k) shortens to "for the provision of European Business Wallet unit attestations to all European Business Wallet units"; Annex 5(1) keeps the key-protection wording | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 | read.
- K1.9 Onboarding assurance. Commission Art 6(1)(e): "substantial" or "high" assurance of the authorised representative's eID; Council Art 6(1)(e): assurance level "high"; Council Annex 1 keeps "substantial" for user access to the unit | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL; SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 | read.
- K1.10 User authentication before any function. "Access to the European Business Wallets Unit shall be granted only after the European Business Wallets user has been successfully authenticated" | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex point 1 (Commission Annex point 1 same idea) | read. Annex 5(2): "mechanisms, independent of Wallets units, for the secure identification and authentication of Wallets users" | read.
- K1.11 Signing and sealing. Core function: "sign by means of qualified electronic signatures and seal by means of qualified electronic seals, as applicable" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 5(1)(d) | read.
- K1.12 Qualified devices may be local, external or remote. Annex 8(1): certificates "linked to qualified signature or seal creation devices that are either local, external, or remote" in relation to the unit; 8(2): the solution can interface with "local, external, or remotely managed" devices | SRC-EBW-ANNEX-CELLAR Annex point 8; SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex point 8 (same) | read.
- K1.13 Signature creation applications. May be provided by the wallet provider, a trust service provider or a relying party (Annex 9(1)). They "may either be integrated into or be external to" the back-end; if integrated and relying on a remote qualified device they "shall support the application programming interface" set out in implementing acts | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex point 9(1), 9(3); SRC-EBW-ANNEX-CELLAR Annex point 9 | read.
- K1.14 Unit attestation shown to relying parties. Commission and Council Annex 13(4): the unit shall present its unit attestation "to European Business Wallet relying parties or European Business Wallet units that request it" (Council wording) | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex point 13(4); SRC-EBW-ANNEX-CELLAR Annex 13(4) | read. Compare K4.6 (EUDI Wallet: relying party does not receive it).
- K1.15 Conformity regime of the provider. Commission Art 11(2)(e): notification includes "a declaration of conformity with the requirements of this Regulation"; qualified trust service providers are not reviewed (Art 11(3)). Council Art 11(2aa): applicants "demonstrate conformity with the requirements in Article 5, 6, 7 and the Annex through a self-assessment report", with a risk assessment (2a) and reuse of valid reports under eIDAS (2b). No source in the EBW texts requires a certificate for the WSCA, the WSCD or the solution | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 11; SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Art 11 | read.
- K1.16 Security duties of providers. Art 7(3): comply with "the requirements set out in Article 19a of Regulation (EU) 910/2014" (not applicable to qualified trust service providers); Art 7(4): NIS2; Art 7(5): suppliers must "conform to the relevant security standards" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 7(3)-(5) | read. Council adds periodic update of the self-assessment "every 24 months or immediately" on a significant incident or a substantial change to "cryptographic components" (Art 7(6b)) | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 | read.
- K1.17 Provider location and control. "shall be established in the Union, have their principal place of business and main operations in the Union"; they "shall not be subject to control by a third country or by a third-country entity" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 7(2) | read.
- K1.18 Export, import, exit. Art 5(1)(l): export of data "in the event of termination of service or revocation of the notification of the provider"; Council adds point (la) to "import their data" for "data portability across European Business Wallet providers" and Annex 10 "secure export, import and portability" "in at least an open format"; Commission Annex 10 ties portability to assurance level "substantial". Art 7(6)(f): provider notifies owners and ensures "transfer or deletion" of owner data on instruction | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 5(1)(l), 7(6)(f); SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Art 5(1)(l), (la), Annex 10; SRC-EBW-ANNEX-CELLAR Annex point 10 | read. None of these texts says whether private keys can be exported or must be re-created.
- K1.19 Provider access to logs. Annex 7(5): logs "shall be accessible to the European Business Wallets provider, where it is necessary for the provision of European Business Wallets services" | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex point 7(5) | read. Neither EBW text contains the explicit prior-consent condition that CIR 2024/2979 Art 9(5) sets for provider access to EUDI Wallet logs (compare K4.4).
- K1.20 Absence. The words custody, hosted, HSM and hardware security module do not occur in the proposal or in the Council text (searched); "cloud" occurs only in the consultation summary ("a cloud-based solution like the European Business Wallets") and in a recital on AI and cloud. No provision of the proposal or Council text states where the keys of an owner must be held | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL; SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 | read (searched).

### A2 Signatory, seal creator, qualified devices and remote management (eIDAS)
- K2.1 Who creates. Signatory: "a natural person who creates an electronic signature"; creator of a seal: "a legal person who creates an electronic seal" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 3(9), 3(24) | read.
- K2.2 Remote devices as a qualified service. "a qualified electronic signature creation device that is managed by a qualified trust service provider in accordance with Article 29a on behalf of a signatory"; for seals: "on behalf of a seal creator" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 3(23a), 3(23b) | read.
- K2.3 Only a QTSP may manage remote keys. Creation data are generated or managed or duplicated for back-up "only on behalf of the signatory, at the request of the signatory, and by a qualified trust service provider" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 29(1a); Art 39 applies mutatis mutandis to seals; Art 39a: Art 29a "shall apply mutatis mutandis" | read.
- K2.4 Back-up of remote creation data. Duplicates for back-up only if "the security of the duplicated datasets must be at the same level as for the original datasets" and "the number of duplicated datasets must not exceed the minimum needed to ensure continuity of the service" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 29a(1)(b) | read.
- K2.5 Device certification. Provider "complies with any requirements identified in the certification report of the specific remote qualified electronic signature creation device" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 29a(1)(c); certification by designated bodies, Art 30; recognition across Member States of devices and of the remote services, Art 24a(2) and (3) | read.
- K2.6 Device requirements. Annex II point 1(d): creation data "can be reliably protected by the legitimate signatory against use by others"; the consolidated text shows a deletion marker after point 2; remote handling is regulated in Art 29(1a) and 29a | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Annex II | read.
- K2.7 Seal certificate content. Annex III point (j): where the creation data are in a qualified seal creation device, "an appropriate indication of this, at least in a form suitable for automated processing" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Annex III(j) | read.
- K2.8 Advanced seal control. The seal is created using data that "the creator of the seal can, with a high level of confidence under its control, use" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 36(1)(c) | read.
- K2.9 Wallet and devices. EUDI Wallets support "the creation of qualified electronic signatures or electronic seals by means of qualified electronic signature or electronic seal creation devices" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 5a(5)(a)(xi) | read.
- K2.10 Standards for qualified devices. Commission Implementing Decision 2016/650 lists ISO/IEC 15408, ISO/IEC 18045 and EN 419 211 (parts 1 to 6) for devices in a user-managed environment; for devices managed by a QTSP the certification uses "security levels comparable to those required by Article 30(3)(a)" and is notified to the Commission, "until" a list of standards exists | SRC-CID-2016-650 (new) Art 1(1), 1(2), Annex | read.
- K2.11 Assurance level of wallet. "European Digital Identity Wallets shall be provided under an electronic identification scheme with assurance level high." | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 5a(11) | read.

### A3 Standards for remote signing and sealing
- K3.1 ETSI TS 119 431-1 scope. Policy and security requirements for trust service providers operating a remote QSCD or signature creation device; "The service consists of a server signing application and a QSCD / SCDev."; does not specify protocols "used to access the SSAS" | SRC-ETSI-TS-119431-1 (new) clause 1 | read.
- K3.2 It builds on EN 419 241. "The present document incorporates EN 419241-1 [3] requirements by reference."; the sole-control levels are applied to seals by replacing sole control with control: "The requirements may be applied mutatis mutandis to electronic seals." | clause 4.1 | read. EN 419 241-1/-2 themselves were not read (see D).
- K3.3 Three policies. Lightweight (LSP), Normalized (NSP) and EU SSAS v2 policy (EUSPv2); EUSPv2 "offers the same quality as that offered by the NSP" with extra requirements for eIDAS | clause 4.3.2, Annex A | read.
- K3.4 EUSPv2 key and activation rules. "Signer's signing key shall be generated in a QSCD."; the QSCD is operated in its certified configuration; the signature activation module "should be certified to be conformant to EN 419241-2"; extended identity proofing "as defined in ETSI TS 119 461"; for identity-based activation the session ends "at most 30 minutes after the end of the identity verification process" | GEN-A.4-01, SIG-A.5-02, SIG-A.5-08, LNK-A.7-01, SIG-A.5-09 | read.
- K3.5 Legal-person signer. Identity proofing for a legal person follows EN 419241-1 clause A.1.3 at level substantial or higher (LNK-6.2.2-02B); for activation by identity "an explicit action (not just a checkbox) of the natural person identified during the identity verification process" who "is allowed to sign in the name of the legal person" (SIG-6.3.1-16); for EUSPv2 the activation-data rule applies to a legal person with control in place of sole control (SIG-A.6-07A) | ETSI TS 119 431-1 | read.
- K3.6 Key life cycle. "If the public key certificate is revoked, the corresponding signing key shall be destroyed." and "The SSASP shall destroy a signing key when requested by the signer." (DEL-6.3.2-01, -02); back-up "shall not exceed the minimum needed to ensure continuity of the service" (GEN-6.3.3-04); one-time keys are "bound to exactly one signature session" (GEN-6.2.1-09) | ETSI TS 119 431-1 clause 6 | read.
- K3.7 Validity before use. "The SSASP should ensure that the public key certificate is valid before using the corresponding signing key."; valid means "not expired not revoked not suspended" | SIG-6.3.1-08 and Note 1 | read.
- K3.8 Delegated authentication. Authentication may be delegated to an external party; if the external party uses a notified eID means or an EUDI Wallet at the needed level "there is no need to demonstrate the conformance to the required level" | LNK-6.2.2-07A Note 3 | read.
- K3.9 ETSI TS 119 431-2. Scope: policy for a "Signature Creation Application Service Component" (SCASC) that supports AdES creation; "The present document does not specify any protocol used to access the SCASC"; sole control "is out of scope" and covered by TS 119 431-1 when a server signing component is used; Annex B: for a seal "the signing certificate shall identify the creator of the seal" | SRC-ETSI-TS-119431-2 (new) clause 1, Annex B, Annex C | read in part.
- K3.10 ETSI TS 119 432 scope. "The present document is limited to remote server signing, i.e. the signing key is held in a remote shared service." Remote creation "with local signing" (key on the signer's device) is a possible solution, but its protocols "are not covered in the present document" | SRC-ETSI-TS-119432 (new) clause 1 | read in part.
- K3.11 EUDI Wallet in the signature process. Clause 6.4.3: the wallet "coordinates AdES signature creation through a SCASC, that may be part of the EUDIW or of the EUDIW backend", interacting with the QTSP-operated server signing component; the request travels as OpenID4VP transaction data of type `https://cloudsignatureconsortium.org/2025/qes`; Annex A.10: "sole control is enforced per EN 419 241-1" and the activation module validates the activation data | ETSI TS 119 432 clauses 6.4.3, A.8, A.10 | read in part.
- K3.12 CSC API v2.0.0.2 scope. Covers architectures where the key is "in the cloud", held by a device managed by a service provider; architectures "where the signing key is in the hand of the signer" are not covered as a particular case; supports sole control levels SCAL1 and SCAL2 and states that for SCAL2 the activation data are "linked to the document or the documents to be signed" and "A two-factor authorization is needed" | SRC-CSC-API-V2 (new) clauses 1, 6.1, 8.2 | read in part. A later version 2.2 is referenced by TS 119 432; its text was not read.
- K3.12a CSC API on seals. Clause 8.5: seals "are created in automated processes and often a large number of documents are to be sealed in one session"; access to the API can use login and password, OAuth client credentials or mutual TLS; for credential authorisation "There are three possible strategies, to avoid human interaction for each signature" (an automatable factor such as a PIN, no additional action if the access token suffices, or an activation data object for "a high but limited number of signatures"); the authorisation "SHALL explicitly specify the total number of signatures to be authorized" for multi-signature operations (clause 9) | SRC-CSC-API-V2 (new) clauses 8.5, 9 | read in part.
- K3.13 Which interface the law names. CIR 2024/2979 Annex IV named "Cloud Signature Consortium (CSC) specification v2.0 (20 April 2023)"; CIR 2026/1731 replaces it with "ETSI TS 119 432 v1.3.1 (2026-03) clauses 6.4.3, A.6, A.7 and A.8" and changes Art 12(3)-(4) of 2979 so that signature creation applications "shall support at least the application programming interface referred to in Annex IV" | SRC-CIR-2024-2979 Annex IV; SRC-CIR-2026-1731 Art 2(7), Annex VI | read. The EBW Annex 9(3) points to "implementing acts", not to a named standard.
- K3.14 ARF on qualified signing. QES_01: every user can receive a certificate for a QSCD "that is either local, external, or remotely managed"; QES_06: at least one of three remote options (QTSP portal, wallet-channelled, relying-party-channelled); QES_15: the wallet "SHALL verify that the qualified electronic signature or seal creation device is part of a qualified service"; QES_23: wallet providers and QTSPs "SHALL comply with Sole Control Assurance Level (SCAL) 2 as defined in CEN EN 419 241-1" and QTSPs support ETSI TS 119 431-1, 119 431-2 and 119 432 | SRC-ARF-HLR Topic 16 | read.
- K3.15 ARF discussion on liability and sole control. "Although the liability on signature creation compliance is on the QTSP, the Wallet Unit shall follow applicable requirements to enable compliance of the involved QTSP." | SRC-ARF-DT-AB (new) section 4.2 (Topic AB v1.1, 16 Sep 2026) | read in part.

### A4 EUDI Wallet architecture for keys (ARF and CIR, for comparison)
- K4.1 Four WSCD architectures, all allowed. Remote WSCD (a "remote HSM"), local external (smart card or token), local internal (SIM, eSIM, embedded secure element), local native (OS-provided). "Within the EUDI Wallet ecosystem, a Wallet Provider is allowed to use any of these architectures." | SRC-ARF-MAIN (new) section 4.5.1 | read.
- K4.2 Remote WSCD. "Typically, it will be implemented by the Wallet Provider using an HSM running on a secure server."; used "if the User device lacks sufficiently secure hardware, or if the Wallet Provider does not want to have a dependency on such hardware"; the provider must ensure "only the legitimate Wallet Instance can access the remote HSM", for example by "a split-key architecture"; the access control is evaluated in certification | section 4.5.2 | read.
- K4.3 Sharing and server wallets. "One WSCD may be a part of multiple Wallet Units, e.g. in case of a remote HSM."; a Wallet Instance can be a web application, and then "the WSCD will be a remote HSM"; the ARF also states the Regulation does not exclude a Wallet Instance "installed on a non-mobile device, for example a server" | section 4.3.2 | read. WIAM_09: if a device holds assets of several units, a unit "can only access assets that are related to that Wallet Unit".
- K4.4 Provider access. WIAM_12a: the provider "SHALL NOT access the contents of a Wallet Instance"; WIAM_12c: where contents sit in a service at the provider, the provider "SHALL specify and implement strict controls to limit access"; the note adds "the Wallet Unit cannot fully prevent the Wallet Provider from accessing" them. CIR 2024/2979 Art 9(5): logs accessible to the provider only "on the basis of explicit prior consent by the wallet user" | SRC-ARF-HLR Topic 40; SRC-CIR-2024-2979 Art 9(5) | read.
- K4.5 Key rules. WIAM_08: the provider "SHALL only activate a new Wallet Unit if it has verified that the Wallet Unit includes a WSCA/WSCD that is certified" for assurance level high; WIAM_14: the device "SHALL authenticate the User at Level of Assurance High before performing any cryptographic operation"; WUA_16a: "A WSCA SHALL NOT enable export of private keys from a WSCD."; WIAM_20: protection for the whole key life, an encrypted export must be equivalent in protection; keystores (non-critical keys) get a security level from iso_18045_high down to none (WIAM_08a); user device assumed personal: "this ARF assumes that a User device is a personal device" | SRC-ARF-HLR Topics 9, 40; SRC-ARF-MAIN chapter 3 | read.
- K4.6 Unit attestation is not shown to relying parties in the EUDI Wallet. WUA_07/WUA_24: a wallet unit presents key attestation and instance attestation "only to a PID Provider or Attestation Provider" during issuance, "not to a Relying Party" | SRC-ARF-HLR Topic 9 | read. The WE BUILD blueprint repeats this for the EUDI Wallet (appendix trust ecosystem), while the WE BUILD handshake rulebook has relying parties check the unit attestation of the holder's business wallet (see K6.3).
- K4.7 Wallet unit attestation after CIR 2026/1731. A unit attestation "shall comprise one or more wallet instance attestations and one or more key attestations"; both are signed JWTs (compact JAdES); the key attestation carries 'key_storage' and 'user_authentication' with value 'iso_18045_high' "where a key attestation mentions a WSCD" and a URL for the certification; the wallet provider issues a separate key attestation for the WSCD and for each keystore; the instance attestation is fresh: its expiry "shall be less than 24 hours" after the provider's integrity check; Art 3(2) of CIR 2024/2979 (unit attestation signed under a listed certificate) is deleted and Art 6(1) is replaced | SRC-CIR-2026-1731 Art 2(1), 2(4), Annex Ib points 1, 2(a)-(c) (TR-WIA-2.1, TR_KA-2, C_KA-1) | read.
- K4.8 Original CIR 2024/2979 wording still binding on EUDI Wallets. Art 4(1): "Wallet instances shall use at least one wallet secure cryptographic device to manage critical assets."; Art 5(1)(h): the WSCA is "the only component" able to run cryptographic operations on critical assets for assurance level high; Art 6(2): attestations contain public keys whose private keys "are protected by a wallet secure cryptographic device"; recital 5: a wallet solution "may be located on the users' devices and environments and the provider's backend structure" | SRC-CIR-2024-2979 | read.
- K4.9 Qualified signing under CIR 2024/2979. Art 11(1): certificates "linked to qualified signature or seal creation devices that are either local, external, or remote in relation to the wallet instances"; Art 11(3): natural persons have free qualified signing "at least for non-professional purposes" | SRC-CIR-2024-2979 Art 11 | read.

### A5 Assurance and certification
- K5.1 Certification of EUDI Wallet. National schemes cover "the provision and operation of wallet solutions"; the WSCD or part of it "may be within the object of certification when it is provided by the certificate holder or applicant", or outside "when it is embedded in a device provided by the end user"; the WSCD "shall be assessed against the requirements of assurance level high" as a prerequisite; its evaluation includes a vulnerability assessment "at level AVA_VAN.5" unless justified; where several WSCDs are used or operations run outside the WSCD, schemes "shall include evaluation activities to ensure that the overall solution offers the expected level of security" | SRC-CIR-2024-2981 (Art 3(2)-(3), Art 8(2), Annex IV points 2(2)-(6)) | read.
- K5.2 Not suitable device. "the wallet solution shall not be issued a certificate of conformity" if verifications of the WSCD remain inconclusive and no compensating requirements for the WSCA are possible | SRC-CIR-2024-2981 Annex IV point 2(6) | read.
- K5.3 Consequence of cancelling a certificate. "The cancellation of a certificate of conformity might have severe consequences such as the revocation of all deployed wallet units." Certification bodies suspend on a confirmed breach (Art 12(1)) and cancel if not remedied (Art 12(2)-(3)) | SRC-CIR-2024-2981 recital on cancellation, Art 12 | read.
- K5.4 Risk register. Threat TR79: "An attacker can prevent suspension or revocation of a wallet." The register has further threats on remote use (identity proofing for remote qualified certificates, consent for remote signatures) | SRC-CIR-2024-2981 Annex I | read in part.
- K5.5 Key keeping by other providers. The ARF says parties such as wallet providers, PID providers and trusted list providers "will typically use a certified Hardware Security Module (HSM)"; relying parties need none | SRC-ARF-MAIN chapter 7 | read.
- K5.6 Assurance level of the EBW. See K1.6, K1.7, K1.9: Commission text sets "substantial" for critical-asset operations, user authentication and portability; Council removes or relocates these. CIR 2015/1502 itself (the technical level definitions) was not read.
- K5.7 Cryptographic mechanisms. CIR 2026/1731 adds Art 5a to CIR 2024/2979: wallet providers use "only the cryptographic mechanisms referred to in Annex Ia" (ECCG Agreed Cryptographic Mechanisms); signature algorithms for attestations: ES256, ES384, ES512 | SRC-CIR-2026-1731 Art 2(3), Annex Ia, Annex Ib SA-1 | read. Applies to EUDI Wallets; the EBW texts do not refer to it.

### A6 Suspension, withdrawal and the effect on keys
- K6.1 Breach of EUDI Wallets. Member State "shall, without undue delay, suspend the provision and the use of European Digital Identity Wallets"; if not remedied within three months it "shall withdraw European Digital Identity Wallets and revoke their validity" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 5e(1)-(2) | read. CIR 2025/847: evaluate within 24 hours whether to revoke "wallet unit attestations of the wallet units affected"; withdrawal within 72 hours after three months; revoked attestations "cannot revert to a valid state"; the user's data portability must not be hindered "without impairing the security of the critical assets" | SRC-CIR-2025-847 (new) Art 4(2)-(4), Art 8 | read.
- K6.2 Provider removed from the EBW list. Commission Art 6(2)(f): validity of the wallet can be revoked "where the provider of the European Business Wallet is not included in the list"; supervisors "revoke the inclusion" of a provider that no longer complies (Art 13(5)(k)); implementing acts may suspend a provider from the list (Art 13(12)); the provider then notifies owners and ensures "transfer or deletion" of owner data | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL | read. The texts do not say how keys held by that provider pass to another provider.

## (C) OPTIONS: what the sources enable, restrict, leave open
No recommendation. The four patterns are not mutually exclusive; a wallet may combine them (for example a provider-hosted wallet key plus a QTSP-managed qualified seal key).

### Option 1: keys on a device the owner holds (local or external WSCD, for example smart card, token, secure element)
- Enables: the ARF lists local external, internal and native WSCDs as allowed for the EUDI Wallet (K4.1); QES texts accept a "local" or "external" qualified device (K1.12, K3.14); the owner keeps physical possession, which matches control wording in K2.8.
- Restricts: the EBW Annex 3(1) places the use of a WSCA and WSCD at the back-end (K1.5) and the sources do not say how a device held only by the owner fits that requirement (the ARF mentions a split-key architecture with keys kept on the instance side in a secure element, K4.2); the ARF assumes a personal device (K4.5), and no EBW text says how several users share one device; WUA_16a excludes key export, so a lost device means new keys (K4.5); TS 119 432 does not cover protocols for the case where the key is on the signer's device (K3.10); CSC API does not cover it as a particular case (K3.12).
- Leaves open: how a device is assigned to an owner and to which users; how an owner revokes access to a physical device when an authorised user leaves; whether and how the unit attestation of an EBW with a local device is built (the Council text drops the device from the unit definition, K1.2).

### Option 2: keys in a back-end operated by the wallet provider (WSCA/WSCD as a remote HSM)
- Enables: the EBW definitions place a back-end in the unit (K1.4) and require it to use a WSCA and WSCD (K1.5); the ARF describes the remote HSM as typical when device hardware is missing, and as the architecture for web wallets and servers (K4.2, K4.3); one WSCD may serve several units with isolation (K4.3); the WE BUILD ADR assumes "a cloud or on-premise environment" (K6.3).
- Restricts: provider access limits are strict controls rather than technical impossibility (K4.4); the provider must be Union-established and not under third-country control (K1.17); under the Council text the provider evidences conformity by self-assessment and risk assessment, not certification (K1.15); keys are not exportable (K4.5), so exit means re-creating keys and re-issuing bound attestations, which no EBW text describes; qualified signatures or seals cannot be created with keys held by a provider that is not a QTSP running a qualified remote device service (K2.3).
- Leaves open: the required assurance level of the HSM (substantial in the Commission text, not stated in the Council text, K1.6-K1.7); whether a certified WSCD is required at all for the EBW (K1.15); how the owner proves control to counterparties; recovery after provider failure (K6.2).

### Option 3: keys in a hardware security module operated by the organisation itself (on-premise or own cloud account)
- Enables: the ADR names "on-premise" operation (K6.3); the ARF notes providers of other roles typically use a certified HSM (K5.5); the owner can be the entity with the strongest control over the key (K2.8).
- Restricts: the EBW provider must be a notified provider on the list, established in the Union (K1.17, K6.2), so the owner-run HSM would have to be integrated as the WSCD of a provider's solution; for qualified seals the remote operator must be a QTSP (K2.3); EN 419 241 sole-control levels apply to the QTSP service (K3.2), not to an owner-run store; Art 3(24) defines the unit attestation as describing the components of the unit or allowing their authentication and validation, and no source describes how a component run by the owner would be covered.
- Leaves open: whether the owner's HSM can be the WSCD in the sense of Art 3(29); who is liable and who evidences certification; how the provider verifies the owner-operated component for the unit attestation; the minimum evaluation level.

### Option 4: remote qualified seal or signature creation by a QTSP, orchestrated by the wallet
- Enables: eIDAS defines the remote qualified device as a trust service on behalf of the creator (K2.2-K2.5); EBW Annex 8 and 9 allow remote qualified devices and a signature creation application from a trust service provider (K1.12, K1.13); TS 119 431-1 EUSPv2 and TS 119 432 give policy and protocol (K3.3-K3.11); the ARF requires SCAL 2 for remote QES in the EUDI Wallet (K3.14); CIR 2026/1731 fixes the API reference (K3.13).
- Restricts: keys can be generated and managed only on behalf of the signatory, at the signatory's request, by the QTSP (K2.3); back-up is limited to the minimum (K2.4); the activation requires an explicit action by the identified natural person for identity-based activation (K3.5); an EBW has no certified device requirement of its own (K1.15), so the qualified path brings the evidence through the QTSP's own conformity regime; the key is destroyed when the certificate is revoked (K3.6).
- Leaves open: how a legal-person seal is activated by several authorised users in the wallet (K3.5 gives the rule for one identified natural person per activation); whether sole control levels for seals are defined where the text says control (EN 419 241-1 not read); the transfer of keys or re-issuance of certificates when changing QTSP; the relation of the QTSP's key to the wallet's own WSCD keys (key attestation of the wallet versus certificate of the QTSP).
