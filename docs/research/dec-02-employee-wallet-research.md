## (A) FACTS FROM SOURCES

### A1 Who is "user" / "owner" / "representative"
- A1.1 Owner. "an economic operator or public sector body that owns or has a right of use of a European Business Wallet" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 3(7) | read. Council: "that owns a European Business Wallet" (words "or has a right of use" dropped) | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Art 3(7) | read.
- A1.2 Economic operator includes natural persons. "any natural or legal person, or a group of such persons ... acting in a commercial or professional capacity" | Art 3(4) | read. (Sole traders can be owners; employees as such are not mentioned in the definition.)
- A1.3 EBW user. "'user' means a natural or legal person, or a natural person representing another natural person or a legal person, that uses European Business Wallets" | Art 3(22) | read. Council 9684 Art 3(22): same wording ("European Business Wallet user").
- A1.4 eIDAS "user" has the same wording. "a natural person representing another natural person or a legal person, that uses trust services or electronic identification means" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 3(5a) | read. ARF Annex 1 repeats it | eudi.dev annex-1-definitions | read (not registered in sources.yml).
- A1.5 Authorised representative (Commission). "a natural or legal person acting on behalf of the European Business Wallet owner in executing and operating functions of a designated European Business Wallet" | Art 3(18) | read. Note: may be a legal person.
- A1.6 Mandate (Commission). "the authorisation granted by a European Business Wallet owner to an authorised representative, enabling that representative to act on behalf of the owner" | Art 3(19) | read.
- A1.7 Council replaced both. Art 3(18) "deleted"; new Art 3(19): "granting or recognition of a right or permission by a European Business Wallet owner to a European Business Wallet user to perform specified actions" | ST 9684/26 | read. ST 7659/26 (22 May) still shows "authorised representative" and "mandate" definitions plus the new "authorisation" definition (markup lost in text extraction) | read.
- A1.8 Wallet unit is issued to a specific owner. "provided by a provider to a European Business Wallet to a specific European Business Wallet owner" | Art 3(25) | read. Front-end "interacts with users acting on behalf of the owner" | Art 3(42) | read.
- A1.9 Multiple wallets per owner exist. "one owner attributed with one unique identifier may have multiple wallets registered to them" | proposal, Legislative financial statement 1.3.4 fn 15 | read. The word "sub-wallet" does not occur in COM(2025) 838, ST 9684/26 or ST 7659/26 (searched).
- A1.10 EUDIW is individual. ARF: "A user who is in control of the Wallet Unit"; Wallet Unit "provided by a Wallet Provider to an individual Wallet User" | eudi.dev Annex 1 A.3 | read.

### A2 How natural persons act for a legal person (EBW side)
- A2.1 EBW purpose. "enable the creation, management and delegation of mandates to authorised representatives" | Art 3(1)(c) | read.
- A2.2 Multi-user authorisation. "authorise multiple users to access and operate the European Business Wallet of the owner" and owner can "manage and revoke such authorisations" | Art 5(1)(j) | read. Council adds "including authorisations associated to roles ... and without prejudice to any power of attorney or legal mandate" | Art 5(1)(j) ST 9684/26 | read.
- A2.3 Authentication of representatives. "allow European Business Wallet owners and their authorised representatives to authenticate themselves" | Art 5(1)(h) | read.
- A2.4 Role/mandate system, employees named. "can delegate authority to multiple users, including employees or other authorised natural or legal persons" | Recital 18 | read. Same in ST 9684/26 recital 18 ("multiple European Business Wallet users").
- A2.5 Two mandate types. "an administrative mandate, allowing the owner of a Business Wallet to assign roles and responsibilities to various users of the solution within their organisation" | Recital 18 | read. Also a "technical mandate" with "full rights to generally use the solution and act on its behalf".
- A2.6 Control and audit. "access to European Business Wallets and their functions is controlled and auditable" | Recital 18 | read.
- A2.7 Link to EU power of attorney. "compatibility with the EU digital power of attorney, as established by Directive (EU) 2025/25" | Recital 18 | read (Directive itself not read).
- A2.8 Council limits legal meaning of authorisations. "to not create, limit, or otherwise affect any power of attorney or legal mandate provided under applicable national or Union law" | ST 9684/26 recital 18 | read. Cover note para 9: "exemptions for any power of attorney and legal mandates provided under national or Union law" | read.
- A2.9 Role integrity. "mappings between roles and attributes are verifiable, auditable, revocable and traceable to their legitimate issuers" | Art 6(2)(b) 1st indent | read.
- A2.10 Segregation/over-delegation. "conflicts of roles, over-delegation, or expired authorisations are automatically detected and prevented in real time" | Art 6(2)(b) 2nd indent | read. Also "all authorisation logic is interoperable across Member States" (Council: "between European Business Wallets").
- A2.11 Council access-control Annex 12(1). Decisions based on "the formal role of the acting subjects within a recognised organisational structure or economic operator" and "the scope, validity and constraints of any mandate, delegation, or power of attorney" | ST 9684/26 Annex pt 12 | read. 12(2)(c): "all access and execution events are logged, timestamped, and bound to cryptographically verifiable proofs of authorisation, suitable for audit and legal proceedings" | read. (No equivalent text in the Commission body; the Commission Annex was not available to me.)
- A2.12 Onboarding by a representative. "securely onboard the European Business Wallet owner remotely via an authorised representative with an electronic identification means of that authorised representative" at LoA substantial or high | Art 6(1)(e) | read. Council: "via a legal representative or other persons that are lawfully empowered ..." at LoA 'high' | ST 9684/26 Art 6(1)(e) | read.
- A2.13 Wallet unit access authentication (Council Annex 1). Access only after user authenticated by "a notified electronic identification (eID) means" at least substantial, or an equivalent alternative mechanism | ST 9684/26 Annex pt 1 | read. (Commission Art 7(6)(c) refers to "point 1 of the Annex"; text not available.)
- A2.14 Representatives' rights. Providers must inform authorised representatives of "the right to request revocation of their wallet unit attestation" | Art 7(6)(c) | read. Council: "owners and their authorised European Business Wallet users" | read.
- A2.15 Qualified certificates for users (Council Annex 8(1)). "ensure that European Business Wallet users are able to receive qualified certificates for qualified electronic signatures or seals" | ST 9684/26 | read.
- A2.16 Authorisation is of a "technical nature" for the system; the legal capacity (mandate, PoA, statutory representation) stays with national/Union law (A2.8). Representation attestations are an eIDAS category: Annex VI item 9 "Powers and mandates to represent natural or legal persons" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Annex VI | read; Recital 35 EBW repeats it | read.

### A3 Can an employee use the personal EUDIW to act for the company?
- A3.1 In-force eIDAS contemplates a natural person representing a legal person. "uniquely represents the natural person, legal person or the natural person representing the natural or legal person" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 5a(5)(f) | read. Retained by EBW Art 20(2) ("the natural person or the natural person representing the natural or legal person") | read.
- A3.2 EBW proposal narrows the EUDIW mandate to natural persons. "to ensure that the mandatory issuance of European Digital Identity Wallets relates only to natural persons" | Recital 55, Art 20(1) | read. In force today: Art 5a(1) "all natural and legal persons"; Art 5a(9)(c) revocation "upon the death of the user or cease of activity of the legal person"; EBW Art 20(3) replaces it with "upon the death of the user" | read. Council keeps Art 20 unchanged | read.
- A3.3 PID can carry legal-person data. CIR 2024/2977 Annex section 2 "Set of legal person identification data": mandatory "current legal name" and a unique identifier; optional EUID, LEI, VAT, EORI | SRC-CIR-2024-2977 Annex | read. Practitioner paper: tables "do not yet contain the necessary data identifiers" (statement of 2025, now out of date with respect to 2977 text I read) | SRC-OID2025 s.1 | snippet.
- A3.4 ARF removed the legal-person wallet. "The topic of Wallet Units for legal persons ... has been removed from this ARF in view of the development of a separate business wallet" | SRC-ARF-HLR note under VCR_08; Topic 28 LP_01-03 "Empty" | read.
- A3.5 ARF representation work excludes legal persons. "not in the case of a natural person representing a legal person" | eudi.dev Discussion Topic I v0.4 (20 May 2025) s.1.1 | read (not registered). ARF Topic 29: Rulebooks "for representation attestations issued to a natural person representing another natural person" with attributes limiting "the operations the representative is authorised to perform" (RP_01); issuer must ensure "the attestations are short-lived or that all entities which ... must have the ability to request revocation ... are able to do so" (RP_02) | SRC-ARF-HLR | read.
- A3.6 Attestation issuance requires a right-to-act check. Issuers shall "verify that the requester of the attestation has the right to act on behalf of the person that is the subject of the attestation" | SRC-CIR-2025-1569 Annex II pt 2(a) | read.
- A3.7 Who can revoke what. PID/attestation in a wallet: "Only providers of person identification data or electronic attestation of attributes can revoke" | SRC-CIR-2024-2977 Art 5(2); qualified/PuB attestations: issuers "the only entities able to revoke" | SRC-CIR-2025-1569 Art 4(2); wallet unit: "Wallet providers shall be the only entities capable of revoking wallet unit attestations" | SRC-CIR-2024-2979 Art 7(1) | all read. Revocation triggers include "explicit request of the wallet user" (2977 Art 5(4)(a)) and, for attestations, request of the holder or "where applicable, of the subject of the attestation" (1569 Art 4(3)(a)) | read. No trigger for "employer request" or "employment ended".
- A3.8 User control. "Users shall have full control of the use of and of the data in their European Digital Identity Wallet" | Art 5a(14) | read; "under the sole control of the user" | Art 5a(4)(a) | read; "The use of European Digital Identity Wallets shall be voluntary" | Art 5a(15) | read.
- A3.9 Free signing for natural persons. EUDIW offers "all natural persons the ability to sign by means of qualified electronic signatures by default and free of charge"; Member States may limit this to "non-professional purposes" | Art 5a(5)(g) + subpara | read. ARF QES_02: "at least for non-professional purposes, free-of-charge access" | SRC-ARF-HLR | read. Issuance/use/revocation of EUDIW "free of charge to all natural persons" | Art 5a(13) | read.
- A3.10 Wallet assurance. EUDIW "provided under an electronic identification scheme with assurance level high" | Art 5a(11) | read.
- A3.11 WE BUILD employee pattern (draft). Employee Credential "the organization issues an attestation about its own employee into the employee's Natural Person Wallet"; classified EAA within EBW ecosystem; employee must be "active employee of the issuing organization at the time of issuance"; issuer "must immediately revoke the attestation if any change occurs" | SRC-WEBUILD-RB rb-employee v0.1 (23.06.2026) | read. AuthorisedSignatories: "any natural person who is authorised to act on behalf of a legal entity"; sources "Statutory Power" (register) or "Delegated Power" (PoA attestation) | rb-authorised-signatories v1.0 | read. EU PoA: principal is "natural person acting on behalf of a legal entity" | rb-eu-poa v0.1 | read.
- A3.12 EBWOID relation to PID. "the RP SHOULD request and verify a PID for the representative in addition to the EBWOID" (where binding to a natural-person representative is needed) | rb-ebwoid, Relying Party obligations | read. ADR on LPID: "WE BUILD will use the EBWOID rulebook as the basis for identification of business wallet owners and deprecate LPID" | SRC-WEBUILD-ARCH adr/001 | read. ADR basic-lpid: "Rely on basic EBWOID everywhere as a minimum identity attestation" | read. EBWOID creation: "A natural person requests and obtains the EBWOID on behalf of the Economic Operator, which is then bound to the Wallet Unit" | adr/wallet-unit-lifecycle-management (Proposed, 11 Feb 2026) | read. Advice noted in ADR basic-lpid (Ronald Koenig, Spherity, 2025-11-18): "Only acceptable if it does not preclude the use of more comprehensive 'identity attestations' (EUCC, KYC, etc.)" | read.
- A3.13 Company wallet holding employee-related attestations (WE BUILD). "holder company has issued Contact Person attestations for all its employees into its Company Wallet"; "The Holder is responsible for the lifecycle of the attestation content, including revocation and role changes" | rb-contact-person | read.
- A3.14 Interaction EBW to EUDIW. EBW shall "request and share ... between European Business Wallets and European Digital Identity Wallets" (Art 5(1)(c)) and "issue electronic attestations of attributes to European Business Wallets and European Digital Identity Wallets" (Art 5(1)(f)) | read. Sole traders: "may also rely on their European Digital Identity Wallets to access trust services offered for the European Business Wallets" | Explanatory memorandum, proportionality | read; QERDS standalone for EUDIW users Art 5(3) | read.

### A4 Signatures and seals
- A4.1 Signatory = natural person; seal creator = legal person. "'signatory' means a natural person who creates an electronic signature" Art 3(9); "'creator of a seal' means a legal person who creates an electronic seal" Art 3(24) | read.
- A4.2 Legal effect. "A qualified electronic signature shall have the equivalent legal effect of a handwritten signature" Art 25(2); "A qualified electronic seal shall enjoy the presumption of integrity of the data and of correctness of the origin" Art 35(2) | read.
- A4.3 EBW core function. "sign by means of qualified electronic signatures and seal by means of qualified electronic seals, as applicable" | Art 5(1)(d) | read. EUDIW has the same dual wording | Art 5a(4)(e) | read.
- A4.4 Individuals acting for an entity sign. "individuals granted the power to act on behalf of an entity ... to exercise their functions by signing" | Recital 17 | read. Council recital 17 specifies "qualified electronic signatures shall have the equivalent legal effect of a handwritten signature".
- A4.5 Seal certificate content for legal persons: "for legal persons: a unique set of data unambiguously representing the legal person" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Annex III(ca) | read.
- A4.6 Remote QSCD custody. "managed only on behalf of ... the signatory by a qualified provider" (candidate NFR-008) | Art 29(1a) | snippet. Council Annex 8/9: qualified signature or seal creation devices "local, external, or remotely managed"; signature creation applications "may either be integrated into or be external to" the back-end | read.

### A5 Exit, revocation, continuity
- A5.1 Wallet revocation grounds (owner level). "upon the explicit request of the owner"; "where the security ... has been compromised"; "upon the permanent or temporary cessation of activity of the European Business Wallet owner"; provider off the list | Art 6(2)(f) | read. No ground relating to an individual user's departure.
- A5.2 Revoking a user's authorisation is an owner function (A2.2). Wallet unit attestation revocation by the wallet provider; representatives may "request revocation of their wallet unit attestation" (Art 7(6)(c)).
- A5.3 Council Annex 6: provider informs affected users within 24 hours of revoking a unit attestation, with reason | ST 9684/26 | read. (EUDIW equivalent: 2979 Art 7(3), candidate OPS-009.)
- A5.4 Export. "export their data ... in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format, at the request of the owner or in the event of termination of service or revocation of the notification of the provider" | Art 5(1)(l) | read. Council: only "in the event of termination of service..." and new (la) "import their data ... to benefit from data portability across ... providers" | read; Annex pt 10 "secure export, import and portability of an owner's ... data" | read.
- A5.5 Provider exit. "ensure the transfer or deletion of the European Business Wallet owner data in accordance with the ... owners instructions" | Art 7(6)(f) | read.
- A5.6 EUDIW portability (natural person): candidate FUN-031 / CIR 2024/2979 Art 13 | snippet.
- A5.7 No source addresses transfer of an individual's authorisations or keys to a successor when staff change; Council 12(3)(b) "expired authorisations are automatically detected" is the closest statement.

### A6 Liability and accountability
- A6.1 Equivalence. "the resulting action shall have the same legal effect as if the action had been lawfully carried out in person, in paper form" | Art 4 | read (the action is that of the "owner"). Council recital 6 narrows equivalence; "national administrative and procedural requirements still apply" (cover note para 8) | read.
- A6.2 TSP liability. "trust service providers shall be liable for damage caused intentionally or negligently to any natural or legal person" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 13(1); presumption of negligence for qualified TSPs | read.
- A6.3 No provision in COM(2025) 838 or in ST 9684/26 (searched "liab") allocates liability between owner, employee and provider for acts done through an EBW. Recital 18: system should "preserve the integrity of the identity of the owner" | read.
- A6.4 Authorisation logic must be "traceable to their legitimate issuers" (Art 6(2)(b)) and logs "suitable for audit and legal proceedings" (Council Annex 12(2)(c)).

## (C) DECISION CRITERIA (business perspective, neutral)
Flag: S = stated in sources (reference); D = architecture driver, not stated in sources. "Partial" = the source states a related provision but not the business need itself.
1. Organisational control and revocation when an employee leaves. S (partial): owner manages and revokes user authorisations (Art 5(1)(j), Art 6(2)(b)); owner-level wallet revocation grounds (Art 6(2)(f)). D: the employee-exit event and speed requirement; no source names it.
2. Continuity of authority when staff change (handover of roles, pending actions, keys, mailbox/QERDS address). D. Nearest: "expired authorisations ... detected" (Council Annex 12(3)(b)); one unique digital address per owner (Art 6(1)(j)).
3. Separation of private and professional use. D. Related: professional-purpose limit of free QES (Art 5a(5)(g)); user "full control" of EUDIW (Art 5a(14)).
4. Accountability (who acted, on whose authority). S: equivalence for owner's actions (Art 4); "logged ... bound to cryptographically verifiable proofs of authorisation" (Council Annex 12(2)(c)); logs (Art 5(1)(m)).
5. Liability allocation (company, employee, provider). D. Only TSP liability is stated (SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 13); nothing on owner/employee.
6. Delegation chains (re-delegation, mandates from mandates). S: "delegation of mandates" (Art 3(1)(c)); "over-delegation ... prevented" (Art 6(2)(b)); constraints of "any mandate, delegation, or power of attorney" (Council Annex 12(1)(c)). Depth limits: D.

## (D) OPTIONS: what the sources enable, restrict, leave open (no recommendation)

### Option (a) Employee uses personal EUDIW plus mandate/representation attestations
Enables
- eIDAS already defines user and PID to cover "a natural person representing ... a legal person" (Art 3(5a), 3(3), 5a(5)(f)); PID Annex has legal-person data tables (CIR 2024/2977); Annex VI lists "powers and mandates" as verifiable attributes (A3.1, A3.3, A2.16).
- Attestation issuers must check right to act (CIR 2025/1569 Annex II 2(a)); employer can be issuer of an Employee/PoA/AuthorisedSignatories EAA that is revocable by the employer as issuer (WE BUILD rulebooks A3.11).
- EUDIW already has QES for natural persons by default (Art 5a(5)(g)), assurance level high (5a(11)), free for natural persons (5a(13)); EBW interacts with EUDIW (Art 5(1)(c),(f)).
- Sole traders may rely on EUDIW for QERDS and signatures without a full EBW (Explanatory memorandum; Art 4 2nd subpara; Art 5(3)).
Restricts
- Wallet and PID are under the user's "full control" and "sole control"; only wallet provider, PID provider or user can revoke wallet unit/PID (A3.7, A3.8). No employer-initiated revocation path in the sources.
- Free QES may be limited to non-professional purposes by Member States (Art 5a(5)(g)); ARF QES_02 phrases it the same.
- ARF has no legal-person wallet (Topic 28 "Empty"), and its representation work excludes natural-person-for-legal-person (A3.4, A3.5); so no ARF requirement describes this pattern.
- Public-sector acceptance duty (Art 16) is defined by EBW core functionalities for economic operators; nothing equates a personal EUDIW with it. Seals need a legal-person creator (Art 3(24)); a personal wallet cannot seal for the company on its own.
- Employee usage logs are user-accessible and provider access needs user consent (CIR 2024/2979 Art 9(5)); no employer access right stated.
Leaves open
- Whether professional use of a personal EUDIW is permitted/limited beyond QES; who is GDPR controller for employee wallet data; how an employer proves the employee's authority is revoked to relying parties in real time (status check by RP vs short-lived attestations, RP_02); legal weight of non-qualified employer-issued EAA vs QEAA/PuB-EAA mandate; relation to the Directive 2025/25 digital EU power of attorney (not read); behaviour when employee leaves (attestation status vs PID unchanged).

### Option (b) Wallet or sub-wallet under the organisation's EBW
Enables
- Owner is the legal person (A1.1); multi-user authorisation with roles and revocation by the owner is a core function (Art 5(1)(j)); employees are explicitly named (Recital 18, A2.4); logs, audit, segregation-of-duties detection (A2.9, A2.10, A2.11).
- Core functions with acceptance duty: identify/authenticate, sign/seal, submit, notify (Art 5, Art 16); QES and seals via qualified certificates for users (Council Annex 8); equivalence of legal effect (Art 4).
- Owner-level exit: export, transfer/deletion, import (A5.4, A5.5); wallet revocation on cessation of activity (Art 6(2)(f)).
Restricts
- Proposal not in force; Commission and Council texts differ (mandate/authorised representative deleted; "authorisation" is technical and does not create legal powers; onboarding LoA 'high'; Annex only in Council text). Application at earliest one year after entry into force.
- A user must still authenticate with an eID (substantial or equivalent, Council Annex 1; representative eID at substantial/high or high at onboarding), i.e. the employee still needs a personal means (A2.12, A2.13).
- No "sub-wallet" concept; one owner can have multiple wallets (A1.9) but employee-level wallet units are not described. A unit is provided to "a specific owner" (Art 3(25)).
- Authorisations cannot replace statutory/PoA authority (Council recital 18, A2.8). Provider must be notified/listed and EU-controlled (Art 7(1),(2)). EBW logs readable by provider "where necessary" (Council Annex 7(5)).
- Companies are not obliged to adopt; ex-ante SME burden and provider pricing not stated.
Leaves open
- Whether signatures by employees under an EBW use the employee's personal qualified certificate or a seal of the company (Art 5(1)(d) "as applicable"); how individual attribution is evidenced in the log; custody/escrow of keys at provider (back-end HSM, remote QSCD); employee-exit effects on personal certificates; who the GDPR controller is for user data in the wallet; implementing acts for role formats and delegation interoperability (Council Annex 12(4)); HR/IAM connectors; Commission Annex text.

### Option (c) Hybrid (EBW for organisational acts and authority, personal EUDIW for the person)
Enables
- The sources already couple the two: EBW exchanges and issues attestations to EUDIW (Art 5(1)(c),(f)); onboarding of the EBW uses a representative's eID (Art 6(1)(e)); EUDIW PID "natural person representing" a legal person (Art 5a(5)(f), retained by Art 20(2)); EBWOID rulebook expects PID of the representative alongside EBWOID (A3.12).
- WE BUILD rulebooks show both patterns: employee attestation into the Natural Person Wallet; contact-person attestations in the Company Wallet; authorised-signatory list as EAA signed by the company (A3.11, A3.13).
- Separation of concerns is possible: owner-controlled authorisation inside EBW (revocation by owner), person-bound identity (PID, personal QES) in the EUDIW.
Restricts
- Two revocation regimes with different controllers: EBW authorisations (owner) vs attestations in personal wallet (issuer) vs PID/wallet (user, provider). No source defines linkage or propagation (cf. ARF revocation chaining only for wallet-unit status).
- Duplicated acceptance logic for relying parties: EBWOID alone, EBWOID + PID, or attestation in personal wallet. Interim period where eIDAS applies without EBW (ADR 001 risk, A9.2).
- Legal-person identity within ARF is out of scope (A3.4); LPID replaced by EBWOID within WE BUILD (A3.12).
Leaves open
- Which actions must be done under the EBW identity and which under the person; how employee identity is disclosed in B2G submissions (privacy vs accountability); who issues mandate attestations (company, QTSP, register-based PuB-EAA); what the public sector accepts (Art 16 is silent on PID + mandate); data protection roles across two wallets; cost per employee for two wallet types; recognition of Directive 2025/25 power of attorney.
