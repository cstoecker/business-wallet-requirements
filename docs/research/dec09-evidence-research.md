# RES-dec09-evidence: evidence, logging and audit (K10)

Scope: facts from primary sources only; no recommendation. Date of work: 2026-10-03. Decision question (DEC-09): what is logged, by whom, with which integrity and access rules, so that actions are attributable and auditable without exposing employees unnecessarily?

## (A) FACTS FROM SOURCES

### A1 EBW proposal and Council text
- A1.1 Core function. Providers shall enable owners to "access a log of all transactions" and to export "communication logs, and interaction records" in a structured machine-readable format | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 5(1)(m), 5(1)(l) | read.
- A1.2 Control and audit. Access to wallets and functions is "controlled and auditable" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL recital 18 | read. Authorisation logic: role-attribute mappings "are verifiable, auditable, revocable and traceable to their legitimate issuers" | Art 6(2)(b) | read.
- A1.3 Equivalence. The owner's action "shall have the same legal effect as if the action had been lawfully carried out in person, in paper form" | Art 4 | read; this gives records evidential weight in dealings but is not an evidence rule.
- A1.4 Council logging policy. Providers "shall provide an appropriate logging policy that shall include, at a minimum, electronic signing, electronic sealing, and notifications of all transactions" with relying parties, other business wallet units and EUDI Wallet units, "irrespective of whether the transaction is successfully completed" | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex 7(1) | read.
- A1.5 Council log content. Time and date; relying party name, contact details and unique identifier and Member State; type or types of data requested and presented; reason for non-completion | Annex 7(2) | read. No field for the acting user or the authorisation used appears in 7(2).
- A1.6 Integrity and access. "ensure integrity, authenticity, availability and confidentiality of the logged information"; the back-end "shall log reports sent by the European Business Wallet user to the competent authorities"; logs are accessible to the provider "where it is necessary for the provision of European Business Wallets services"; they "shall remain accessible for as long as required to be accessible by Union law or national law" | Annex 7(3) to 7(6) | read.
- A1.7 Access events. "all access and execution events are logged, timestamped, and bound to cryptographically verifiable proofs of authorisation, suitable for audit and legal proceedings" | Annex 12(2)(c) | read. Implementing acts shall cover "requirements for secure logging, timestamping and auditability of authorisation events" | Annex 12(4)(d) | read.
- A1.8 Termination. Owner data is transferred or deleted on instruction when the provider ends service; export covers "communication logs, and interaction records" (Art 5(1)(l), 7(6)(f)) | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL | read.
- A1.9 Supervision. Providers shall "respond without undue delay to any request for information or documentation necessary to verify compliance" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 7(6)(d) | read. Audit powers of supervisory bodies beyond this were not analysed.

### A2 eIDAS: duties of (qualified) trust service providers and legal effect
- A2.1 Record keeping duty. A qualified trust service provider shall "record and keep accessible for as long as necessary after the activities of the qualified trust service provider have ceased" all relevant information concerning data issued and received, for evidence in legal proceedings and continuity | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 24(2)(h) | read.
- A2.2 Breach notification. Security breaches or disruptions with significant impact are notified "without undue delay and in any event within 24 hours of the incident" | Art 24(2)(fb) | read.
- A2.3 Trustworthy storage. Qualified providers "use trustworthy systems to store data provided to it, in a verifiable form" so that only authorised persons can make entries and changes and "the data can be checked for authenticity" | Art 24(2)(f) | read.
- A2.4 Time stamps. A qualified time stamp "is based on an accurate time source linked to Coordinated Universal Time"; it carries a presumption of accuracy of date and time and integrity | Art 42(1)(b), 41(2) | read.
- A2.5 Registered delivery evidence. Data sent through a qualified service enjoys the presumption of integrity, sending by the identified sender, receipt by the identified addressee and accuracy of date and time; dates and times of sending, receiving and any change "are indicated by a qualified electronic time stamp" | Art 43(2), 44(1)(f) | read.
- A2.6 Preservation. A qualified preservation service uses "procedures and technologies capable of extending the trustworthiness of the qualified electronic signature beyond the technological validity period" | Art 34(1); applies to seals under Art 40 | read.
- A2.7 EUDI Wallet dashboard. The wallet enables access to "a log of all transactions carried out through the European Digital Identity Wallet" | Art 5a(4)(d) | read.

### A3 Implementing regulations
- A3.1 Wallet logs (EUDI Wallet). Wallet instances "shall log all transactions with wallet-relying parties and other wallet units, including electronic signing and sealing" with four content fields; provider ensures "integrity, authenticity and confidentiality of the logged information"; logs accessible to the provider "on the basis of explicit prior consent by the wallet user"; kept "for as long as they are required by Union law or national law"; user can export | SRC-CIR-2024-2979 Art 9 | read. Article 9 is not amended by CIR 2026/1731 (searched).
- A3.2 ARF dashboard logs. "The Wallet Unit SHALL log all transactions executed through the Wallet Unit, including any transactions that were not completed successfully." | SRC-ARF-HLR Topic 19 | read.
- A3.3 QTSP termination plan. Providers keep a plan "including how information is kept accessible in accordance with Article 24(2), point (h)"; the plan secures "the accessibility and usability of all relevant records held by the qualified trust service provider"; review "at least every two years" | SRC-CIR-2025-2530 (new) Art 3(1), 3(4), 3(7)(e) | read. Records must allow evidence of compliance with Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 and Regulation (EU) 2016/679 and continued verification of earlier outputs | Art 3(8) | read. Where the Annex standards and Articles 1 to 3 differ, "the requirements set out in Article 1, Article 2 and Article 3 of this Regulation shall prevail" | Art 4(2) | read.
- A3.4 Registered delivery evidence. "The ERDS shall generate and make available to legitimate interested parties ERDS evidence about ERD events" and "The ERDSP shall archive the evidence and/or evidence digests for each evidence that it issued." | SRC-CIR-2025-1944 Annex I, REQ-ERDS-5.4.1-06, -07 | read. Evidence semantics follow EN 319 522-2 clause 8 (REQ-ERDS-5.4.1-08).
- A3.5 Preservation. Qualified preservation services ensure "long-term integrity, authenticity, proof of existence and accessibility of preservation evidence"; time stamps in preservation evidence "shall be qualified time-stamps"; the Annex applies ETSI TS 119 511 V1.1.1 and TS 119 172-4 with adaptations | SRC-CIR-2025-1946 (new) recital 1 and Annex OVR-A-03 | read.
- A3.6 NIS2 implementing rules for trust service providers. The Annex requires procedures and tools "to monitor and log activities", logs maintained, documented and reviewed, a list of assets to be logged, logs maintained and backed up "for a predefined period" and protected "from unauthorised access or changes", synchronised time sources, redundant logging systems | SRC-CIR-2024-2690 (new) Annex 3.2.1 to 3.2.7 | read. Suggested log content includes creation or modification of users, access to systems, authentication events, privileged activity (3.2.3).
- A3.7 Significant incidents for trust services. Significant if a trust service is unavailable "for more than 20 minutes" or integrity, confidentiality or authenticity is compromised for "more than 0,1 % of users or relying parties" (or more than 100) | SRC-CIR-2024-2690 Art 14 | read.

### A4 Standards: records and retention
- A4.1 EN 319 401 V3.2.1 clause 7.10. "The TSP shall record and keep accessible for an appropriate period of time" all relevant information on data issued and received, "including after the activities of the TSP have ceased" (REQ-7.10-01); "The confidentiality and integrity of current and archived records concerning operation of services shall be maintained." (-02); records are "completely and confidentially archived" per disclosed practices (-03); "The time used to record events as required in the audit log shall be synchronized with UTC at least once a day." (-06); retention period is "as notified in the TSP's terms and conditions" (-07); "The events shall be logged in a way that they cannot be easily deleted or destroyed" (-08) | SRC-ETSI-EN-319401-V3.2.1 (new) 7.10 | read.
- A4.2 EN 319 401 V3.2.1 clause 7.9.1. "The TSP shall maintain, document and regularly review logs" including network traffic, user administration, authentication events, privileged activity, access to critical configuration; "shall maintain and back up logs for a predefined period and shall protect them from unauthorised access or changes" | 7.9.1 REQ-7.9.1-04, -14 | read.
- A4.3 Mapping annexes. Annex C maps clauses of EN 319 401 to CIR 2024/2690 (for example "3.2 - Monitoring and logging" to REQ-7.9.1-01 to -18); Annex A maps to DORA Article 30 (for example REQ-7.10-01 to Article 30(2)(c)) | EN 319 401 V3.2.1 Annexes A and C | read.
- A4.4 EN 319 411-1 V1.5.1 (certificates). "All security events shall be logged"; "The TSP shall log all requests and reports relating to revocation, as well as the resulting action."; the retention period must be documented precisely in the practice statement; "The TSP shall retain the following for at least seven years after any certificate based on these records ceases to be valid", namely the CA key life-cycle event log and the registration documentation | SRC-ETSI-EN-319411-1 OVR-6.4.5-02, REV-6.4.5-09, OVR-6.4.5-11, OVR-6.4.6-01 | read. A note in the same clause says ETSI TS 119 511 suggests provisions on how to preserve digital data objects. REG-6.4.5-05: "The TSP shall maintain the privacy of subject information."
- A4.5 EN 319 522-1 evidence. An ERDS evidence is "an attestation provided by an ERDS that a specific event related to the process of transferring some specific data ... happened at a certain time"; it "can be used to prove to third parties, if needed also in legal proceedings"; an evidence repository "grants the persistence of ERDS evidence for a period of time which depends on the specific policies of the service" | SRC-ETSI-EN-319522-1 (new) 4.1, 4.2.1 | read.
- A4.6 TS 119 511 V1.2.1 scope. Policy and security requirements for providers of long-term preservation "of digital signatures and of general data"; "The present document covers different strategies for the preservation service" (storage, no storage, temporary storage) | SRC-ETSI-119511 (new) clause 1 | partly-read. TS 119 512 V1.2.1 specifies protocols between client and preservation service, with XML and JSON data objects and SOAP and REST bindings | SRC-ETSI-119512 (new) clause 1 | partly-read.

### A5 Horizontal law: AI Act, NIS2, DORA, GDPR
- A5.1 AI Act. High-risk AI systems "shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system"; providers keep logs under their control "for a period appropriate to the intended purpose" of "at least six months, unless provided otherwise" in other Union or national law, "in particular in Union law on the protection of personal data" | SRC-AIACT Art 12(1), 19(1) | read. Documentation kept "for a period ending 10 years after the high-risk AI system has been placed on the market" | Art 18(1) | read. Deployers keep logs under their control for at least six months | Art 26(6) | read.
- A5.2 NIS2. Article 21(2) lists ten minimum measures (policies on risk analysis, incident handling, business continuity, supply chain, secure development, effectiveness assessment, hygiene, cryptography, HR security and access control, multi-factor authentication); logging is not named; trust service providers fall under the implementing rules of Article 21(5) (A3.6) | SRC-NIS2 Art 21(2), 21(5) | read. Significant incidents: early warning "within 24 hours", and for trust service providers notification "within 24 hours of becoming aware" | Art 23(4) | read.
- A5.3 DORA contract terms. Contracts with ICT third-party providers include "provisions on ensuring access, recovery and return in an easily accessible format of personal and non-personal data" on termination and, for critical or important functions, "unrestricted rights of access, inspection and audit" | SRC-DORA Art 30(2)(d), 30(3)(e)(i) | read.
- A5.4 DORA RTS 2024/1774 logging. Financial entities "develop, document, and implement logging procedures, protocols and tools" covering access control and identity management, capacity, change, operations and network traffic, protection of logs against tampering and unauthorised access, detection of failure of logging systems, and clock synchronisation; "financial entities shall establish the retention period" from business and security objectives, reason for the log and the risk assessment | SRC-DORA-RTS-1774 Art 12 | read. Evidence of ICT incidents is retained "no longer than necessary" and securely | Art 22 | read.
- A5.5 GDPR. Identifiable data kept "for no longer than is necessary" (Art 5(1)(e)); record of processing includes envisaged time limits for erasure "where possible" (Art 30(1)(f)); security includes the ability to ensure ongoing "confidentiality, integrity, availability and resilience" (Art 32(1)(b)) | SRC-GDPR | read. Roles for logs are discussed in RES-dec08.
- A5.6 AI Act and employees. Employer-deployers inform "workers' representatives and the affected workers" before using a high-risk system at the workplace | SRC-AIACT Art 26(7) | read.

## (C) OPTIONS: what the sources enable, restrict, leave open (no recommendation)

### Option (a) Provider-operated log under trust-service-provider style controls
The provider keeps a tamper-resistant transaction and access log under policies modelled on EN 319 401 and the NIS2 rules for trust service providers, and gives owners access and export.
- Enables: direct fit to Council Annex 7 and 12 and to CIR 2024/2979 Art 9 (A1.4 to A1.7, A3.1); established controls for integrity, time and archiving (A4.1, A4.2); the same controls are already assessed for qualified providers (A3.3, A4.3); export and termination duties are already stated (A1.8).
- Restricts: the owner depends on the provider for availability and for evidence after exit unless export is complete and verifiable; provider access to logs is limited to necessity (Council) or consent (EUDI Wallet), which constrains analytics; retention periods must be set by the provider and stated in terms (A4.1).
- Leaves open: whether the provider's copy or the owner's export is evidence; how user identity is represented; how long logs are kept after termination (A8.1, A8.3).

### Option (b) Owner-held evidence store with provider-signed receipts
The provider issues signed, time-stamped receipts per event; the full record sits with the owner, who is responsible for retention and access.
- Enables: owner control and data minimisation at the provider (A6.2, A5.5); qualified time stamps and registered delivery evidence already exist as signed evidence (A2.5, A3.4); portability (A1.1).
- Restricts: the owner (including small firms) must run archiving and long-term preservation (TS 119 511/512, qualified preservation services, A3.5, A4.6); provider-side duties for logs of its own operation remain (A3.6, A4.2); breach investigation by the provider may be harder without full logs.
- Leaves open: how receipts are verified after the provider ends service; who is liable for lost owner archives; how supervisors access owner-held logs.

### Option (c) Provider log plus external transparency or preservation layer
Option (a) plus an external, independently verifiable layer for selected events: qualified time stamps, qualified preservation, or a transparency log with receipts (RFC 9162 style or SCITT style).
- Enables: third-party verifiability and detection of log tampering or equivocation (A7.1, A7.2); legal presumptions where qualified time stamps or preservation are used (A2.4, A2.6); long-term proof of existence of signed records (A3.5).
- Restricts: neither transparency specification is mandated by law or tied to the EBW (A7.3); SCITT's published scope is software supply chains (A7.2); additional publication of events can conflict with minimisation and confidentiality unless only digests are published; cost per event for qualified services.
- Leaves open: which events are anchored; whether anchoring is by the provider or the owner; how deletion requests under data protection law interact with append-only structures (not addressed in any source read).
