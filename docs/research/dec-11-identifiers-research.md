## A. Facts

### A1 EBW proposal (SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL, read)
- A1.1 Owner identification data (EBWOID) is issued to the owner wallet. "Providers of European Business Wallet owner identification data shall issue European Business Wallet owner identification data" | Art 8(1) | read.
- A1.2 Form. Format of one of the standards in Annex II of CIR 2024/2979, as QEAA (QTSP), attestation by or for a public sector body for an authentic source, or Commission attestation | Art 8(3)(a)-(c) | read.
- A1.3 Minimum content. "the official name of the economic operator or public sector body" and "the relevant unique identifier attributed in accordance with Article 9" | Art 8(5)(a),(b) | read. (The annex table of the data-flow section cites this as Art 8(6); the article body numbers it 8(5).)
- A1.4 Authentic sources. "Member States shall notify to the Commission the relevant authentic sources"; the Commission publishes a machine-readable list | Art 8(2) | read.
- A1.5 Association with wallet. "ensure that the European Business Wallet owner identification data is digitally associated with the European Business Wallet of the owner" | Art 6(2)(a) | read.
- A1.6 Primary identifier is the EUID where it exists. "that identifier shall be used as the unique identifier" | Art 9(1) | read. Otherwise "a unique identifier shall be created" under an implementing act | Art 9(2) | read. Union entities: Commission creates it | Art 9(3). No owner gets more than one | Art 9(4) | read.
- A1.7 Scope of EUID use. Recital 36: the framework "should rely on the issuance and recording process of European Unique Identifiers" for operators under Directive 2017/1132 and under Directive (EU) 2015/849 (AML). Recital 37: "it is also necessary to assign a unique identifier to other economic operators and public sector bodies" | read.
- A1.8 EBWOID attestation scheme. "The Commission shall establish and maintain an attestation scheme" listed in the CIR 2025/1569 catalogue | Art 8(6) | read.
- A1.9 Digital Directory. Commission-run, "trusted source of information for European Business Wallet owners", API plus portal; accessible to owners, their representatives and providers; changes communicated "within one working day" | Art 10(1),(4),(5) | read. Providers send the categories of information at wallet provision; kept up to date and "corroborated using the providers of the European Business Wallet owner identification data" | Art 7(g), 10(2) | read. Data-flow table: "Information on national registries holding information on economic operators" Art 13(5)(f), format "Not specified" | read.
- A1.10 Core functions that touch identification: selective disclosure of EBWOID and attributes (Art 5(1)(b)); share with "European Business Wallets and European Digital Identity Wallets and with European Business Wallet-relying parties" (5(1)(c)); issue attestations to EBW and EUDIW (5(1)(f)); authenticate with "qualified and non-qualified electronic attestations of attributes" (5(1)(h)); authorise relying parties to request attestations (5(1)(k)); QERDS (5(1)(i)) | read.
- A1.11 Protocols. Wallets support common protocols for "issuance of European Business Wallet owner identification data, qualified and non-qualified electronic attestations of attributes" and for relying parties to request and validate them | Art 6(1)(a),(b) | read. Interaction between EBWs and EBW to EUDIW | Art 6(1)(f) | read.
- A1.12 Assurance. Remote onboarding via representative with eID means "assurance levels 'substantial' or 'high'" | Art 6(1)(e); critical-asset operations at level substantial per CIR 2015/1502 | Art 6(1)(l) | read. Creation of owner identification data is not tied to a LoA in Arts 8-9; no LoA is stated for relying party reliance on EBWOID (searched).
- A1.13 Relying party. "a natural person, an economic operator or public sector body that relies upon European Business Wallets" | Art 3(23) | read. Owner is operator or public body; operator includes natural persons | Art 3(4) (see RES-employee).
- A1.14 Third-country wallets/frameworks. Commission "may adopt implementing acts" that business wallets or similar systems from third countries "offering assurances that are equivalent"; conditions: interoperable with Regulation 910/2014 trust framework, support at least identification, authentication and exchange of attestations | Art 17(1),(2) | read. Prior assessment (17(3)); repeal/suspend (17(4)); public list (17(5)) | read.
- A1.15 Third-country operators as owners. Providers "may provide European Business Wallets to economic operators established in a third country" on condition they have EBWOID and unique identifier | Art 18(1); one set only (18(2)); provider notifies the supervisory body of the request (18(3)); provider requests EBWOID on their behalf (18(4)); issuer may issue if identity proofing fulfils "one or, when needed, a combination, of the methods" of eIDAS Art 24(1a) and no prior set (18(5)); Member State cooperation to check duplicates (18(6)); supervisor cooperates with Art 46b authorities (13(5)(l)) | read.
- A1.16 Recitals. 52: operators outside the Union may get a wallet "provided that their identity can be verified with a high level of certainty"; 53: implementing act for the identifier "should encompass the conditions for their issuance to third country economic operators"; 54: third-country recognition "to support and promote partnerships" | read. Chapter IV summary: "facilitate trusted global exchanges with non-EU partners" | Explanatory memorandum | read.
- A1.17 Third-country identifiers other than EBWOID: not mentioned (LEI, EORI, GLN, KERI do not occur in the proposal text; searched) | read.
- A1.18 Interplay with non-wallet parties. QERDS "as a standalone service to users of European Digital Identity Wallets" | Art 5(3); public bodies may keep alternative solutions with a gateway for 36 months | recital 48, Art 16 | read. Nothing states a fallback for an arbitrary counterparty with neither wallet nor QERDS.
- A1.19 Council text (ST 9684/26) was not re-read for this note; see RES-employee for its Art 3(18-19) and Art 6(1)(e) changes (onboarding at LoA "high"). Outcome of 9 June 2026 Council meeting unread.

### A3 Company law (BRIS, EUID, EUCC) [N]
- A3.1 EUID base. Directive 2017/1132 Art 16(1): companies "have a unique identifier allowing them to be unequivocally identified in communications between registers"; elements "Member State of the register, the domestic register of origin and the company number" | SRC-DIR-2017-1132 | read (Arts 16, 22, 29(4) only).
- A3.2 Directive 2025/25 renames it. Art 16(1) as amended: "companies listed in Annexes II and IIB have a European unique identifier ("EUID"), as referred to in point (9) of the Annex to" CIR 2021/1042; partnerships (Annex IIB) are newly covered | SRC-DIR-2025-25 Art 1(19) | read. Member States adopt transposition measures by 31 July 2027 | transposition article (read).
- A3.3 EUID structure. "The structure of the EUID shall be compliant with ISO 6523"; elements: country code (mandatory), register identifier (mandatory), registration number (mandatory), verification digit (optional) | SRC-CIR-2021-1042 Annex pt 9 [N] | read. "The EUID shall be used to unequivocally identify companies and branches for the purpose of the exchange of information between registers" | same.
- A3.4 EUID scope limit. It is a register-to-register identifier; Dir 2017/1132 recital on the unique identifier: companies "should not be obliged to include the unique identifier in the company letters" and keep the domestic number | read. Covers companies in Annex II/IIB only (capital companies and partnerships), not sole traders, associations or public bodies (inferred from Art 16(1) scope, read).
- A3.5 EUCC (EU Company Certificate). Art 16b(1): "accepted in all Member States as sufficient evidence, at the time of its issuance, of the incorporation of the company"; content incl. "the EUID of the company" (16b(2)(d)), registration number and Member State, status, legal representatives (2(j)) | SRC-DIR-2025-25 | read. Obtainable electronically, also via BRIS (16b(4)); free once per year (16b(5)); electronic form "authenticated by means of trust services" and "compatible with the European Digital Identity Wallet" (16b(6)) | read. Exempt from legalisation (16d(2)); safeguards against doubt (16e) | read.
- A3.6 Digital EU power of attorney Art 16c: companies in Annexes II/IIB "can use a template ... to authorise a person to represent the company"; authenticated by trust services, granting/revocation "compatible for use with the European Digital Identity Wallet" (16c(1)); "accepted as evidence" (16c(2)); template to be set by implementing act (16c(4)) | read. Scope: procedures within the Directive "in particular" formation, branches, conversions.
- A3.7 Directory/identity bridge: Directive 2025/25 recital 39 links EUCC to AML "know-your-customer" identification; BRIS-BORIS-IRI interconnection | read.

### A4 CIR 2024/2977 and CIR 2015/1502 (SRC-CIR-2024-2977 read; SRC-CIR-2015-1502 [N] read)
- A4.1 Annex section 2 Table 3, mandatory legal person identification data: "current legal name" and "a unique identifier constructed by the sending Member State ... as persistent as possible in time". Table 4 optional: current address, VAT registration number, tax reference number, EUID, "Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) referred to in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/1860", "Economic Operator Registration and Identification (EORI) referred to in" 1352/2013, excise number | read.
- A4.2 Wallet users enrolled "at assurance level high" per CIR 2015/1502 | CIR 2977 Art 3(7) | read. Wallet cryptographic operations at LoA high | CIR 2979 | read.
- A4.3 CIR 2015/1502 Annex 2.1.3 legal person proofing. Low: evidence recognised by the Member State, valid, "not known ... to be in a status that would prevent it from acting". Substantial adds name, legal form, "(if applicable) its registration number", checked against authoritative source. High: name, legal form "and at least one unique identifier representing the legal person used in a national context" and "checked to determine that it is valid according to an authoritative source". Alternatives: reuse of previously proven identity if equivalent assurance confirmed by a conformity assessment body; issuance on the basis of a notified means of the same level | read.
- A4.4 CIR 2015/1502 2.1.4 binding of natural-person and legal-person eID means: "It shall be possible to suspend and/or revoke a binding"; levels differ (nationally recognised procedures; registered in authoritative source; verified from authoritative source) | read. Recital 30: "This Regulation should not affect representation rights of legal persons" | read.
- A4.5 CIR 2015/1502 is an eID-means (natural/legal person means) regulation; EBW Art 6(1)(l) and 18(5)/Art 24(1a) reuse it only for specific points. No text states that EBWOID or a secondary-identifier attestation "has" a LoA; eIDAS LoA attaches to eID schemes, whereas attestations are covered by QEAA/PuB-EAA status and Art 24(1a) identity verification (inference, marked).

### A6 GLEIF / LEI / vLEI (official GLEIF and ISO TC68 pages only)
- A6.1 LEI. "a unique 20-character alphanumeric code"; "A public good, available free of charge to all users"; "Every LEI is unique and can only ever represent one entity"; issued via LEI issuers (LOUs); reference data from "authoritative local sources"; overseen by the Regulatory Oversight Committee | SRC-GLEIF-LEI [N] | read. - A6.5 ISO 17442-3:2024. ## C. Third-country and non-EBW interaction facts
- C1 EBW Art 17: equivalence of third-country wallets/frameworks only by Commission implementing act after assessment, with repeal/suspension and a public list (A1.14). No such act exists (proposal).
- C2 EBW Art 18: a third-country operator can own an EBW only after obtaining one EBWOID set and unique identifier, identity proofing by eIDAS Art 24(1a) methods, supervisory body notified, duplicate checks (A1.15). The identifier for such operators follows the Art 9(2),(4) implementing act (not yet drafted in sources read).
- C3 eIDAS Art 14: third-country trust services equivalent only by implementing act or agreement; third countries must publish a trusted list (A2.6). QEAA from third-country providers have no automatic recognition in text read.
- C4 eIDAS Art 45b(3) recognition covers attestations "issued by or on behalf of a public sector body responsible for an authentic source" in other Member States only; it says nothing on third countries or on private-source credentials such as LEI, GLN, BPN, DUNS (A2.5).
- C5 A counterparty that holds no business wallet and no EUDIW: EBW Art 5(1)(c) shares data "with European Business Wallet-relying parties", defined as natural persons, operators or public bodies relying upon EBWs (A1.13), so a non-wallet party can be a relying party of a presentation only if it can verify it; the proposal text read does not define a verification page, plain-web verification or fallback format for such a party. Directory (Art 10) is accessible only to owners, representatives and providers (A1.9), so a non-EBW party cannot look up an EUID there.
- C6 Non-EBW parties can still verify: EUID via the public BRIS/e-Justice portal (Dir 2017/1132 Art 22; harmonised search by name and registration number in CIR 2021/1042 section 11); EUCC in paper form via "unique protocol or identification number ... electronic verification" (Dir 2025/25 Art 16b(7)); Union trusted lists and qualified signatures/seals (eIDAS); LEI via free public GLEIF data (A6.1); LEI in a qualified seal certificate (A5.3). (BRIS public availability read in Dir 2017/1132 recitals; portal itself not tested.)
- C7 Non-wallet delivery: QERDS identifies sender with high confidence and addressee before delivery (A2.7); standalone QERDS for EUDIW users (Art 5(3)); EBW proposal does not extend QERDS to partners with neither.
- C8 No disadvantage for non-wallet persons (eIDAS Art 5a(15)); public bodies keep alternative channels for 36 months with a gateway (A1.18).
- C9 LoA across borders: eIDAS Art 6 cross-border recognition requires level equal to or higher than the service requires and at least substantial (read, Art 6(1)); EBW Art 6(1)(e) substantial or high; CIR 2015/1502 gives legal person proofing criteria per level (A4.3). Third-country identity proofing at EBW Art 18(5) refers to Art 24(1a) methods, which are "high level of confidence" methods, not to a named LoA.
- C10 Legal compliance across Member States: EUCC and digital EU power of attorney accepted in all Member States, exempt from legalisation (A3.5, A3.6); power of attorney and statutory representation remain under national law (Dir 2025/25 recital 75, read); EBW Art 6(2)(b) "all authorisation logic is interoperable across Member States". National registers differ: Art 9(2) and Art 8(2) rely on Member State notification of authentic sources.

### D1 Primary identifier model
Criteria: [S] EUID mandatory where attributed (Art 9(1)); [S] one unique identifier per owner (Art 9(4)); [S] EBWOID minimum is name plus identifier (Art 8(5)); [S] LPID replaced by EBWOID in WE BUILD (ADR 001); [S] EUID scope limited to Annex II/IIB companies (A3.4); [D] coverage of sole traders, associations, public bodies and non-EU entities; [D] persistence of identifier on cross-border conversion; [D] machine matching across sources.
Options (facts only): O1 EUID alone where it exists, equivalent national identifier otherwise (proposal text); O2 EBWOID as the primary attestation with EUID inside (WE BUILD profile); O3 EUCC as richer primary evidence in addition (Dir 2025/25; Spherity advice in ADR basic-lpid); O4 allow several primary identifiers for one owner (conflicts with Art 9(4) and Art 18(2), as stated).
Relationship: the proposal uses EUID inside EBWOID; Dir 2025/25 EUCC contains EUID; CIR 2977 lists EUID as optional next to mandatory "unique identifier".

### D2 Secondary identifiers issued quickly after authentication
Criteria: [S] QTSP verifies identity (Art 24(1a)) and may verify attributes "by means of a qualified electronic attestation of attributes" (Art 24(1b)(c)); [S] DUNS rulebook anchors self-issued EAAs in EBWOID; [S] Art 5(1)(g),(f) lets an EBW issue attestations; [S] authentic source of each identifier differs (tax, customs, GS1, LEI LOU, Catena-X CSP); [S] EBWOID not selectively disclosable in WE BUILD vs Art 5(1)(b); [D] latency of authentic-source lookup; [D] whether an issuer needs to re-prove identity if the presenter holds a valid EBWOID; [D] user control of linkage between identifiers (EBWOID id appears in every credential).
Options: O1 authentic-source issuer issues a credential after verifying a presented EBWOID (QEAA or PuB-EAA: tax, customs, register); O2 self-issued EAA by the owner anchored in EBWOID (WE BUILD DUNS pattern, EBWOID in header); O3 ecosystem authority issues (GS1 MO, Catena-X CSP, QVI) after validating EBWOID, then QTSP route per Art 24(1b)(c); O4 EBW provider issues all secondary credentials on behalf of the owner (not stated in sources).
Note: no source quantifies "quick"; Directory update is one working day (Art 10(5)) and QTSP revocation publication within 24 hours (Art 24(3)), which are the only time figures.

### D3 LEI credential without KERI
Criteria: [S] vLEI is ACDC/KERI-bound by GLEIF's EGF (A6.3); [S] ISO 17442-3 defines vLEI with ACDC (A6.5); [S] EBW/EU formats are mdoc, SD-JWT VC (ARF), VCDM listed in CIR 2024/2979 Annex II (RES-formats); [S] LEI in X.509 and as PID attribute is already standardised (A5.3, A4.1); [S] GLEIF itself states bridging KERI/ACDC to SD-JWT VC is future work (A6.6c); [D] who is entitled to issue a non-KERI "LEI credential" (GLEIF says vLEI issuers are QVIs; no non-KERI issuer role exists); [D] whether any LEI credential would be called vLEI; [D] whether a QTSP or an EU authentic-source issuer can attest the LEI relationship using free GLEIF data; [D] revocation when LEI lapses.
Options (all outside the official GLEIF scheme unless noted): O1 LEI as optional attribute in an EU PID/EBWOID-type attestation (CIR 2977 Table 4 pattern); O2 QEAA/PuB-EAA issued by a QTSP that verifies the LEI record via GLEIF public data (not defined in sources); O3 LEI in qualified seal/signature certificates (ETSI 319 412-1, ISO 17442-2); O4 an LEI credential in SD-JWT VC or W3C VC issued under GLEIF governance (no such offering found in official pages; would need GLEIF/ROC statement); O5 use vLEI as is (KERI/ACDC) through a gateway (excluded by project owner).

### D4 Interaction with non-EU partners
Criteria: [S] Art 17 equivalence and Art 18 owner route; [S] eIDAS Art 14; [S] LEI is global, free, public (A6.1); [S] GS1/DUNS/CX identifiers are global/ecosystem; [D] counterparties without any EU trust list; [D] unique-identifier assignment for non-EU entities (Art 9(2)); [D] sanctions/KYC use of LEI; [D] vLEI-only partners.
