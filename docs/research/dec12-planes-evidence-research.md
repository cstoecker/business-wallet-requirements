# RES-dec12-planes-evidence: control plane, data plane and the access-controlled verifiable evidence graph (K18)

Scope: facts from primary sources only; no recommendation. Date of work: 2026-10-04. Decision question (DEC-12): where are policies decided and enforced, how do credentials, evidence and data move, and should evidence be expressed as one access-controlled linked-data graph with open-world semantics that serves policy, register, product passport and AI processes?
Source IDs reuse `_data/graph/sources.yml`; sources not yet registered are marked (new) and listed in `/tmp/review/RES-dec12-new_sources.yml`. Read states: read = the cited passage was read in the full text of the original; partly-read = the cited section was read, the rest of the document was not; snippet = read only through a grep hit, a search hit or a citation in another document; carried = taken from an earlier research note of this project (RES-formats / RES-dec07 / RES-dec09 / company-law note), not re-read today; not read.
Method: EUR-Lex texts via publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/<CELEX> with `Accept: application/xhtml+xml` and `Accept-Language: eng` (eIDAS amending Regulation 2024/1183, Directive 2017/1132, CIR 2026/1778); W3C TRs, OASIS, Catena-X, Gaia-X, IDTA and OPA pages fetched directly (HTML converted to text; line searches). ## (A) FACTS FROM SOURCES

### A1 Control plane: policy decisions, authorisation, enforcement

- A1.1 Definition of authorisation in the Council text. "'authorisation' means the granting or recognition of a right or permission by a European Business Wallet owner to a European Business Wallet user to perform specified actions on specified resources or functionalities, and the corresponding access-control decision" | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Art 2(19) | read. The Commission proposal and the Council text differ: the Council text deletes the definitions of authorised representative and mandate (registered note). Whether the Commission text defines "authorisation" the same way was not re-checked.
- A1.2 User authorisation function. Wallets enable owners to "authorise multiple European Business Wallet users to access and operate the European Business Wallet of the owner" and to "manage and revoke authorisations, including authorisations associated to roles ... without prejudice to any power of attorney or legal mandate" | Council Art 5(1)(j) | read. A separate function lets owners "authorise European Business Wallet-relying parties to request electronic attestations of attributes issued to the" owner and revoke it | Art 5(1)(k) | read.
- A1.3 Role mappings. Providers ensure mappings between roles and attributes are "verifiable, auditable, revocable and traceable to their legitimate issuers"; conflicts of roles, over-delegation or expired authorisations are "automatically detected and prevented in real-time"; "all authorisation logic is interoperable between European Business Wallets" | Council Art 6(2)(b) and Annex 12(3); SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 6(2)(b) carried | read.
- A1.4 Access control mechanism (Council Annex point 12). Authorisation decisions are "based on one or more of the following criteria": attestation of the acting subject; formal role "within a recognised organisational structure or economic operator"; "scope, validity and constraints of any mandate, delegation, or power of attorney"; "contextual information or policies and rules adopted at Union or national level for sector-specific compliance" | Annex 12(1) | read. Outcomes shall be "fine-grained and auditable": visibility of credentials "selective and conditioned on access rights"; access to processes "controlled by real-time validation of roles and mandates"; all access and execution events "logged, timestamped, and bound to cryptographically verifiable proofs of authorisation" | Annex 12(2) | read.
- A1.5 Policy language is left to implementing acts. Implementing acts "shall cover in particular ... the formats for the representation of roles and attributes; interoperability mechanisms for mandates and delegations across wallets; protocols, policy language and constraint enforcement; requirements for secure logging" | Council Annex 12(4)(a) to (d) | read. Compliance presumed where those standards are met | Annex 12(5) | read. Nothing in the text names ODRL, XACML, OPA or any policy language.
- A1.6 Nature of the authorisation. Council recital 18: authorisations "should be understood to be of a technical nature and to not create, limit, or otherwise affect any power of attorney or legal mandate" and should be "compatible with the EU digital power of attorney, as established by Directive (EU) 2025/25" | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 recital 18 | read. Recital only, not operative in the Commission text (review note on EBW-CON-005).
- A1.7 Relying-party side, authorisation of requests. Wallet units "authorise requests and, where applicable, authenticate those made through European Digital Identity Wallets relying-party access certificates or European Digital Identity Wallet unit attestations. Authentication of the relying party shall be required where attestations are intended for a restricted audience; in all other cases, attestations may be presented by any requesting party" | Council Annex 13(1) | read. This is the only place read where open presentation is allowed.
- A1.8 EUDI relying-party registration (eIDAS). Relying parties "shall register in the Member State where [they are] established" and provide "the intended use ..., including an indication of the data to be requested"; they "shall not request users to provide any data other than that indicated" | SRC-REG-2024-1183 (new, eIDAS Art 5b(1), (2)(c), (3)) | read. Member States publish the information "in electronically signed or sealed form suitable for automated processing" | Art 5b(5) | read. Art 5b applies to relying parties of European Digital Identity Wallets; the EBW texts read do not state that EBW-relying parties must register (to verify).
- A1.9 Registration and access certificates (CIR 2025/848). A "wallet-relying party registration certificate" is "a data object that describes the intended use of the relying party and indicates the attributes the relying party has registered to intend to request"; certificates "include a general access policy, being syntactically and semantically harmonised across the Union, informing users that the wallet-relying party is only allowed to request the data specified" | SRC-CIR-2025-848 Art 2, Art 8(2)(d) | read. Registered data per intended use are listed "in a machine-readable format for automated processing" | Annex I | read. An access certificate is "a certificate for electronic seals or signatures authenticating and validating the wallet-relying party" | Art 2 | read. Member States "shall authorise at least one certificate authority" for access certificates (Art 7) and "may" authorise issuers of registration certificates (Art 8(1)).
- A1.10 Wallet-side checks. The wallet "shall validate the wallet-relying party registration certificate" before presenting a request to the user, and compares requested and registered attributes ("over-asking") | SRC-CIR-2026-1731 Annex XII clauses WRP-VALIDATION-01, WRP-OVERASKING-01/-02 | carried from RES-dec07 A2.6 (read). Access-certificate validation must be done in the wallet "without delegating execution ... to an operating system browser or other intermediary application"; Art 3(4) (registration certificate) applies from 11 August 2028 | Art 4 of 2026/1731 | carried. Both are placed in the wallet, so the enforcement point for registered-use checks in the EUDI model is the holder wallet.
- A1.11 ARF. "The EUDI Wallet shall show ... the User-friendly description of the Relying Party's intended use and the link to the applicable privacy policy" | SRC-ARF-HLR (registration certificate requirements) | read via grep of the local copy. Access Certificate Authorities follow ETSI TS 119 411-8 and need a revocation policy | ARF Topic 27 text | read via grep. Providers of PID, QEAA and PuB-EAA "SHALL have a policy" governing issuance and revocation | ARF | read via grep.
- A1.12 Agent mandates. Previous passes (DEC-10 note): RFC 9396 authorization_details, RFC 8693 token exchange, MCP authorization, A2A and the W3C AI Agent Protocol CG draft describe delegated authority for agents | SRC-RFC-9396, SRC-RFC-8693, SRC-MCP-AUTH, SRC-A2A, SRC-W3C-AIAP-ID | carried. None of these defines a legal mandate; the Council text states EBW authorisations are technical (A1.6).
- A1.13 ODRL (W3C). Policies "represent permitted and prohibited actions over a certain asset, as well as the obligations required to be meet by stakeholders"; constraints and duties can be attached; Policy, Set, Offer and Agreement classes; conflict strategy values perm, prohibit, invalid, with "invalid" as default; ODRL Evaluator defined as "a system that determines whether the Rules of an ODRL Policy expression have meet their intended action performance" | SRC-ODRL-IM-2.2 (new) sections 2.1, 2.5, 2.6, 2.10 and terminology | read (sections). W3C Recommendation 15 February 2018. The model describes policies; it does not define a PDP or PEP architecture (no such section found).
- A1.14 XACML 3.0 (OASIS Standard, 22 January 2013). PDP: "the system entity that evaluates applicable policy and renders an authorization decision"; PEP: "the system entity that performs access control, by making decision requests and enforcing authorization decisions"; "The PEP MUST abide by the authorization decision" | SRC-XACML-3.0 (new) glossary and section on PEP | read (glossary and PEP section). Only an option: no EU text read names it.
- A1.15 OPA (CNCF graduated project documentation). "OPA decouples policy decision-making from policy enforcement" and decisions are "not limited to simple yes/no or allow/deny answers" | SRC-OPA-DOCS (new) overview page | read (overview). Project documentation, not a vendor product page; no EU text read names it. Option only.
- A1.16 Dataspace Protocol (DSP, Eclipse, Release 2025-1, repository 8c20a5b). "This document specifies how data usage requirements are expressed as Policies (reusing terminology from ODRL)"; "A Dataset MUST have at least one hasPolicy attribute that contains an Offer"; an Agreement "MUST have a target property"; policy semantics beyond that are not given (correction: the quote "The semantics of such tokens are not part of this specification" concerns authorization tokens, not policy semantics; see evidence-claims-3); presentation of proofs for a catalog request "is outside the scope of the Dataspace Protocol" | SRC-DSP scope.md, catalog.protocol.md, contract.negotiation.protocol.md, common.protocol.md | read (those files); partly-read overall. DSP does not define where policies are evaluated.
- A1.17 Catena-X. The connector must support credential checks driven by policy constraints; "the conventions for policy constraints ... are defined in the Catena-X standard CX-0152"; the mapping rule derives a credential name from the constraint operands; ODRL is listed in the terms | SRC-CX-0018 v4.3.0 (preview; page title) 2.4 and terminology | read (those sections). The earlier note read v4.2.1; version to be reconciled. CX-0152 not read today.
- A1.18 Gaia-X. Compliance rules and trust anchors are documented in the Compliance Document 24.04-prerelease (read in part, SRC-GAIAX-COMPLIANCE-24.04); the Gaia-X ontology publishes SHACL shapes and a JSON-LD context for Gaia-X Credentials, which "ensure the fulfillment of the policies and rules agreed by the participants" | SRC-GAIAX-ONTOLOGY (new) home page | read. No PDP/PEP statement found in the pages read. IDSA: the IDSA knowledge-base URL returned a stub; no IDSA text read beyond DSP (an IDSA-led specification in origin; hosted by Eclipse).
- A1.19 VCDM 2.0 `termsOfUse`. "The value of the termsOfUse property MUST specify one or more terms of use policies"; each value "MUST specify its type"; a recipient unwilling to adhere "do[es] so on their own responsibility and might incur legal liability" | SRC-VCDM-2.0 5.5 | read. Policy content is left to the type definition; the spec does not require enforcement.

### A2 Data plane: issuance, presentation, exchange, delivery

All items below are carried from RES-dec07 (read in full or by section there) unless stated; today only the protocols that the graph depends on were re-checked.
- A2.1 Common protocols. Providers "shall ensure support for common protocols and interfaces" for issuance, request and validation, sharing and presenting, automatic interaction, wallet-to-wallet, relying-party authentication, registered delivery and others | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 6(1)(a) to (l) | carried, read. Standards are left to implementing acts (Art 6(5)).
- A2.2 EUDI protocols. CIR 2024/2982, as amended by 2026/1731, cites ETSI TS 119 472-3 (issuance, OpenID4VCI) and ETSI TS 119 472-2 (presentation, OpenID4VP/HAIP profile and ISO/IEC 18013-5/-7 profile) | SRC-CIR-2026-1731 Annexes XI, XII | carried, read.
- A2.3 OpenID4VCI 1.0 and OpenID4VP 1.0 (final). Issuance flows (authorization code, pre-authorized code), batch issuance, DCQL queries with claims queries, response encryption, transaction data, and "Wallets SHOULD obtain explicit, informed consent" before release; no passage addresses unattended machine presentation | SRC-OID4VCI-1.0, SRC-OID4VP-1.0 | carried, read in parts. DCQL claim queries address claims by path inside one credential; no cross-credential traversal was found in the sections read (to verify).
- A2.4 Exchange between data-space participants. DSP defines catalog, negotiation and transfer process states (REQUESTED, STARTED, TERMINATED, COMPLETED, SUSPENDED); the data transfer itself is out of scope ("This document does not apply to the Data Transfer Protocol"); DCP conveys identity and presentations between participants and says "presentation protocols that rely on end-user (i.e., human) consent are not applicable" | SRC-DSP, SRC-DCP | read (parts). DCP profiles: VCDM 2.0 with JOSE enveloped proofs and Bitstring Status List, or VCDM 1.1 with StatusList2021 | SRC-DCP dcp.profiles.md | read.
- A2.5 Registered delivery. Qualified electronic registered delivery is mandatory in the wallet in the Council text (Annex 11); ETSI EN 319 522-4-1 binds messages to AS4 ("ERDS shall only use the push message exchange pattern"; messages "signed and encrypted by the sending ERDS") | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex 11; SRC-ETSI-EN-319522-4-1 | carried, read.
- A2.6 AAS API (high level). AAS Part 2 defines technology-neutral operations and an HTTP/REST API for shells and submodels; the 3.2 annex maps the DPP API of EN 18222 to AAS services (life-cycle API, DPP registry API, fine-grained API), and states that "access control rules shall be implemented" referring to IDTA-01004 Part 4 | SRC-IDTA-AAS-P2-DPP (new) annex and index | read (annex and index only). Part 4 not read.
- A2.7 Separation of planes in EU text. The EBW proposal and the Council text read use no term "plane"; the word is this project's (see the concept model figure, `_figures/concept-model-planes.md`: "the project's own reference model ... not a figure of any standard").

### A3 Evidence graph: linked-data standards and what they state

Verbatim quotes are at most 25 words.
- A3.1 VCDM 2.0 (W3C Recommendation 15 May 2025). Open world: "This approach to data modeling is often called an open world assumption, meaning that any entity can say anything about any other entity" | SRC-VCDM-2.0 section 5.2 | read. JSON-LD 1.1 is the serialisation, and "JSON-LD compacted document form MUST be used" for application/vc | 6.1 | read. "Verifiable credentials and verifiable presentations MUST include a @context property." and "Application developers MUST understand every JSON-LD context used by their application" | 4.3 | read.
- A3.2 VCDM 2.0, graphs. "merging data between two verifiable credentials without confirming that they are discussing the same entities ... can lead to a corrupted data set"; each credential in a presentation is "a separate named graph of type VerifiableCredentialGraph"; do not merge objects lacking "an id property that uses a global identifier" | SRC-VCDM-2.0 5.12 | read. Directly relevant to Option B (verifier-side merge).
- A3.3 VCDM 2.0, evidence and zero-knowledge. The `evidence` property exists (5.6: "If present, the value of the evidence property MUST be either a single object or an array of objects"; type REQUIRED). Section 5.7: ZKP mechanisms can give selective disclosure, unlinkable disclosure and non-correlatable identification; "Specification authors that create securing mechanisms MUST NOT design them in such a way that they leak information that would enable the verifier to correlate a holder" | SRC-VCDM-2.0 5.6, 5.7 | read. Informative note on machine actors holding credentials carried from RES-formats.
- A3.4 JSON-LD 1.1 (W3C Recommendation 16 July 2020). A syntax to serialise "an RDF graph, or an RDF Dataset in a JSON syntax"; named graphs supported (section 4.9); `@protected` terms | SRC-JSONLD-1.1 (new) abstract, 4.9 | read (abstract, TOC, section lines). Processing algorithms are in a companion (JSON-LD 1.1 API) not read.
- A3.5 RDF 1.1 Concepts (W3C Recommendation 25 February 2014). Defines graph-based data model of IRIs, blank nodes and datatyped literals; section 1.7 "Equivalence, Entailment and Inconsistency"; semantics are in RDF 1.1 Semantics | SRC-RDF11-CONCEPTS (new) | read (abstract, TOC, intro). RDF 1.1 Semantics: only the statement that "All entailment regimes MUST be monotonic extensions of the simple entailment regime" was read | SRC-RDF11-MT (new) | snippet.
- A3.6 RDFS (W3C Recommendation 25 February 2014). Schema vocabulary; "does not say whether or how an application should use it" for some constructs (section on documentation vocabulary); does not state closed- or open-world | SRC-RDFS-1.1 (new) | partly-read.
- A3.7 OWL 2 Primer (W3C Recommendation 11 December 2012, second edition). "If some fact is not present in a database, it is usually considered false (the so-called closed-world assumption) whereas in the case of an OWL 2 document it may simply be missing (but possibly true), following the open-world assumption"; OWL "does not make the assumption that different names are names for different individuals" (no unique name assumption) | SRC-OWL2-PRIMER (new) | read (those passages). OWL 2 Overview: no passage on world assumption found.
- A3.8 SHACL (W3C Recommendation 20 July 2017). "A language for validating RDF graphs against a set of conditions", expressed as shapes; produces a validation report; `sh:closed` constrains which properties a focus node may have | SRC-SHACL (new) abstract, section 4.8.1, example | read (those parts). The text read does not use the terms closed or open world; its validation is a check of a given data graph, which this note calls closed-world validation by the project's usage (analysis, not a quote).
- A3.9 Data Integrity 1.0 (W3C Recommendation 15 May 2025). Securing mechanism over canonicalised JSON/RDF (JCS or RDFC); "Not all software or cryptosuites are capable of providing selective disclosure"; a cryptosuite supporting it is bbs-2023; selective disclosure "in a way that does not preserve unlinkability" is possible; unlinkability "ensures that no correlatable data are used in a digitally-signed payload" | SRC-VC-DI-1.0 (new) 6.1, 6.2 | read (those sections).
- A3.10 Data Integrity ECDSA Cryptosuites 1.0 (Recommendation 15 May 2025) defines ecdsa-sd-2023 (base proof, derived proof, mandatory N-Quads); its privacy section points to BBS for "unlinkable disclosure" (so ecdsa-sd-2023 does not offer it) | SRC-VC-DI-ECDSA (new) 3.6, 5.1 | read (headings and 5.1).
- A3.11 Data Integrity BBS Cryptosuites 1.0 is a W3C Candidate Recommendation Draft of 10 September 2026: "unlinkable derived proofs"; optional anonymous holder binding and credential-bound pseudonyms; privacy sections on linkage via mandatory reveal and holder selective reveal | SRC-VC-DI-BBS (new) | read (abstract, TOC, 6.2 start). Not a Recommendation.
- A3.12 VC-JOSE-COSE (W3C Recommendation 15 May 2025): secures VCDM with JWS and SD-JWT; ETSI TS 119 472-1 clause 7.6 uses it (A3.13) | SRC-VC-JOSE-COSE (new) | snippet.
- A3.13 ETSI TS 119 472-1 V1.2.1 (2026-02), clause 7 "Implementation of EAA based on W3C Verifiable Credentials (JSON-LD W3C-VC)": "A JSON-LD W3C-VC EAA shall use the JSON-LD syntax"; secured "either with embedded proofs or with enveloping proofs"; with selective disclosure "shall be signed using SD-JWT"; `@context` shall be an array of URIs; `ShaclSchemaCredential` is one schema type (SHACL) | SRC-ETSI-119472-1 clauses 7.1, 7.2, 7.6, 4.2.1.4 | read. So the standard has a JSON-LD plus SD-JWT realisation; it is not in the legal list (A6.1).
- A3.14 SD-JWT (RFC 9901) selective disclosure covers "individual elements of a JSON data structure"; unlinkability limited: "does not meet the security properties for anonymous credentials"; applies to salted-hash approaches "including mDL/mDoc"; SD-JWT VC defines no `vct` values or claim semantics and has no `@context` | SRC-RFC-9901, SRC-IETF-SDJWT-VC | carried from RES-formats A1 (read).
- A3.15 Evidence and graph in ESPR implementing text: see A4.4 (links between DPP attributes and underlying evidence).
- A3.16 PROV-O (W3C Recommendation 30 April 2013). Classes and properties include prov:Agent, prov:wasGeneratedBy, prov:wasDerivedFrom, prov:wasAttributedTo, prov:actedOnBehalfOf and prov:Bundle; "Agent ... bears some form of responsibility for an activity ... for the existence of an entity, or for another agent's activity"; encoding of Bundles in RDF is not specified (illustrated with TriG) | SRC-PROV-O (new) | read (vocabulary lists and agent definition).

### A4 Registers and product passports

- A4.1 BRIS and EUID. Directive 2017/1132 Art 16(1): Member States "shall ensure that companies have a unique identifier allowing them to be unequivocally identified in communications between registers through the system of interconnection of registers"; Art 22: the system is "composed of the registers of Member States, the platform, the portal", and Member States "ensure the interoperability of their registers" via the platform | SRC-DIR-2017-1132 (original text, as fetched today) | read (Arts 16(1), 22(1)-(3)). Recital 169 states the Directive "is not aimed at establishing any centralised registers database storing substantive information". Amendments by Directive 2025/25 (EU Company Certificate, digital power of attorney, wallet compatibility; application 31 July 2028) and CIR 2021/1042 point 9 (EUID structure, ISO 6523) | company-law research note | carried.
- A4.2 ESPR (Regulation (EU) 2024/1781). DPP "shall be connected through a data carrier to a persistent unique product identifier"; data "based on open standards, developed with an interoperable format, and shall be, as appropriate, machine-readable, structured, searchable, and transferable through an open interoperable data exchange network without vender lock-in"; "access to data ... shall be regulated" by access rights at product-group level; the DPP "shall be fully interoperable ... in relation to the technical, semantic and organisational aspects"; access "based on their respective access rights"; write rights "restricted based on the access rights"; "data authentication, reliability and integrity shall be ensured"; stored by the economic operator or by DPP service providers who may not reuse the data; the Commission "may adopt implementing acts setting out procedures to issue and verify the digital credentials of economic operators and other relevant actors that have access rights" | SRC-ESPR Arts 10(1), 11, 9(2)(f),(g) | read.
- A4.3 DPP registry (ESPR Art 13). By 19 July 2026 the Commission sets up a registry that "stores in a secure manner at least the unique identifiers"; economic operators upload; the registry returns a unique registration identifier; Commission, national and customs authorities have access; an implementing act sets the arrangements | SRC-ESPR Art 13(1)-(6) | read. Art 14: public web portal consistent "with their respective access rights".
- A4.4 CIR (EU) 2026/1778 of 16 July 2026 (OJ 17.7.2026) on the registry. Components: a secure user interface, "an API for registering the digital product passport and receiving information", a verification platform, a scheme for unique registration identifiers, a storage component for identifiers and commodity codes, a list of verified service providers, "a semantic repository" and a log system | SRC-CIR-2026-1778 (new) Art 3(1) | read. Legal-person economic operators are verified by "a qualified electronic seal supported by a qualified certificate" or, outside the Union, also by "an electronic attestation of attributes issued under Union law" | Art 4(2) | read (extract). "All data contained within a digital product passport shall be structured in accordance with the common data models and semantic definitions published in the semantic repository" | Art 11(3) | read. The semantic repository holds "technical specifications for creating, where relevant, typed and resolvable links between different digital product passports, and links between digital product passport attributes and underlying evidence communicated through the product value chain", data models "and their formats", role semantics, and metadata "in conformity with the DCAT-AP"; semantic specifications include "ontologies, data models, controlled vocabularies, and code lists" | Art 12(2),(3); Art 2 definitions | read. Registration data are versioned and logged (Art 10). This is the only EU legal text read that speaks of ontologies and typed, resolvable links between records and evidence. It does not require RDF, JSON-LD or an open-world reading (the text read names no serialisation); the registry stores identifiers, not the passport content.
- A4.5 DPP standards. Eight CEN standards of 2026 are named in the IDTA annex (EN 18219 identifiers, 18220 data carriers, 18216 exchange protocols, 18222 APIs, 18223 system interoperability, 18221 storage, 18239 access rights and security, 18246 authentication and integrity; the last two "not yet released") | SRC-IDTA-AAS-P2-DPP annex | snippet. Not read; their content is not stated here.
- A4.6 IDTA DPP4.0 (high level). A DPP is composed of an AAS for the product, a DppMetadata submodel (semanticId https://admin-shell.io/idta/cds/dppMetadata/1) with header fields (including `contentSpecificationIds`) and content submodels; the AAS Part 1 metamodel uses `semanticId` and has an RDF serialisation | SRC-IDTA-AAS-P2-DPP; SRC-IDTA-AAS-P1 | read (annex, Part 1 by term search). IDTA Part 4 (security) not read. No IDTA text on verifiable credentials found (carried).
- A4.7 Ecosystem credentials. Catena-X CX-0050 defines credentials as JSON Schema structures, and the connector maps policy constraints to those credentials (A1.17); DCP profiles use VCDM 2.0/1.1 (A2.4) | SRC-CX-0050, SRC-CX-0018, SRC-DCP | read (parts). Gaia-X Credentials use JSON-LD contexts and SHACL shapes (A1.18). Whether CX-0050 credentials carry `@context` and how it is resolved was not visible (carried).

### A5 AI processing, provenance and logs

- A5.1 AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, consolidated version of 27 July 2026). ## (C) OPTIONS

### C0 Control plane policy language and decision point (short; sources enable, restrict, leave open)

| Option | Enables | Restricts | Leaves open |
|---|---|---|---|
| ODRL policies (as in DSP and Catena-X) | Same vocabulary as data-space contracts (DSP offers and agreements); linked-data policy; W3C Recommendation | ODRL is an information model and defines no PDP/PEP; evaluation semantics (conflicts, constraint evaluation) needs a profile; not named in EBW texts | Decision point placement; mapping to Annex 12 criteria (role, mandate scope) |
| XACML 3.0 or OPA-style PDP/PEP | PDP/PEP split documented (XACML); decision separate from enforcement (OPA) | Not named in EU text; XACML is an XML-era standard of 2013; policy not linked data | Which attributes (attestations) feed the PDP; EU policy language to be set by implementing acts (Annex 12(4)(c)) |
| EBW-specific role/mandate attestations plus wallet-internal rules | Directly matches Annex 12(1) and 6(2)(b); registration certificate "general access policy" is of this kind | Format and interoperability left to implementing acts; no cross-ecosystem reach | Bridge to ODRL for data space use |

### Option G1 Credential-per-fact only (no graph; each fact an independent credential, SD-JWT VC/mdoc)

- Enables: full fit with the legal list (C1); mature SD-JWT VC/mdoc flows (OpenID4VCI/VP, HAIP); per-claim selective disclosure; relying-party registration checks by attribute lists (C5); simple revocation per credential; offline presentation (mdoc); AI and policy engines work over claims plus rulebook (`vct`, catalogue).
- Restricts: no standard way to state typed links between credentials beyond ETSI EAA chain linkage (Council Art 5(1)(g), "chain") and identifiers inside claims; semantics only via rulebooks and Type Metadata, not via `@context`; DPP "typed and resolvable links ... to underlying evidence" (CIR 2026/1778 Art 12) need an outside mapping; each added link requires a new credential and issuer.
- Leaves open: how a verifier assembles facts into a view (application logic); open-world extension at the verifier; DPP and register links.

### Option G2 Per-credential JSON-LD graph with verifier-side merge (VCDM 2.0, JSON-LD, optional SHACL; secured by SD-JWT via VC-JOSE-COSE, Data Integrity ECDSA-SD or BBS)

- Enables: VCDM 5.2 extensibility and 5.12 named graphs, ETSI TS 119 472-1 clause 7 (JSON-LD with SD-JWT), DCP profiles, Gaia-X credentials, DPP semantic repository alignment (ontologies, typed links); verifier chooses open-world reasoning (RDFS/OWL) or closed-world SHACL validation locally; DI BBS offers unlinkable disclosure (CRD only).
- Restricts: not in the legal list (clause 7 outside clauses 2 to 6; recital 18 conditional); the merge rule (VCDM 5.12) forces global identifiers, which are correlatable (DI 6.2); ECDSA-SD is not unlinkable; BBS is a draft; JSON-LD processing has security burdens (context validation: "MUST understand every JSON-LD context"); proof by canonicalisation (RDFC) costs; ISO mdoc has no equivalent.
- Leaves open: whether EU implementing acts will list JSON-LD profiles (recital 18); handling of shapes when attributes are hidden; who publishes contexts and vocabularies (DPP semantic repository exists for products only).

### Option G3 Shared access-controlled graph or service (provider or ecosystem holds merged graph; queries answered under policy)

- Enables: cross-credential queries and provenance traversal in one place; central policy evaluation (PDP/PEP) matching Annex 12 "real-time validation"; DPP-style registry plus services (ESPR Art 11 service providers, Art 13 registry) and AAS/DPP APIs; machine and AI access through a single API; logging (Annex 12(2)(c)) in one place.
- Restricts: custody and minimisation questions: provider-held data of the owner; the EBW articles read name no such graph service; export and portability (Art 5(1)(l), NFR-082) must cover it; selective disclosure and holder control are replaced by access policy; offline use not possible; wallet-side relying-party checks (A1.10) do not apply to a service; GDPR assessment not done (not read).
- Leaves open: legal basis and role of the provider; whether a graph answer carries the issuers' signatures (verifiable evidence) or only the service's; trust in the service (notified provider status, trust list); alignment of DSP/ODRL policies with the graph policies.

## (D) CONFLICTS WITH THE CLOSED-WORLD, SD-JWT-FIRST DIRECTION OF CIR 2026/1731

1. Legal list. Annex IV (new Annex II of 2024/2979) applies clauses 2 to 6 of ETSI TS 119 472-1 V1.2.1; the JSON-LD realisation is clause 7 (A3.13). By my reading of the clause range, a JSON-LD graph credential is not within the list; whether it is nevertheless allowed as an additional format needs legal review (2024/2979 Art 8 wording not re-read). Recital 18 allows W3C VCDM support "when the new profiles on the W3C VCDM format are available" (a conditional, not an obligation).
2. Semantics. SD-JWT VC defines no semantics and has no `@context`; meaning comes from `vct`, Type Metadata and rulebooks (carried). Open-world statements (VCDM 5.2) have no equivalent in the legal list; the attribute catalogue is a closed enumeration per scheme.
3. Closed attribute lists vs graph traversal. Registration certificates list the attributes a relying party "intends to request" and the RP "shall not request ... any data other than" those (eIDAS 5b(3)); wallets check over-asking (WRP-OVERASKING). A graph query that returns linked facts not enumerated in the registration is not covered by this check (to verify for EBW, because Art 5b is written for EUDI Wallet RPs).
4. Merge and correlation. VCDM 5.12 asks for global identifiers when merging; ARF and RFC 9901 aim at unlinkability, with the salted-hash limits stated in RFC 9901 10.1. Shared identifiers (EUID, EBWOID, product identifiers) are correlatable by design (DI 6.2 example of a correlatable number).
5. Unlinkable selective disclosure. BBS is a Candidate Recommendation Draft (10 Sep 2026) and not in the list; ECDSA-SD gives selective disclosure but not unlinkability (A3.10).
6. Status and revocation. 2026/1731 cites a Token Status List draft; DCP uses Bitstring Status List or StatusList2021 (A2.4). A graph of mixed credentials needs several status mechanisms.
7. Presentation protocols. OpenID4VP/HAIP and ISO 18013-7 present one credential per claim query set; the sections read give no graph-level query (A2.3). The consent assumption (end user) does not fit unattended agents (A2.3); DCP says human-consent protocols are not applicable (A2.4).
8. Closed-world validation versus selective disclosure. SHACL validates the data graph at hand (A3.8). A selectively disclosed subgraph may violate a shape that the full credential satisfies. This is the project's analysis, not stated in a source.
9. Provider-held graph versus holder control. Council Annex 12(2)(a) conditions visibility on access rights, which suits a service; EUDI/EBW rules (user "sole control", RP authentication in the wallet, A1.10) assume the holder wallet as the decision point. The EBW text read does not say a provider may store a merged graph (to verify against GDPR and Art 5).
10. DPP registry. CIR 2026/1778 puts identifiers, not passport data, in the registry; a shared graph of passport content is not required by it and would sit beside it.
