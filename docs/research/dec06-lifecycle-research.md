# RES-dec06-lifecycle: credential lifecycle and revocation (DEC-06, cluster K07)

Scope: facts from primary sources only; no recommendation. Date of work: 2026-10-03. Question: how are validity, status and revocation handled for credentials, wallet units and authorisations, with which latency and which privacy properties?
Source IDs reuse `_data/graph/sources.yml`; IDs marked (new) are in `/tmp/cand/I3-new_sources.yml`; SRC-EBW-ANNEX-CELLAR is registered in `/tmp/cand/I2-new_sources.yml`. States: read = relevant passage read in full text; part = read in part (named clauses); not read.
Texts used: EBW proposal /tmp/claude-0/s/838.txt and its Annex (Cellar DOC_2); Council ST 9684/26 (2 June 2026); consolidated eIDAS (18.10.2024); CIR 2024/2977, 2024/2979, 2024/2981, 2025/1569, 2025/847, 2026/1731 (OJ texts); ARF v3.0.0 as rendered at eudi.dev/latest (HLR annex 2.02 topics 7, 9, 29, 38, 40; main chapter 4; Topic AB not needed here); IETF draft-ietf-oauth-status-list-21 (21 June 2026; the CIR cites version -20 of 20 April 2026, also read); WE BUILD architecture repo (commit 8fcfea0) and rulebook catalogue (commit de79cca).
Caveat 1: COM(2025) 838 is a proposal; ST 9684/26 is the Council general approach text; the outcome of the meeting of 9 June 2026 was not read.
Caveat 2: The detailed status rules (CIR 2024/2977, 2024/2979, 2025/1569, 2026/1731, ARF) bind EUDI Wallets, PID providers and qualified or public-sector attestation providers. The EBW texts give no status mechanism of their own beyond the points in A1; they point to implementing acts.

## (A) FACTS FROM SOURCES

### A1 What the EBW texts say about validity, revocation and authorisations
- L1.1 Wallet revocation grounds. "ensure that the validity of the European Business Wallets can be revoked" upon explicit request of the owner, compromise of security, permanent or temporary cessation of the owner's activity, or where the provider is "not included in the list" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 6(2)(f); SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Art 6(2)(f) (same four grounds) | read.
- L1.2 Validation mechanism. Providers "provide validation mechanisms, in order to ensure that the authenticity and validity of European Business Wallets can be verified" and notify the mechanism to the Commission, which publishes it in signed form (Art 6(2)(d), (g), 6(3)); relying parties get protocols "for relying parties to verify the authenticity and validity" (Art 6(1)(h)) | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL | read.
- L1.3 Unit attestation revocation (Annex 6). Providers publish a policy "specifying the conditions and the timeframe for the revocation of Wallets unit attestations"; inform affected users "no later than 24 hours from the revocation" with reason and consequences; "make publicly available the validity status of the European Business Wallet unit attestation" and describe its location in the attestation. The Commission and Council wording of points 6(1) to 6(3) is the same | SRC-EBW-ANNEX-CELLAR Annex point 6; SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex point 6 | read. Unlike CIR 2024/2979 Art 7(4) the EBW text does not say "in a privacy preserving manner".
- L1.4 Right to request revocation. Providers inform representatives (Council: owners and authorised users) of "the right to request revocation of their wallet unit attestation" using the authentication mechanism of Annex point 1 | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 7(6)(c); SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Art 7(6)(c) | read.
- L1.5 Authorisations inside the wallet. The owner can "manage and revoke such authorisations" (Art 5(1)(j)); role mappings are "verifiable, auditable, revocable and traceable to their legitimate issuers"; "conflicts of roles, over-delegation, or expired authorisations are automatically detected and prevented in real time" (Art 6(2)(b)); Council Annex 12(2)(b): access "is controlled by real-time validation of roles and mandates"; Annex 12(1)(c): decisions rest on "the scope, validity and constraints of any mandate, delegation, or power of attorney" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 5(1)(j), 6(2)(b); SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex point 12 | read. Authorisations of relying parties: owner can "manage and revoke" them (Art 5(1)(k)).
- L1.6 Owner identification data. May be issued as qualified attestation of attributes by a QTSP, as an attestation by a public sector body, or by the Commission (Art 8(3)); the Council recital states "Qualified electronic attestations of attributes can be easily updated or revoked." The proposal sets no validity period and no revocation trigger for it | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 8(3); SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 recital 33 | read.
- L1.7 Provider level events. Providers notify owners "in the event of suspension, revocation or voluntary termination" of services and of removal from the list (Art 7(6)(f)); supervisory bodies "revoke the inclusion" of non-compliant providers (Art 13(5)(k)); implementing acts may provide for "temporarily suspending the provider from the list" (Art 13(12)); supervisors report list changes to the Commission "within 24 hours" (Art 12(1)); Council: self-assessment is updated immediately where "a function affecting the security, validity, authenticity or portability of the European Business Wallet is suspended, revoked or materially modified" (Art 7(6b)(c)) | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL; SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 | read. The EBW texts do not say what happens to the status of attestations or authorisations in units of a delisted provider.
- L1.8 Directory. "Any modification or revocation concerning the information" in the European Digital Directory goes to the Commission "without undue delay and in any event within one working day" | SRC-EBW-PROPOSAL Art 10(5) | read.
- L1.9 Use of the EUDI Wallet statutory text. Art 5a(9) of eIDAS (EUDI Wallet): revocation upon explicit request of the user, compromise, and "upon the death of the user or cease of activity of the legal person" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 5a(9) | read. The EBW text mentions cessation of activity of the owner instead (L1.1).

### A2 Qualified certificates, seals and attestations (eIDAS)
- L2.1 Timing of publication. A QTSP issuing qualified certificates that revokes one "publish the revocation status of the certificate in a timely manner, and in any event within 24 hours after the receipt of the request"; "The revocation shall become effective immediately upon its publication." Paragraphs 3 and 4 apply to qualified attestations of attributes (Art 24(4a)) | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 24(3), (4a) | read.
- L2.2 Status service. Information on validity or revocation "available at least on a per certificate basis at any time and beyond the validity period of the certificate" in an automated manner | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 24(4) | read. Annex III(i) and Annex V(i): the certificate or attestation carries "the information or the location of the services that can be used to enquire about the validity status" | read.
- L2.3 Irreversibility. Revoked qualified certificates for signatures and seals, qualified attestations and public-sector attestations "shall lose" validity "from the moment of its revocation" and the status "shall not in any circumstances be reverted" (Art 45f(4) omits the words in any circumstances) | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 28(4), 38(4), 45d(4), 45f(4) | read.
- L2.4 Suspension exists for certificates only, nationally. Member States "may lay down national rules on temporary suspension" of qualified certificates; the certificate "shall lose its validity for the period of suspension" and the suspension status "shall be visible, during the period of suspension, from the service providing information on the status" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 28(5), 38(5) | read. No suspension rule appears for qualified attestations of attributes (Art 45d).
- L2.5 EUDI Wallet breach. Suspension and withdrawal of EUDI Wallets (Art 5e): suspension "without undue delay"; revocation of validity after three months without remedy; users and relying parties informed. CIR 2025/847: within 24 hours the Member State shall "evaluate whether revoking wallet unit attestations of the wallet units affected" is necessary; after three months the withdrawal within 72 hours revokes the attestations, "cannot revert to a valid state"; the wallet user's portability right continues "without impairing the security of the critical assets" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 5e; SRC-CIR-2025-847 (new) Art 4, 8 | read.
- L2.6 Wallet validation. Member States provide validation mechanisms "free-of-charge" so that "the authenticity and validity of European Digital Identity Wallets can be verified" | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 5a(8) | read.
- L2.7 Privacy of the framework. The EUDI Wallet framework not to allow any party, after issuance, "to obtain data that allows transactions or user behaviour to be tracked" unless authorised by the user | SRC-EIDAS-CONSOL Art 5a(16)(a) | read.

### A3 Implementing acts on status of PID, attestations and wallet units
- L3.1 CIR 2024/2977 Art 5 (PID). Policy for validity status management is public (5(1)); "Only providers of person identification data or electronic attestation of attributes can revoke" what they issued (5(2)); user informed "within 24 hours of the revocation" with reasons through a secure channel (5(3)); revocation upon user request, upon revocation of the wallet unit attestation, and in other cases in the policy (5(4)); revocations "cannot be reverted" (5(5)); revoked PID "shall remain accessible for as long as required by Union law or national law" (5(6)); validity status published "in a privacy preserving manner" with its location in the PID (5(7)); "privacy preserving techniques which ensure unlinkability" (5(8)) | SRC-CIR-2024-2977 Art 5 | read. CIR 2026/1731 Art 1(3) rewords 5(4)(b): "where the wallet unit attestation of the wallet unit to which the person identification data was issued to has been revoked".
- L3.2 CIR 2025/1569 Art 4 (qualified and public-sector attestations). Written public policies on "validity or revocation status management" including "measures for ensuring the availability of the validity status information" (4(1)); the provider is "the only" entity able to revoke (4(2)); for attestations "issued with a validity period of more than 24 hours" revocation at least on explicit request of the person or subject, on known compromise, and in other cases required by law or policy (4(3)); "revocation techniques and management methods that are privacy preserving and hindering linkability or traceability" (4(4)); status made available "in a manner that ensures the integrity and authenticity" (4(5)) | SRC-CIR-2025-1569 Art 4 | read. Recital (5): attestations "should be able to be revoked, or alternative measures should be implemented to compensate for the risks related to non-revocability".
- L3.3 CIR 2024/2979 Art 6-7 (wallet unit attestation). Wallet provider is "the only" entity able to revoke; public policy on "the conditions and the timeframe"; users informed "within 24 hours"; validity status public "in a privacy preserving manner"; recital 9 names an alternative: "to limit the lifetime of the wallet unit attestation as an alternative to the use of revocation identifiers" | SRC-CIR-2024-2979 Art 6(3), Art 7, recital 9 | read.
- L3.4 CIR 2026/1731 on attestation formats (Annex IV, replaces Annex II of 2979, applying ETSI TS 119 472-1 with adaptations). EAA-4.2.11.1-06: a status element "shall only indicate whether the attestation is revoked or not revoked and shall not support any other status values, such as suspension" for PID, qualified and public-sector attestations; EAA-4.2.11.1-06.1: revoked means "permanently revoked"; EAA-4.2.13-03: "Where short-lived electronic attestations of attributes with a validity period of 24 hours or less are issued, revocation shall not be required." | SRC-CIR-2026-1731 Annex IV | read.
- L3.5 mdoc status. The Mobile Security Object carries a status structure with an attestation status list (bit per index, Token Status List "in CWT format") or an attestation revocation list (identifier list); provider uses "one of" these methods; the identifier list uses an identifier "unique per MSO" and the status list index combined with URI "unique per MSO"; "Verification of the MSO revocation list is optional for the wallet-relying party"; a relying party that checks "shall support both" mechanisms; the list token carries `exp` (required) and `ttl` (optional) | SRC-CIR-2026-1731 Annex IV points EAA-6.2.10.1-01 to -17 | read.
- L3.6 SD-JWT VC status. EAA-5.2.10.1-06: "The status member may contain the status_list member as specified in clause 6.2 of IETF draft-ietf-oauth-status-list-20" | SRC-CIR-2026-1731 Annex IV | read. PID: `nbf` and `exp` "shall be used to express the technical validity period"; the PID attributes include an administrative expiry date and issuance date (Annex I of CIR 2026/1731, section 4 and the attribute table) | read in part.
- L3.7 Chaining PID status to the wallet. Wallet instance attestations and key attestations carry `client_status` and `key_storage_status` with a status list reference and an `exp` "until which the wallet provider will maintain the revocation status"; the wallet provider "shall use Token Status Lists" for both (R_GEN-1); instance attestation `exp` "shall be less than 24 hours" after the integrity check (TR-WIA-2.1); a wallet unit can always present attestations whose status periods are "at least 31 days in the future" (LC_GEN-1); PID technical validity "shall end before both" status periods (LC_GEN-3); a PID provider with validity above 24 hours "shall check the revocation status of both the wallet instance attestation and the key attestation received during issuance at least once every 24 hours" and revoke if either is revoked (LC_GEN-4); status lists should "relate to at least 10 000 attestations" where possible (R_WIA-5); a type-shared key status index may be revoked "only" for a vulnerability in the device type (R_KA-4) | SRC-CIR-2026-1731 Annex Ib points 2(c)-(e) | read.
- L3.8 Wallet-level revocation effects. To revoke a unit the provider revokes all index values in the instance attestations (R_WIA-4); revoking a wallet unit attestation ends display of the trust mark (Art 14a(8)) | SRC-CIR-2026-1731 Annex Ib, Art 2(9) | read.

### A4 ARF (v3.0.0) rules
- L4.1 Methods per format. PID, QEAA, PuB-EAA in mdoc: short-lived (24 hours or less), attestation status list or attestation revocation list; in SD-JWT VC: short-lived or status list ("No suitable specification of Attestation Revocation Lists in JSON format is available") | SRC-ARF-HLR Topic 7, VCR_01, VCR_01b, VCR_11, VCR_11a | read. VCR_02: for non-qualified EAAs "the relevant Rulebook SHALL specify whether that type of EAA must be revocable".
- L4.2 Who revokes, triggers, irreversibility. VCR_03: the provider "SHALL be the only party" executing revocation (it may outsource the operation); VCR_04: "SHALL NOT reverse the revocation"; VCR_05-09: policy; revoke on compromise, on user request (PID shall; attestation should), if the wallet unit is revoked (PID shall, attestation may), on death, and on changed attribute values when the credential is valid for at least 24 hours | SRC-ARF-HLR Topic 7 | read.
- L4.3 Relying party behaviour. VCR_13: a relying party instance "SHOULD verify the revocation status of a revocable PID or attestation upon obtaining it from a Wallet Unit" or perform a risk analysis; VCR_14: risk analysis to decide acceptance "in case no reliable information regarding the revocation status" exists (examples: not revocable, offline with expired cache); VCR_15: "SHOULD NOT request the relevant Attestation Status List or Attestation Revocation List each time an attestation is presented" but download once, distribute internally and cache; VCR_16: no authentication of the relying party before download | SRC-ARF-HLR Topic 7 | read.
- L4.4 Privacy of lists. VCR_17: "SHALL randomly assign the index for each PID or attestation, to prevent this index from becoming a correlator"; VCR_18: enough entries on each list "to ensure herd privacy", decoys allowed | SRC-ARF-HLR Topic 7 | read.
- L4.5 Wallet side. VCR_19: the wallet instance "SHOULD regularly check the revocation status of its PIDs and attestations" and whether itself or its devices are revoked, and notify the user | SRC-ARF-HLR Topic 7 | read.
- L4.6 Wallet unit revocation (Topic 38). Revocation is of the Wallet Instance (status list index) or of a WSCD or keystore; triggers: user request after authentication (WURevocation_10), request of a PID provider upon death of the person (WURevocation_11), security breach found by the provider's regular check (WURevocation_09); the user is informed within 24 hours through a channel "independent of the Wallet Unit" (WURevocation_14, _16); PID providers "immediately revoke" PIDs on a revoked unit (WURevocation_18); attestation providers "MAY" do so and publish the policy (WURevocation_19) | SRC-ARF-HLR Topic 38 | read.
- L4.7 States. Wallet unit: installed, operational, valid, revoked; "Revocation cannot be undone."; "Wallet Units can only be revoked" (no suspension), while the wallet solution can be suspended, restored or withdrawn; PID provider, attestation provider and relying party registrations can be suspended and unsuspended; a PID or attestation expires or is revoked as independent transitions | SRC-ARF-MAIN (new) sections 4.6.3-4.6.7 | read.
- L4.8 Representation attestations. RP_02: an issuer of representation attestations "SHALL ensure that either the attestations are short-lived" or that all entities entitled by law to request revocation are able to do so | SRC-ARF-HLR Topic 29 | read. The ARF has no legal-person wallet (Topic 28 empty) and no rule for a natural person representing a legal person.
- L4.9 The 24-hour figure. The ARF explains it as originating from ETSI EN 319 411-1 requirement REV-6.2.4-03A: "the process of revocation must take at most 24 hours" | SRC-ARF-HLR VCR_01 note | read (EN 319 411-1 itself not read in this pass).

### A5 Token Status List (IETF draft -21, CIR cites -20)
- L5.1 Statuses. 0x00 VALID, 0x01 INVALID, 0x02 SUSPENDED ("temporarily invalid"); values 0x03 and 0x0C to 0x0F are application specific; list bits per entry 1, 2, 4 or 8 | SRC-IETF-TSL (new) sections 4.1, 7.1 | read.
- L5.2 Freshness. Status List Token `exp` (absolute) and `ttl` ("the maximum amount of time, in seconds, that the Status List Token can be cached"); relying party options: cache for ttl, or check at issue time plus ttl "for critical use cases", or after `exp` if no ttl; the draft leaves the interval to ecosystems | sections 5.1, 13.7 | read.
- L5.3 Privacy. Herd privacy: "The herd privacy is depending on the number of entities within the Status List called its size."; issuer can track by generating one list per token or a unique URI; relying parties that store uri and index can profile validity, for example "profiling the suspension of an employee credential"; outsiders can infer counts and revocation rates; mitigations include random indices, decoys, several lists, no historical resolution, batch issuance of one-time-use tokens, re-issuance with a fresh list entry, an external status provider ("the Issuer has no means to identify the Relying Party") | sections 12.1-12.7, 13.2 | read.
- L5.4 Other statuses carry leak risk. Ecosystems using more than VALID and INVALID "should consider the possible leakage of data and profiling possibilities before doing so and evaluate if revocation and re-issuance might be a better fit" | section 12.8 | read.
- L5.5 Batch effect. "Batch revocation of a batch of Referenced Tokens might reveal that they are all members of the same batch." | section 13.2 | read.
- L5.6 Aggregation, external issuer and scaling. Optional aggregation URI; status issuer may differ from the token issuer; lists may be served by CDNs ("greater scalability") while authenticity remains by signature | sections 9, 13.5, 13.6 | read in part.

### A6 Key life cycle and remote signing
- L6.1 Signing key follows certificate status. ETSI TS 119 431-1: "If the public key certificate is revoked, the corresponding signing key shall be destroyed."; the service "should ensure that the public key certificate is valid before using the corresponding signing key" with valid meaning "not expired not revoked not suspended" | SRC-ETSI-TS-119431-1 (new) DEL-6.3.2-01, SIG-6.3.1-08 | read.
- L6.2 Certification body actions. Suspension "without undue delay" after a confirmed breach, cancellation if not remedied; recital: "The cancellation of a certificate of conformity might have severe consequences such as the revocation of all deployed wallet units." | SRC-CIR-2024-2981 Art 12, recital | read. Risk register: "A public attestation/relying party revocation list can contain information about the user's usage of their attestation" (TR37) and "An attacker can prevent suspension or revocation of a wallet" (TR79) | Annex I | read in part.

### A7 WE BUILD
- L7.1 ADR lifecycle model (Proposed, 11 Feb 2026). Wallet unit states uninstalled, installed, operational, valid; "Revocation of WUA immediately suspends infrastructural legitimacy." and revocation of the EBWOID suspends identity validity "while preserving structural trust"; "Where EBWOID is short-lived, the dependency between WUA revocation and EBWOID validity must be further specified in the implementing acts" | SRC-WEBUILD-ARCH adr/wallet-unit-lifecycle-management.md | read.
- L7.2 Revocation scenarios. The blueprint lists triggers for PID, EBWOID and units: explicit request ("A change in ownership of a company could be a reason for authorized representatives to revoke the EBWOID"), data change, regulatory change, loss or theft, provider revocation, abusive use, inactivity, service-term violation, end of life ("Termination or dissolution of the legal entity/business activity such as liquidation of a company"); provider duties: publish policy, only the issuer revokes, notify within 24 hours, revocation irreversible | SRC-WEBUILD-ARCH blueprint/06-trust-and-security.md | read.

Employee and contact-person rulebooks require a status claim when validity exceeds 24 hours; the employee rulebook adds "A revoked or suspended attestation must be treated as invalid for credential-validity purposes by all RPs." and requires the issuer to maintain an "active IETF Token Status List" | SRC-WEBUILD-RB rb-employee/README.md section 6; rb-contact-person/README.md | read. ## (C) OPTIONS: what the sources enable, restrict, leave open
No recommendation. The patterns can be combined by credential type.

### Option 1: short-lived credentials (24 hours or less), no status check
- Enables: CIR 2026/1731 EAA-4.2.13-03 and ARF VCR_01 allow it for PID, qualified and public-sector attestations; the draft lists re-issuance as a privacy mitigation (L5.3); no status list means no issuer observability of checks.
- Restricts: the issuer must be online and willing to re-issue at least daily per credential; the ARF explains the 24 hour threshold as the maximum duration of the revocation process, so a credential valid for less than that may expire before revocation completes (L4.9); the EBWOID rulebook allows validity above 24 hours and then requires revocation (L7.3).
- Leaves open: how re-issuance interacts with issuer-side authentication of the business wallet; whether a status of a wallet unit still needs checking at each re-issuance (LC_GEN-4 addresses PID with longer validity only); offline use.

### Option 2: status list (revocation only) for longer-lived credentials
- Enables: Token Status List is the named mechanism for the wallet instance and key attestations and for SD-JWT VC credentials, and for mdoc as attestation status list (L3.5-L3.7, L4.1); herd privacy by list size, random indices and caching (L4.4, L5.3); a third party may host lists (L5.6).
- Restricts: only revoked or not revoked for PID, qualified and public-sector attestations (L3.4); irreversible (L2.3, L3.1); the status uri and index are traceable data (L5.3); relying party verification is "optional" in the mdoc profile and a "SHOULD" in the ARF (L3.5, L4.3); small populations make small lists, which an owner with few credentials may produce (R_WIA-5 says "where possible" at least 10 000 attestations).
- Leaves open: list sizes and decoys for businesses with few credentials; cache time values per use case (the draft gives none); the status format for non-qualified EAAs issued by businesses, which ARF VCR_02 leaves to the rulebook.

### Option 3: identifier-based revocation list (mdoc only)
- Enables: described as the second mdoc mechanism; the identifier is unique per MSO (L3.5); relying parties that check must support both options (L3.5).
- Restricts: only for mdoc ("No suitable specification of Attestation Revocation Lists in JSON format is available", L4.1); list grows with revoked entries only.
- Leaves open: observability of revocation rate by outsiders (draft 12.4 treats it for status lists); not analysed in a source for identifier lists.

### Option 4: status values beyond revoked, including suspension
- Enables: the draft defines SUSPENDED (L5.1); eIDAS allows national temporary suspension of qualified certificates with visible status (L2.4); the WE BUILD employee rulebook speaks of "revoked or suspended" (L7.4); ARF registration states suspend providers and relying parties (L4.7).
- Restricts: CIR 2026/1731 forbids other status values than revoked for PID, qualified and public-sector attestations (L3.4); the ARF has no suspension of wallet units (L4.7); the draft warns of leakage and profiling and suggests considering revocation with re-issuance instead (L5.4).
- Leaves open: whether non-qualified EAAs and EBW authorisations may carry a suspended state (EBW Art 5(1)(j) speaks only of revoking authorisations); legal effect of a suspension for a business (no source).

### Option 5: online per-credential validation by the issuer or a validation service
- Enables: eIDAS requires status "on a per certificate basis at any time" for qualified certificates (L2.2); the EBW text speaks of validation mechanisms for the wallet (L1.2) and of real-time validation of roles and mandates inside the wallet (L1.5).
- Restricts: the sources describe lists as the approach that stops the issuer from learning which credential is checked (L5.3, L4.3) and require status information "in a privacy preserving manner" (L3.1, L3.3); the risk register notes that lists can reveal usage (TR37).
- Leaves open: whether a direct query service is acceptable for business credentials where unlinkability to a natural person is not at stake; relying party burden and availability.

## (D) OPEN QUESTIONS AND WHAT I COULD NOT READ
- The EBW texts contain no status mechanism for owner identification data, attestations issued to businesses, or authorisations beyond Art 5(1)(j), 6(2)(b), 6(2)(f), and Annex 6 for unit attestations; implementing acts under Art 5(5), 6(5) and 8(7) are pending. Whether the EUDI Wallet instruments (CIR 2024/2977, 2025/1569, 2026/1731) apply to EBW credentials is not stated in the EBW texts except through Art 8(3) (format standards of CIR 2024/2979 Annex II).
- No source gives a maximum time for revocation of non-qualified attestations or for revoking an authorisation in a wallet; 24 hours is stated for qualified certificates and attestations (eIDAS Art 24(3)-(4a)) and as the exemption threshold for short-lived credentials.
- Not read: ETSI EN 319 411-1 (the REV-6.2.4-03A requirement is known through the ARF note and the existing requirement register), ETSI TS 119 471 and TS 119 472-1 beyond CIR extracts, OID4VCI Appendix D and E, ISO/IEC 18013-5 second edition, W3C Verifiable Credentials status specifications (Bitstring Status List), CRL and OCSP profiles (RFC 5280, RFC 6960), ARF Technical Specification 3 (a pointer page only), CIR 2015/1502.
- Not read: Directive (EU) 2025/25 (digital power of attorney, EU Company Certificate) and its rules on validity and revocation of mandates; Parliament report A10-0240/2026; EDPS opinion 5/2026; outcome of the Council meeting of 9 June 2026.
- Open: who may revoke credentials issued to a business wallet when an authorised user leaves (owner, issuer or user); how a relying party learns that a mandate attestation ended (ARF RP_02 offers short life or revocation by those entitled).
- IETF draft -21 differs from the version -20 cited in CIR 2026/1731 in dates and some sections; a change of normative reference needs a check against -20.
- Stale register items: EBW-TRU-029 cites CIR 2024/2979 Art 3(2), deleted by CIR 2026/1731 Art 2(1); EBW-TRU-035 (PID provider verifies the unit attestation) and EBW-TRU-030/-031 (CIR 2024/2979 Art 7) should be checked against the replaced Art 6 and Annex Ib (wallet instance attestation and key attestation).
