- A1.1 Legislative intent. Council recital text is the same in substance | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 | read.
- A1.2 Providers must be on the list. "European Business Wallets shall be provided by providers of European Business Wallets that are included in the list established pursuant to Article 12(5)." | Art 7(1) | read. The reference to Article 12(5) is dangling: Article 12 of the copy read has paragraphs 1 to 3 only (the list is in 12(3)). Council text refers to "paragraph 3" in Article 12(2).
- A1.3 What the list is. "establish and maintain on the Commission's website, in a machine-readable format, a list of providers of European Business Wallets" | Art 12(3) | read. The text does not say the list is signed or sealed and does not name a location or a signing key. Contrast with the information on validation mechanisms: "in electronically signed or sealed form suitable for automated processing" | Art 6(3) | read.
- A1.4 Council text adds timing. "the Commission shall include the provider in the list within two working days" and removal "within two working days" after a supervisory notification | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Art 12(3) | read. The title changes from notified to authorised providers. A Council recital says the Commission may "temporarily suspend the provider from the list of trusted providers established under Article 12" | recital on Union intervention | read.
- A1.5 List drives validity. A wallet can be revoked "where the provider of the European Business Wallet is not included in the list referred to in Article 12(5)." | Art 6(2)(f) fourth indent | read.
- A1.6 Validation mechanisms are notified. Providers shall notify "the mechanism by which to validate the authenticity and validity of European Business Wallets" and the Commission publishes it | Art 6(2)(g), 6(3) | read.
- A1.7 Wallet unit attestation anchors. "shall be issued under a certificate listed in the trusted list referred to in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2980" | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Annex point 2 | read. Same list reference in CIR 2024/2979 Art 3(2) | SRC-CIR-2024-2979 | read (CIR 2026/1731 deletes Article 3(2) of 2979 and replaces the wallet-unit-attestation rule in its own text; see A2.7).
- A1.8 Authentic sources list. "a list of the notified relevant authentic sources" published "in a machine-readable format" | SRC-COUNCIL-ST-9684-26 Art 9(2) | read. - A1.9 Directory as trusted source. "which shall act as the trusted source of information for European Business Wallet owners" with "a machine-readable interface exposed through an API for automated system-to-system communication" | Art 10(1) | read. - A2.8 Who is on which list. Non-qualified attestation providers are not notified and "their trust anchors are not included in a LoTE by the Commission"; the relying party "must know how to obtain the trust anchor"; a LoTE "does not have to comply with the requirements in Topic 31" | SRC-ARF-TRUST 6.3.2.4 | read. - A2.9 No central discovery for issuers. "no centralised service discovery mechanism for PID or attestation issuance is foreseen" and the wallet provider "may configure each Wallet Unit with a pre-defined list of PID Providers and Attestation Providers" | SRC-ARF-TRUST 6.6.2.2 | read.
- A2.10 Anchors per attestation class. The relying party instance "is able to distinguish between trust anchors usable either for PIDs, for QEAAs, for PuB-EAAs, or for non-qualified EAAs" | SRC-ARF-TRUST 6.6.3.6 | read.
- A2.11 Trust rooted in authority. Trust in conformity assessment bodies and list providers "is primarily rooted in authority and in procedural measures ... rather than in technical measures" | SRC-ARF-TRUST 6.1 | read.
- A2.12 Wallet providers have no access certificates. "Wallet Providers consequently do not receive access certificates or registration certificates" | SRC-ARF-TRUST 6.2.2 | read. Reason given: "there is no need for interoperability between Wallet Providers and Wallet Units".

The normative annexes are profiles for lists of providers of person identity data (D), wallet providers (E), providers of wallet relying party access certificates (F), registration certificates (G), public sector bodies issuing attestations (H), and registrars and registers (I). There is no profile for business wallet providers or for their access certificates | SRC-ETSI-119602 table of contents | read.

### A3 Discovery mechanisms in the specifications
- A3.1 Pointers. A trusted list carries "a string containing the URI of the machine processable format of another TL" with "one or more digital identities, all representing the issuer of the TL pointed to"; for EU lists the field "shall include the pointer to a European Commission compiled list of links" (the LOTL) | SRC-ETSI-119612 5.3.13 | read. For non-EU schemes the field is optional and "may reference any relevant trusted list or list of trusted lists" | read.
- A3.2 Same mechanism in LoTE. "One of such digital identities shall allow successful authentication of the pointed-to list before its use." | SRC-ETSI-119602 6.3.13 | read. A LoTE can also state distribution points that "all shall provide identical copies" and a tag that lets "a web-searching tool" recognise a LoTE (6.3.16 and LoTETag) | read.
- A3.3 Scheme-of-schemes. A common URI denotes participation "in a wider scheme of lists of trusted entities (i.e. a LoTE listing pointers to all members ...)" | SRC-ETSI-119602 6.3.9 | read.
- A3.4 Discovery from the trust token. "Trust Service Providers may wish to give information on how to locate a TL of the scheme they operate under" through an extension in certificates, time-stamp tokens or other tokens; it "should not be marked critical" and must always resolve "to the latest available applicable TL or to a scheme including a pointer to it (e.g. LOTL)" | SRC-ETSI-119612 6.3 | read.
- A3.5 List availability. "TLSOs shall make their TLs available 24 hours a day and 7 days a week" with "an availability percentage of minimum 99,9 % over one year". | SRC-ETSI-119612 6.4 | read.
- A3.6 Location of the LOTL and key changes. The LOTL is at https://ec.europa.eu/tools/lotl/eu-lotl.xml; its location and signing certificates are authenticated by an Official Journal publication and can change "through the mechanism of pivot LOTL"; EU lists have "a legal constitutive value" | SRC-ETSI-119615 (new) 4.1.1 | read.
- A3.7 Live LOTL snapshot. The instance read has sequence number 395, issue date 2026-09-24T12:04:06Z, next update 2027-03-17T12:04:06Z, 43 pointers including the EU list-of-lists entry, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway, and an entry with territory UK, scheme operator "tScheme Limited", type EUgeneric, location https://ec.europa.eu/tools/lotl/Final_EU_TSL-UKsigned.xml. Its scheme-information URIs include OJ C/2026/1944 and pivot LOTL files | SRC-LOTL-EU (new) | read (machine-read; the content of the UK list and the status of its services were not analysed).
- A3.8 ERDS recipients and capabilities. The receiver identification service is bound to OASIS BDXL (DNS) and the capability discovery service to OASIS SMP; the identity of an ERDS "should be registered in BDXL, i.e. in DNS, by a domain name" | SRC-ETSI-EN-319522-4-3 (new) clauses 4 to 5 | read.
- A3.9 ERDS trust domains. Trust "may be established bilaterally", by the EU Trusted List system, by a domain trusted list, or by a domain PKI; a trust infrastructure "shall have governance, at least for policy regarding conditions for an ERDS to join" | SRC-ETSI-EN-319522-4-3 7.1 | read. Check for EU lists: verify that the service digital identity matches, "verify that the service current status is "granted"" and that the service type identifier fits the trust domain | 7.2 | read. Service types: .../Svctype/EDS/Q, EDS/REM/Q, EDS, EDS/REM | read.
- A3.10 Query-side declaration of acceptable trust (OpenID4VP). A DCQL credential query may carry "trusted_authorities"; defined types are aki, etsi_tl (identifier of a trusted list; such a list can contain "references to other Trusted Lists, creating a list of trusted lists") and openid_federation (an Entity Identifier of an entity that "would usually have the Entity Configuration of a Trust Anchor") | SRC-OID4VP-1.0 (new) 6.1.1 | read. "Verifiers must verify that the issuer of a received presentation is trusted on their own"; the feature "mainly aims to help data minimization" | 6.1 | read.
- A3.11 Privacy cost of online resolution. "Mechanisms that require online resolution can leak information that could be used to profile the usage of the Credentials." | SRC-OID4VP-1.0 15.10 | read.
- A3.12 HAIP position. Trust management is out of scope ("the methods for establishing trust or obtaining root certificates are out of the scope"); the `aki` query "MUST be supported" and "can be used to support multiple X.509-based trust mechanisms, such as ISO mDL VICAL ... or ETSI Trusted Lists" | SRC-HAIP-1.0 (new) 3.4 and 5 | read. The ISO VICAL text itself was not read (paywalled).
- A3.13 EU wallet profile. "The Authority Key Identifier shall use the ETSI trusted Lists (specified in ETSI TS 119 612 [12]) mechanism ("etsi_tl" type)." | SRC-ETSI-119472-2 (new) OIDFVP-HAIP-COMMON-REQ-RO-17 | read. The sentence mixes the names of two query types; the intended type was not clarified in the text read.
- A3.14 Signed issuer metadata. "the Wallet MUST establish trust in the signer of the metadata"; keys can be conveyed by "x5c, kid or trust_chain"; "the concrete mechanisms ... are out of scope" | SRC-OID4VCI-1.0 (new) 12.2.3 | read. In the EU profile the signing certificate "shall be the access certificate of the PID/EAA Provider" | SRC-ETSI-119472-3 (new) ISS-MDATA-4.2.1-02 | read.

### A4 OpenID Federation 1.0 (final, 17 February 2026)
- A4.1 Model. Entity Statements are signed JWTs; a chain runs "from an Entity (typically a Leaf Entity) to a Trust Anchor"; the Trust Anchor and intermediates issue Subordinate Statements | SRC-OIDF-FEDERATION-1.0 (new) section 2 | read.
- A4.2 No Web PKI. The mechanism "intentionally does not rely on Web PKI / TLS [RFC9525] certificates for signing keys" | 2.1 | read.
- A4.3 Anchor keys out of band. "The Trust Anchor's public keys are distributed to Entities ... in some secure out-of-band way not described in this document." | 4 | read.
- A4.4 Starting inputs. A party wanting trust "MUST have Party B's Entity Identifier and a list of Entity Identifiers of Trust Anchors and their public signing keys" | 10 | read.
- A4.5 Enumeration of members. Anchors and intermediates may expose a subordinate listing endpoint that lists immediate subordinates (the list "MAY be a very large list") | 8.2 | read. A resolve endpoint lets a trusted third party do the chain evaluation | 8.3, 10.6 | read.
- A4.6 Several valid chains. "If multiple valid Trust Chains are found, Party A will need to decide on which one to use." | 10.3 | read.
- A4.7 Trust marks. Trust marks are "statements of conformance to sets of criteria determined by an accreditation authority"; accepted issuers are listed in the anchor's configuration | 7 | read.
- A4.8 Revocation. "this specification does not define a revocation process"; chains carry an expiry that is the minimum "exp" in the chain and must be refreshed | 11, 10.4 | read. Redundant retrieval of anchor keys is RECOMMENDED | 11.3 | read.
- A4.9 Use in OID4VP. The client identifier prefix `openid_federation:` makes the verifier identifier "an Entity Identifier"; the request may carry a "trust_chain" | SRC-OID4VP-1.0 5.9.3 | read. No EU law text read mandates or mentions OpenID Federation for the wallet ecosystem (searched the proposal, Council text, CIR 2024/2979, 2024/2980, 2024/2982, 2026/1731).

## (B) NEUTRAL DECISION CRITERIA
[S] = stated or implied by a source; [D] = project driver, to be confirmed by stakeholders.
- B1 [S] Legal effect of the anchor. EU trusted list status carries legal constitutive value for qualified services (A3.6); ecosystem anchors carry contractual effect only.
- B2 [S] Authenticity of the list itself: signed or sealed, location and keys anchored outside the list (A2.3, A3.6).
- B3 [S] Machine readability and open access without registration (A2.3, A2.5).
- B4 [S] Status and history: ability to evaluate the status at the time of signing, and speed of withdrawal (concept article; A2.4 Invalid status; A4.8 chain expiry; DCP revocation lists).
- B5 [S] Coverage of issuer classes: qualified, public sector, other attestation providers, ecosystem members (A2.8).
- B6 [S] Support for several trust domains at once and per-credential policy (A2.10, A3.10, A5.2).
- B7 [S] Privacy of trust resolution: online lookups that reveal which credential is used (A3.11).
- B8 [S] Availability and caching: 99,9 % list availability, offline validation with cached data (A3.5; ARF 6.6.3.2 offline CRL caching).
- B9 [S] Third-country recognition path (A1.11, A2.1, A3.1, A6.3).
- B10 [S] Governance of the anchor: who admits and removes members, EU establishment and independence from third-country control (A1.12, A1.11 Council, A3.9).
- B11 [S] Discovery path from the credential or service to the applicable list (A3.4, A3.8, A3.10; A2.9 shows the gap for issuers).
- B12 [D] Number of parsers and signature profiles a relying party has to run (XML TL with XAdES, JSON LoTE with JAdES, JWT entity statements, DID documents).
- B13 [D] Operational dependence on a single operator (Commission, clearing house operators, one core service provider) and exit path.
- B14 [D] Effort and cost for small relying parties to maintain anchor stores per attestation class (ARF 6.6.3.6 requires a regular process).
- B15 [D] Liability and recourse when a list entry is wrong or late.

## (C) OPTIONS: what the sources enable, restrict, leave open (no recommendation)

### Option (a) EU lists as the single root
Relying parties and wallets trust only entries reachable from the EU LOTL, the Commission LoTEs for wallet roles, and the Commission list of business wallet providers.
- Enables: legal constitutive value for qualified services (A3.6); authentication chain anchored in the Official Journal with pivot LOTL (A3.6); open, signed publication (A2.3); the same rule set as the EUDI Wallet (A2.5 to A2.7); EEA and a UK entry already appear in the LOTL (A3.7); ERDS trust evaluation is defined for the EU list (A3.9).
- Restricts: non-qualified and ecosystem issuers have no entry (A2.8, A10.1); no profile for business wallet provider lists in TS 119 602 (A2.14); the Article 12 list in the proposal has no stated signature, location or key (A1.3); third-country anchors enter only through implementing acts or agreements (A1.11, A2.1).
- Leaves open: how a relying party learns of a new EU list location (OJ publication and pivot LOTL are defined for the LOTL only, A3.6); whether the Article 12 list will be published as a signed list or LoTE; how long a withdrawal takes to appear (no timing in 119 612 beyond list validity, concept article).

### Option (b) Separate lists per ecosystem, selected per credential
Each ecosystem (EU qualified, EU wallet roles, data space, sector, global) keeps its own list or anchor, and the relying party selects the domain per credential type or per counterparty.
- Enables: DCP and OID4VP support exactly this (multiple anchors, `trusted_authorities` per credential query, several governance authorities; A3.10, A5.2, A5.3); Catena-X and Gaia-X already run own anchors (A6, A7); ARF already separates anchors per attestation class (A2.10).
- Restricts: the specifications give no decision rule between domains (A10.2); ecosystem lists are out of scope of DCP and not specified in Catena-X pages read (A5.3, A7.5); each domain brings its own format and revocation behaviour; wallets in the EU ecosystem have no discovery service for issuers (A2.9).
- Leaves open: how a business wallet advertises accepted domains to counterparties without revealing its credential portfolio (A3.11); how a domain lists non-EU participants; who audits ecosystem operators.

### Option (c) Bridged or federated trust layer
A federation or registry layer maps ecosystem anchors to common entries and lets a relying party resolve trust through a resolver or federation chain.
- Enables: OpenID Federation offers anchors, intermediates, trust marks, member enumeration and resolution as a service (A4.1 to A4.7); Gaia-X already admits eIDAS trust service providers as anchors and uses notaries over registers (A6.2, A6.4); the OID4VP query type `openid_federation` exists (A3.10).
- Restricts: no EU legal text read mentions OpenID Federation (A4.9); the federation specification defines no revocation process and leaves anchor key distribution out of band (A4.3, A4.8); delegated resolution adds a trusted third party and an online lookup (A4.5, A3.11); equivalence between ecosystem and EU anchors is not defined (A10.3).
- Leaves open: the legal status of a federation anchor next to an eIDAS list; governance and liability of a bridge operator; whether resolved results may be cached for offline presentation.
